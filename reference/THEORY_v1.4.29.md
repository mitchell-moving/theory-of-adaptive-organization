## 0. The claim in one paragraph

**The spine (v1.4.20; `theory/BOOKENDS.md`).** Everything obeys the second law: total entropy rises, though local order can grow by exporting entropy. That law, together with gravity, the speed of light, quantum physics and the universe's low-entropy start, sets the stage; GTAO adds no new physics. Atoms and crystals carry information but have no use for a model; stars hold themselves up on a flow but track nothing outside; cells, people, firms and cities last only by staying matched to a world that changes and can ruin them, and once acting late is costly, carrying a model and acting ahead can pay. That is judgment: not a new physical principle, but a way of staying alive under the old one. What follows is the budget that judgment runs on.

An adaptive organization (a cell, an immune system, an ant colony, a person, a firm, a city) persists by holding information about a world that keeps changing, while paying for that information and for every delay in acting on it. GTAO models this with one budget at every scale: the cost of holding memory, the loss from prediction error (including acting late), and the cost of the reads and effort that keep the memory current, plus the risk of ruin. Only the implementation and the currency change: heat and receptors in a cell, attention in a person, money in a firm. **The time-spectrum part of this is not new.** For a world whose change has spectral slope α (S(f) ∝ f^−α), classical optimal filtering and approximation theory, plus a price on effort, give the design exponents for a Gaussian world, or for the best linear tracker in any world (premises per row in §2.2): read rate ∝ (stakes·drift/price)^{1/α} for reset reads; precision effort ∝ stakes^{α/(2α−1)}·drift^{1/(2α−1)} under linear precision pricing; memory time ∝ (noise/drift level)^{1/α}; a forgetting kernel with tail τ^{−(1+α/2)} that is exponential only at α = 2; and a number of memory stores ∝ ln(span)·ln(stakes/upkeep) away from α = 2 (§2.2; Afterman et al. 2022; Kleptsyna et al. 2020; Nuzman & Poor 2000; Hosking 1981; Gatheral, Jaisson & Rosenbaum 2018; Jiang et al. 2017; Benna & Fusi 2016; Mora & Nemenman 2019; Lee, Flack & Krakauer 2024). These are known or elementary results, assembled. For the Bayes tracker of a non-Gaussian world the noisy-sensing exponents can differ: in a world that switches between two levels, jump rate and jump size enter separately and the adaptation time grows only logarithmically with the switching time (§2.2 Regime A premise bullet). The mode-spectrum part (§2.2 Regime C: model size ∝ Q^{1/β} and an interior minimum of the model's budget share iff β < 1, for Gaussian modes (wider scope and what is not derived in §2.2); a minimum viable size from unstable modes) is PROOF under stated assumptions with known tools and was not reviewed by the spectrum-map referee. **GTAO's open, falsifiable bet is empirical:** real adaptive systems at every scale sit near these optima once α, the drift level q, the sensing noise N and the stakes are measured on the same system (where the optimum compared is the Bayes optimum of a non-Gaussian world, together with the further inputs it needs, such as jump rate and jump size separately; which optimum is compared is fixed in the prereg before the data, §1 N6); and the same relations, not the same constants, hold at every level, from cells to cities, with only the implementation and the currency of the three costs differing. That bet is untested. The costs themselves are physically priced at every scale, from atoms to the cosmic horizon (Chad, t1927); at the atomic end, where nothing selects among designs, a model buys nothing and the optimum is the zero-model corner, and a black hole is the extreme of that frozen class (§2.3; CLAIM_MAP N25–N26: PROVED (in-model), the first partly definitional, the second conditional), so the bet concerns the selected systems in between. In the model, the same three costs at every level is an axiom (Axiom 5; CLAIM_MAP J16), so the model alone cannot test it. As a claim about real systems it is testable through its consequences: at each level the three costs can be measured separately, and the claim fails if a level's design needs a fourth kind of cost to be explained, or if design does not respond to the measured prices of the three as the relations require (J6).

It is **not** a theory of everything. It says nothing about what an organization wants, only about what it must pay to keep tracking a world while it pursues it.

### What changed in v1.4 (and why)
1. **Demoted the headline.** v1.0–v1.3 called "the shape of the world's change sets the exponents of the organization's design" the *central new bet*. It is kept as the organizing frame, credited, not as a novelty claim.
2. **Credit corrected.** The square-root memory scaling is Kalman/Muth filtering, and its α = 2 form for a sensing system predates Lee, Flack & Krakauer 2024 (Mora & Nemenman 2019). LFK is cited as an application with an energy cost, not as its source. On a telegraph world their √ form belongs to their learner class, not to the Bayes optimum, which adapts on a single log (§2.2 Regime A premise bullet; `reviews/night2/PRIORART_LFK_ALPHA/REPORT.md`, verdict item 4).
3. **Regimes restated.** Regime A is "α = 2 (Markov)", not "one characteristic pace". Regime B's "adaptation time slope 1" and "(log-span)^{2/3} layers" are withdrawn (K13, K12). Forgetting kernels are separated from re-occurrence hazards, which are a different object (§2.2).
4. **Two corrections to field readings:** MATCH1 D3's opposite sign came from a mis-derived prediction, not from the world (H19); and the D3S pass does not discriminate (N10).
5. **The formal layer's pooling-onset exponent does not depend on α** for the sensing technologies covered, under stated premises (§2.5; elementary; refereed and narrowed). Other formal exponents (vigilance growth; the private-drift exponent when level and members use different technologies) and all constants do depend on α, and the tracking-layer exponents depend on it throughout.

Earlier changes (v1.0–v1.2) are in §13: prior art credited up front; drift reduction added as a lever (§5); "no one in charge" narrowed (§6); optimality claimed only under strong selection (§7); test results reported as they came out (§8).

## 2. The tracking layer: what the drift spectrum implies (KNOWN and ELEMENTARY), and the empirical bet

### 2.1 Setting

A system holds a state M about a world X. Keeping M accurate costs upkeep. Being wrong costs a loss, at stakes w. Being late costs extra, because a prediction that arrives after the moment to act buys nothing. Running out of buffer B ends the system. The objective is

  **J = upkeep(information held and refreshed) + w · E[error loss] + lateness cost + Λ · (failure hazard) + cost of reducing drift (§5),**

minimized subject to staying solvent (`theory/GENERAL_THEOREM.md`, Theorem P: solvency needs at least R(y − c̄) bits).

The causal chain is: the world's change destroys the information M holds; re-buying it costs reads, precision or upkeep; error and lateness cost stakes; selection or design keeps the cheapest allocation that stays solvent (CLAIM_MAP J15). What matters about the world is not one drift rate but how much variance arrives at each timescale: a spectrum S(f) ∝ f^−α in time (over a measured band), or, across many modes, mode variances λ_i ∝ i^−β. For a Gaussian world, or for the best linear tracker in any world (only second moments enter), three quantities set the tracking optimum in time: the slope α, the high-frequency drift level q (S = q|ω|^−α), and the sensing noise N, together with stakes w and the prices of reads, precision and stores. For the Bayes tracker of a non-Gaussian world more of the world's law can enter (in the two-state switching world, jump rate and jump size separately; §2.2 Regime A premise bullet).

- **Premise: Gaussian world, or the best linear tracker (v1.4.3; CLAIM_MAP N16, in-model, refereed).** The memory-time and forgetting bullets above hold for a Gaussian world, or for the best linear tracker in any world, because only second moments enter. They need not describe the Bayes-optimal tracker of a non-Gaussian world with the same spectrum, and in the one case derived they do not. For a world that switches between two levels ±μ at rate λ each way, sensed in white noise of density N with κ = λN/μ² ≪ 1, the Bayes-optimal tracker adapts after a switch (mean time for its estimate to cross zero) in t_A = (N/2μ²)[ln(2μ²/λN) − γ − 1] + o(1) (γ is Euler's constant; a single logarithm), and its mean squared error is exactly μ²·2K₀(κ)/(K₀(κ) + K₁(κ)) ≈ 2λN[ln(2μ²/λN) − γ] (K₀, K₁ modified Bessel functions). With ℓ = ln(2μ²/λN) − γ − 1, the adaptation time's elasticities in switching time, noise and amplitude are 1/ℓ, 1 − 1/ℓ and −2 + 2/ℓ (about 0.17, 0.83 and −1.67 at κ = 10⁻³), instead of the linear tracker's ½, ½ and −1; they approach 0, 1 and −2 only logarithmically. The error's elasticity in λ and in N is about 0.85–0.89 at κ = 10⁻³ to 10⁻⁴, rising slowly toward 1, against ½ for the linear tracker. The drift level q = 4λμ² alone does not set these: jump rate and amplitude enter separately. **Definition and sampled data (v1.4.9; `reviews/night2/DISCRETE_TA/REPORT.md`, reviewed in `reviews/night2/DISCRETE_TA_N16_REVIEW.md`; in-model):** t_A is derived for the filter's resting state after a long quiet spell (the quiet law). For a switch at a typical time (the average over all switches of a stationary record) the value differs by O(κ ln 1/κ): 0.5% or less at κ ≤ 10⁻³, 3% at κ = 10⁻². A prereg fixes which law it uses. On data sampled every Δ (per-sample noise variance N/Δ, switches between samples, time counted from the last sample before the switch), the adaptation time read at the samples is longer, t_A + c√(ΔN)/μ + O(Δ). As κ → 0, c → −ζ(½)/√(2π) ≈ 0.58, the mean overshoot of the sampled log-odds past zero in per-sample standard deviations (Siegmund's constant, as stated by Broadie, Glasserman & Kou 1997); c is lower by O(κ ln 1/κ), about 0.57 at κ = 10⁻² (numerical). So, for the quiet law, the formula is within 10% only when t_A ≳ 10–27 samples (κ = 10⁻⁶ to 10⁻²) and within 5% only when t_A ≳ 30–120 samples; for the typical-switch law, 10–16 and 30–47 samples. Timing from a switch placed inside the sampling interval subtracts about Δ/2 on average and changes these thresholds. Otherwise compare with the exact sampled value (`tools/discrete_ta.py`). The grid term's elasticities in N and μ are about the linear tracker's (½, −1), so a sampled Bayes-versus-linear exponent test uses grid-corrected exponents. The best linear tracker's memory time on the grid is exact in closed form, cosh(βΔ) = cosh(2λΔ) + (μ²Δ/N) sinh(2λΔ) with memory time 1/β, and is within about 1% of the continuous value at ≥ 2 samples per memory time (1.0% at 2.0). **Credit:** the leading-order error is prior art (Khasminskii & Zeitouni 1996 for the error probability, Golubev 2000 for the mean squared error, continuous-time form per Chigansky 2005; the two originals were not read); the form of the adaptation time, a log threshold divided by the KL rate, is the classical quickest-detection asymptotic (Shiryaev 1963; Tartakovsky & Veeravalli 2005; neither read, only search excerpts of later papers); logarithmic Bayes adaptation in sensing is Conti & Mora 2022, whose printed ln² does not hold for the exact filter (§10). ### 2.3 Where the physics is literal and where it is not

- **Literal:** floors. The minimum heat cost of tracking a random walk to error D is kT·σ²/(2D). Adding quadratic loss gives σ√(2kT·w), the EOQ form with c = kT per nat (`theory/MATH_ATTACK.md` O1). Holding I bits against a drifting world needs an inflow of at least (1 − η)·I bits per step, met with equality by the Kalman filter, consistent with Mitter & Newton 2005 and Horowitz & Sandberg 2014 (KNOWN).
- **Not literal above the cell.** kT ln 2 per bit does not bind for neurons, people or firms. A chemical synapse pays about 10⁵–10⁶ times that: Laughlin, de Ruyter van Steveninck & Anderson 1998 measured about 10⁴ ATP per bit at a blowfly-retina chemical synapse (2–6×10⁴ in their text) and 10⁶–10⁷ ATP per bit for graded signals and spike coding; at ΔG_ATP ≈ 20 kT (our conversion, not theirs) 10⁴ ATP ≈ 2×10⁵ kT ≈ 3×10⁵ × kT ln 2, the low end of their range. Above the cell the exponents come from information theory plus network transport costs, not from the second law. The objection "decision theory in a lab coat" mostly survives above the cell.

### 2.5 The formal layer inside the tracking layer, and the rule "regime first"

- **The bridge (CLAIM_MAP N7, PROVED in-model).** A tracker that reads exactly and predicts in between, in a world with fractional drift of Hurst exponent H, has stakes and drift elasticities a = b = **1/(1+2H) = 1/α** (formal Lemma 1(i), `reviews/night1/formal/THEOREMS.md`) under squared-error loss; under ℓ = |e|^p they are 2/(2 + p(α−1)) and p/(2 + p(α−1)), equal only at p = 2 (§2.2 loss premise, v1.4.16; the α = 2 case is formal Lemma 1(iv)). The referee confirmed that this holds for the optimal linear predictor too, and for any predictor class closed under time rescaling, but not for a predictor with a fixed time constant (`SPECTRUM_MAP_REFEREE.md` §1). Every formal theorem is stated in (a, b), so the formal constants (Π\* ≈ 4, V ≈ 2, 29%, the vigilance floor 1 + (1+ρ)^{−1/2}) are α = 2 instances (`reviews/night1/ARCH_EQUATION.md` §0.2).
- **The pooling-onset exponent does not depend on α (CLAIM_MAP N12; ELEMENTARY; refereed and narrowed, `reviews/night2/N12_REFEREE.md`).** Put the §2.2 technologies into the power class φ = k p^{1−a} w^a q^b. Exact reset reads (hold-last-value or any rescaling-closed predictor) have a = b = 1/α, so the onset exponent −(1−b)/(1−a) is **−1 at every α**; information-rate pricing also gives a = b and −1. Precision-priced continuous sensing with price ∝ precision^θ has a = αθ/(α(θ+1)−1) and b = θ/(α(θ+1)−1), so the onset exponent is **−(1+θ) at every α > 1** (−2 for linear pricing; at α = 2 this is formal Lemma 1(iii), A_θ). Reason: the error of an optimal linear tracker scales by λ when the drift level and the sensing noise are both scaled by λ, so α enters (a, b) only through a ratio that cancels. That degree-1 homogeneity holds for linear trackers. For the Bayes tracker of a two-state jump world (§2.2 Regime A premise bullet) the error is μ²·f(λN/μ²) (from the closed form; checked in `reviews/night2/TLP_EDITS_REVIEW_checks/`): degree-1 homogeneous when the noise and the squared amplitude are scaled together, but not when the noise and the switching rate are, so rate and amplitude enter separately and the onset exponent there is not derived (QUEUE JUMP-WORLD-ONSET). Premises: (i) shared and private drift have the same α (for priced precision it suffices that α_g − α_u < 1; otherwise, by an asymptotic argument that matches numerics to within about 3% but is not proved, the exponent is −(1/Δ − b_g)/(1 − a_g) with Δ = α_g − α_u, and depends on α); (ii) reads are exact at the **level's** operating point: with any read noise σ² the level, tracking a small shared drift, becomes noise-limited and the small-ρ exponent tends to −2 at every α (`reviews/night2/ONSET_NOISY_READS/REPORT.md`; derived at physicist's rigour and refereed, `reviews/night2/ONSET_NOISY_READS_REVIEW.md`: the dense-sampling asymptote of the sampled-fBm error, assumed by the author, follows from the Kolmogorov–Szegő formula for every α > 1 (reviewer's sketch, checked to 10⁻⁵ at α = 1.25 without truncation); "tends to −2" is proved in the ratio sense N\*ρ²/σ² → C_α, the local slope's limit is numerical). For periodic noisy reads (member units q_u = w = c = 1; the constants below take the members' own read noise as negligible, which moves their marginal cost by < 1% for σ² ≤ 0.1, otherwise use the members' measured marginal cost), the onset obeys N\*ρ = X_α(σ²/ρ) to leading order in ρ, so the crossover depends only on s = σ²/ρ; the level's reads then cost linearly in precision (θ = 1 class), giving N\* ≈ C_α σ²/ρ² at large s against N\* ≈ α^{α/(α−1)}/ρ at small s. ## 4. Failure, buffers and the "temperature"

**The index.** With buffer B, diffusive shock size σ and one fixing timescale τ, the hazard must take the form h = F(z)/τ with z = B/(σ√τ). This holds for any fix mechanism, by dimensional analysis (PROOF, `theory/MATH_ATTACK.md` §2). Its inverse, σ√τ/B, is the "temperature".

**Credit.**
- z is the **inventory safety factor** in the textbook rule SS = z·σ·√(lead time) (e.g. Silver, Pyke & Thomas 2017; the formula was checked through search summaries, not the book). The ratio itself is not new.
- For diffusive errors with full resets, per-window ruin is exactly erfc(B/(σ√(2τ))). That is **Black & Cox's (1976) first-passage default probability** with zero drift (checked numerically, `theory/GENERAL_THEOREM.md` R2). Merton's (1974) terminal-default version is off by a factor of 2. Earlier wording that called this "Merton's distance to default" is superseded.
- Related: Kramers 1940; Lande 1993; Galbraith 1974 and Thompson 1967 (slack vs information-processing speed); Chevin, Lande & Mace 2010; Scheffer et al. 2009 (critical slowing down).

**What may be new.**
- **The identity** ε_B = −ε_σ and ε_τ = ε_σ/2 − 1, for every fix mechanism. Slow fixing raises the hazard iff ε_σ > 2. In the "hot" regime (small z) the unit fails before any fix arrives and τ becomes invisible. In a discrete-incident regime ε_τ = ε_σ/2.
- **The fixed-spread sign** (N3): ε_τ = −1 at fixed observed spread.
- **Universality classes set by how fixing works**, not by scale: fixed delay gives an e^{−z²/2} tail; proportional restoring (OU) e^{−z²}; fixing at a random Poisson rate a fat e^{−√2·z} tail; heavy-tailed fix times a power law in z.
- **Coarse-graining rules:** pooling cools, z′ = z·√(N/(1+(N−1)ρ)) up to a ceiling z/√ρ; the weakest link heats, z′ ≈ √(z² − ln N). Heterogeneous fix speeds aggregate to long memory, and a single-τ fit then mispredicts failure by 1.4–2.4×.

**Results.** See §8, which follows the independent review of DECISIVE and F1. Every sign was right, and the numbers reproduce, but the τ term as a theory-specific claim failed on these proxies under MATH_ATTACK's literal joint kill rule (populations on any reading; glucose on the full sample; banks are sign-only, not a hit; CLAIM_MAP K4, H11–H15 PROXY-FAIL). "Fewer crossings at fixed spread with slower correction" follows from dimensional analysis and standard first-passage results. The theory-specific structure — the −1 exponent, the √τ weighting, the identities, the τ-buffer interaction, and one collapse curve — failed on these proxies (CLAIM_MAP K4 and H11–H15, PROXY-FAIL). F1's τ mostly measures fixed-cost share. C3 found no τ effect in open-source projects, and the math attack showed that test could not have seen τ as measured. C3 also ruled out the reset-clock mechanism at that scale: at C3's numbers it predicts a hazard ratio of about 11 per doubling of τ, while C3 measured about 1.0 (upper CI ≈ 1.09), so at most about 1–5% of open-source dormancy can come from diffusive error reset at issue close (`theory/GENERAL_THEOREM.md` §0 item 5). That kills the mechanism for that proxy and scale, not the index where errors really are diffusive.

## 5. The missing lever: reduce the world's drift

Every earlier rule treated drift σ as given. Real adaptive organizations spend heavily on *lowering* it:
- **homeostasis and allostasis** hold an internal milieu steady so fewer variables need tracking (Sterling 2012);
- **niche construction and stigmergy** reshape the environment so it changes less, which Lee, Flack & Krakauer (2022 preprint; 2024) call outsourced memory; planetary-scale versions appear in Lenton et al. 2018;
- **buffering and smoothing** protect a firm's core (Thompson 1967, *classic, not re-fetched*);
- **contracts, schedules, regulation and routines** fix parts of the future in advance (no single citation was verified for this list in the sweep; treat it as the theory's own framing).

**The change.** Add a control on σ with its own cost g(σ₀ → σ). Tracking and drift reduction then become substitutes. The draft makes three predictions (HYPOTHESIS, untested):
1. **Substitution:** at matched stakes, organizations that spend more on reducing drift spend less on watching, so "under-watching" relative to the tracking rules is expected wherever drift has been bought down. Without this lever, such cases look like failures of the theory.
2. **Who pays:** drift reduction is favoured when its benefits are private. When they are shared, it is a public good, under-supplied without enforcement (Lee, Flack & Krakauer 2024; §6).
3. **One testable form** (speculative): firms facing more demand volatility lengthen customer contracts when they can capture the benefit (SEC full-text contract terms vs sales volatility).

## 6. Levels, shared memory and who is in charge

**When a new level pays.** A level is a shared model of a common driver. Three gains can justify it, each with its own condition: pooling (one model serves many facts; T3a threshold, N2), per-level clocks (at most 29% saving for two levels; T3b, KNOWN via Roundy 1985), and per-level checking tiers. Against these it pays upkeep plus a decoupling error that grows as timescales collapse (T3c). There is no theorem that nested always beats flat; with no shared drift, nesting loses by its upkeep.

**Shared memory is a commons.** A trail, a price, a wiki page or a shared library is stigmergic memory. Lee, Flack & Krakauer 2024 show environmental stabilization is a public good that free riders invade unless its benefits are monopolized. The math attack shows decentralized parts under-watch shared memory unless the whole's failure cost reaches them (`theory/MATH_ATTACK.md` §3.4), whether by incentives, shared fate or a central controller. Ostrom's work (Ostrom 1990, *classic, not re-fetched*; Ostrom 2010) shows that lasting commons have rules, monitoring and sanctions.

**Narrowed claim.** Earlier drafts said "no one in charge, at every level". The claim now reads:
> **No one *needs* to be in charge *when* maintaining shared memory is privately rewarded** (each contributor's own use pays for its deposits). **Otherwise expect shared fate or enforcement, usually both**: shared fate needs no one in charge but is usually partial, and enforcement means monitors, sanctions, owners or hierarchy, appearing where shared memory is a public good. Social insects mostly fit these routes rather than private reward: workers (many cannot breed, but workers of many species lay male eggs) *partly* share the colony's fate (relatedness r; in the α = 2 reset-read case watching falls to about √r of its optimum, and r^{1/α} in general; r = 3/4, full sisters under single mating, is the best case: ≈ 0.87, so ≈ 13% under-watching, not zero; with multiple mating r is lower and under-watching larger), and since self-restraint through relatedness is not sufficient on its own (Frank 1995), some bees, wasps and ants also police (Ratnieks 1988 ties worker policing to multiple mating, per the technical review's reading of its abstract) (v1.4.20; `theory/BOOKENDS.md` §3). Hypothesis, not a consequence: CLAIM_MAP J9 stays OPEN.

Prediction (HYPOTHESIS, untested): the staleness of shared memory rises with users per maintainer (e.g. package dependents per active maintainer vs documentation lag after an API change), and enforcement structures appear where that ratio is high.

**Fractal vigilance (night 1).** The idea that every level has a tracker that watches what its own members share (shared and private are relative to the level) is **prior art qualitatively**: Beer's Viable System Model puts System 4 ("outside and then") at every level of recursion, and Mintzberg makes every manager the unit's "monitor". The quantitative version is the theory's own and is **untested on fair data**:
- the exact onset vigilance V(N\*) = ½(1 + (1+ρ)^{−1/2})(1 + √(1 + 4h′(√(1+ρ)−1)/(rρ))) for reset-type sensing at α = 2 (the level's effort over a *flat* member's effort), where r = p_g/p is the level's price over a member's and h′ is upkeep in member-cost units (`reviews/night1/CLAIM_MAP_FINAL.md` E6; the r in the denominator is intended, not a typo);
- its floor 1 + (1+ρ)^{−1/2} for any upkeep and price;
- its price invariance at zero upkeep;
- its growth as N^a (a = 1/α for reset reads).

The PIAAC and Enron tests measured a different quantity (supervisors versus the people they supervise, self-report, received mail). A fair-kill specification is in `reviews/night1/CLAIM_MAP_FINAL.md` §3.2.

**The √N sign flip** (N1) lives here: pooled independent deposits make faster turnover optimal for drifting targets, while repeated use preserves forms for stable targets. Its required condition is *independent* deposits; trail-followers who deposit where the trail already is have a smaller effective N.

## 7. Where optimality should hold, and where it should not

The theory describes efficient design. Efficient design appears only where selection is strong enough to find it.
- **Drift barrier.** Lynch et al. 2016 (and Lynch 2010) show that below a selection-strength threshold, set by effective population size, traits such as replication fidelity drift away from their optimum because selection cannot see small gains. The same ceiling should apply to any level: small populations, protected firms, bureaucracies and monopolies should sit *further* from the rules here.
- **Inertia and lock-in.** Hannan & Freeman (1984, as cited in the sweep) argue selection can favour inertia because reorganizing raises mortality; lock-in means past choices, not current drift, can set memory.
- **Rule of use.** Expect the predictions to hold in strongly competed, high-turnover settings (bacteria under strong selection, competitive industries, sensory systems tested every second). Expect misses where selection is weak. A miss in a weakly selected system is weak evidence against the theory; a miss under strong selection is strong evidence. That split must be fixed **before** a test, never used to explain a miss afterward.
- **Speculative extension:** how close each level gets to its cost floor (c_eff/kT for cells; cost per decision-relevant bit for firms) should scale with selection strength.

## 9. What would sink the theory

- **Measured-input optimality fails twice (§1 N6):** in strongly selected systems at two scales, with α, q, N and stakes measured on the same system and a locked test scored against the optimum its prereg fixed (best linear or Bayes; best linear if it named none; §1 N6), the observed read rate, memory time or store count misses its §2.2 optimum beyond measurement error, and a named rival (fixed budget share; universal ½) fits better.
- **The causal relation fails twice:** at two scales, with locked tests, the design quantity's measured move, after the adjustment lag fixed in its prereg, falls short, beyond measurement error, of the share κ_F fixed in its prereg of the move its locked optimum predicts (§1 N6: 0 < κ_F ≤ ½, the prediction scaled by measured adjustment share and input reliability), while the system's measured spectrum, drift level, noise or stakes (or, for a Bayes-locked test, the further inputs of §1 N6) change.
- **The onset exponent tracks the spectrum instead of the technology:** across systems with the same sensing technology (including read noise relative to drift per read), the same α for shared and private drift within each system, and different measured α, the pooling-onset exponent moves with α (§2.5 predicts it does not; in-model result, refereed).
- **The fixed-spread sign reverses** (positive slope) at two scales in the cold regime.
- **√N fails on languages and wikis** as specified in N1, and the drift-dependent sign flip is absent.
- **New levels ignore ρ** (N2) where the threshold can be measured.
- **Strong selection, still no fit:** in strongly selected systems, designs show no relation to drift, buffers or spectra at all. Misses in weakly selected systems do not count toward this (§7), but only if the system was classified before the test.
- **Vigilance under a fair instrument:** on a pre-declared, same-driver, all-channel sampling measure against a flat comparator, in units measured to be reset-type (class R, α = 2), the level's effort stays clearly below the floor 1 + (1+ρ)^{−1/2} that holds for every upkeep and price (spec: `reviews/night1/CLAIM_MAP_FINAL.md` §3.2).
- **The onset relation in agents not given the cost function:** with ρ manipulated over ≥ 1.5 decades in a regime where the prize exceeds decision noise, and the level's sensing class measured separately, the onset exponent excludes −(1−b_L)/(1−a_L).
- **Structural:** every claim must stay tied to an exponent, a sign or a threshold with a named competitor. A theory of "any adaptive organization" that drifts into unfalsifiable language should be treated as failed, as the free-energy principle has been criticized for (Aguilera et al. 2022; Bruineberg et al. 2022).

# The core: one cost, one method, one hypothesis

*Sunday 22 November 2026. The full argument behind the front page, with every number. Rewritten as one argument from the previous version ([`history/2026-11-22_CORE_before_synthesis.md`](https://github.com/Chad-Mitchell/theory-of-adaptive-organization/blob/1eed183f3888e8e3eead5956bbf1e8cb4233510a/history/2026-11-22_CORE_before_synthesis.md)); keep, fold, and demote decisions are in [`history/2026-11-22_SYNTHESIS_DECISIONS.md`](https://github.com/Chad-Mitchell/theory-of-adaptive-organization/blob/1eed183f3888e8e3eead5956bbf1e8cb4233510a/history/2026-11-22_SYNTHESIS_DECISIONS.md). The picture is [`figures/master_curve.png`](figures/master_curve.png) (claims and sources: `figures/master_curve.md`); the earlier derivation map is `figures/derivation_map.png`.*

Thermodynamics tells an engineer two things about any engine: the best it could possibly do, and how far a real one falls short. This theory aims to do the same for adaptive systems: cells, colonies, brains, teams, firms, AI and robot systems, anything that must act before its world arrives. Its object is the cost of judgment: being wrong or late plus the cost of holding, acquiring, and sharing information, under a cap on the chance of ruin. In the proved physical class, the memory term is bath heat—at least \(kT\) times the rate at which the memory learns, for bipartite chains and additive-noise diffusions—plus the stake on error (a separate display from `THEORY.md`'s five-term sum, open work T40). In worked classes the minimum is computed, in closed form where the class allows, and it says what the best design looks like: how many layers, what to share, where a boundary is enough, when a shock should strengthen a system rather than harm it. If the hypothesis below holds, any adaptive system can be measured against its minimum, and the gap says where it wastes and what would close it.

**The law of adaptive organization (hypothesis): strongly selected systems minimize the cost of judgment.**

The argument runs in one line. The cost is defined for any adaptive system (§1). The method is the same everywhere: write the cost, find its minimum, read off the design (§2). Physics puts a floor under the cost, proved now for broad classes and extended class by class (§3). In a worked class the minimum is computed exactly, and it yields layers, holographic sharing, boundaries, rebuilding, and acting (§4). Where no closed form exists, selection is a search scored by the cost, with a predicted gap to the minimum under stated conditions (§5). The hypothesis is that strongly selected systems sit at that minimum, and the real-world test decides it (§6).

The theory has two parts, and they are different kinds of claim. **Part 1 is mathematics:** the cost of a memory that must act before its world arrives, and the designs that minimize that cost inside a named class. Those limits hold by proof. **Part 2 is a hypothesis about nature.** Only Part 2 can miss a measurement.

Until a system qualifies under THEORY §3, the working name is a principle: one cost, and the design is what that cost selects. The heat floor, class by class, is §3. The heat floor and its minimizers in the worked class are proved in the class named in §4. The telegraph is in that class. Other Gaussian systems stay open, and other linear systems stay open. The list of what stays open is §7. No system has been shown to qualify.

[`THEORY.md`](THEORY.md) is the canonical statement (v2.9 draft). It still displays the five-term sum. Where this page and `THEORY.md` disagree, `THEORY.md` governs. Putting the physics display into THEORY §3 is open work, T40 in [`README.md`, Waiting on Chad](README.md#waiting-on-chad).

## 1. The cost

For bipartite Markov chains and additive-noise diffusions, in steady state the bath of a learning subsystem gives off heat at least equal to temperature times the rate at which the memory learns the world it acts on (Horowitz and Esposito 2014, doi 10.1103/PhysRevX.4.031015). In those classes the inequality is **KNOWN**; beyond those classes it is open:

$$\dot Q \ge kT \times \text{(learning rate)}.$$

It bounds a heat. It does not choose a stake, a link price, or a ruin cap.

Inside the class below, the cost of judgment is that heat plus a stake on the decision loss. The ruin cap stays outside the sum:

$$J = \dot Q + w\,\mathbb{E}[\ell] \qquad\text{subject to}\qquad P(\text{ruin})\le\varepsilon.$$

\(w\) converts the loss into the currency of the heat. Temperature does not set \(w\).

The five-term sum is the display THEORY §3 still prints. It is a different object from the heat-plus-stake sum. Lemma LY, proved in-model under H1–H3 (`theory/GENERAL_FORM.md`), says a growth maximizer is Pareto in a chosen bad vector. It does not choose the vector. The five-term sum is the special case in which a Pareto front is ranked by risk-neutral expected currency, the prices are linear and nonnegative, and ruin is a half-space cap. Growth and that currency have different argmins. The ideal-gas comparison in that note is this list of extra assumptions. Occupation of the front is open. IP5, the claim that the master equation is the physical frontier, is false (`theory/GENERAL_FORM_PHYSICS_2026-11-22.md` §2).

In that display, \(\kappa\) is the price of one nat of memory \(I_{\mathrm{mem}}\), \(h\) is the upkeep of one layer, \(K\) is the number of layers, \(A\) is the price of a read, and \(L\) is the price of a link. The stake \(w\) and the expected loss \(\mathbb{E}[\ell]\) are the same quantities as in the heat-plus-stake sum.

$$\kappa I_{\mathrm{mem}}+hK+w\,\mathbb{E}[\ell]+A+L \qquad\text{subject to}\qquad P(\text{ruin})\le\varepsilon.$$

| Term | Inside the proved class |
|---|---|
| \(\kappa I_{\mathrm{mem}}\) | Replaced by the bath heat \(\dot Q\). Floor: \(kT\) times the learning rate. The stock rental is not the coordinate. |
| \(hK\) | Outside the floor. A constant \(h\) still on the books is excess over the heat. |
| \(w\,\mathbb{E}[\ell]\) | Survives. \(w\) stays assumed. |
| \(A\) | Stays assumed as a separate summand. A continuous coupling's read is inside \(\dot Q\). |
| \(L\) | Stays assumed, and stays outside the heat-plus-stake sum. The five-term display still prints it. |
| Ruin cap | Stays assumed, and stays outside both. |

## 2. The method, and what holds in general

The method is the same in every class: write the cost, find its minimum, and read off the design. Shannon's theory is the pattern for why proved limits can be decisive before any experiment: a quantity fixed by the task, a converse that no design beats, achievability, and a formula computed from the world's statistics alone (`theory/SHANNON_2026-11-21.md` §0). Here the converse is the information floor: in a stationary world the system does not control, every design pays at least for the predictive information it holds (Theorem S1, building on CT1 and CT4). The minimum over designs is fixed by a price-free frontier: every minimizer at positive prices is undominated, and on a convex feasible set every undominated design is a minimizer at some nonzero nonnegative prices (Theorem S3), and the minimum is the support function of that set (Theorem S2). So waste splits in two, distance from the frontier (technical, measurable without prices, after Farrell 1957) and the wrong point on it (allocative). The Shannon note is a draft whose fixes have not been re-reviewed. Problems also nest: a team's minimized cost folds exactly into its parent's problem by partial minimization, with a stated error when it does not enter as upkeep (CT34, `THEORY.md`).

These hold without a Gaussian or linear step. The first is a theorem about any system that meets the representation axioms A1–A7 (`theory/REPRESENTATION.md`). The others hold under the conditions stated with each (`theory/GENERALITY_2026-11-22.md`).

1. **The cost is a priced sum.** For any system that meets A1–A7, on at least three essential interval coordinates, a continuous cost that represents its preferences is an additive price sum, up to one positive scale and one additive constant, and its memory coordinate is mutual information in the sense of A6 (Theorem R1): finite-law Shannon information on finite alphabets, and, when the mutual information is finite, the Gelfand–Yaglom–Dobrushin supremum of discrete mutual informations, which agrees with a differential mutual information when the densities exist (`theory/CONTINUOUS_A6_2026-11-23.md`). R1 does not choose the coordinate list (A6 fixes only which information is the memory coordinate), so it does not derive the five terms of THEORY §3. Nonnegative prices are a further assumption, A8, and the integer layer count \(hK\) needs an extra separability assumption.
2. **Memory costs heat.** A bipartite memory's bath gives off at least \(kT\) times the information flow of its own transitions, for bipartite chains and for additive-noise diffusions.
3. **Higher stakes buy more judgment.** On a menu that does not depend on the stake, for a cost \(S+wE\) with ruin slack or absent, judgment spend does not fall as the stake rises, and the error does not rise.
4. **A boundary can be enough.** A joint law that factorizes across a cut screens the two sides. The expected loss is a function of the blanket posterior when the loss depends on the world only through the exterior and the decision is a function of the screened side.
5. **Convex payoffs gain from shocks, and caps can reverse it.** An uncapped convex payoff has expectation at least the payoff of the mean; a cap can reverse that comparison.

6. **Zero-gain control drops out.** If the control enters the generator only as an additive drift \(b\,u\), and the martingale problem for the uncontrolled generator is unique, the process at \(b=0\) is the uncontrolled process.

The closed forms in §4 (the holographic-memory factor, the turnover rate, the gains), and the named floors in §3, are worked examples in clean classes. Whether real systems sit near the minimum is the open hypothesis.

## 3. The floor, class by class

The method is the same as everywhere: write the cost, find its minimum, and read off the design. The cost of judgment, as a long-run rate, is the memory-bath heat plus a stake on the decision loss, with the ruin cap outside the sum (§1):

$$J=\dot Q_M+w\,\mathbb{E}[\ell]\qquad\text{subject to}\qquad P(\mathrm{ruin})\le\varepsilon.$$

A lower bound on the heat at a fixed error is a lower bound on \(J\) at that error. The minimum over designs is at least the minimum of that bound plus the stake, over attainable error. Whether a real system sits at that minimum is a measurement (Part 2). No system has been shown to qualify under THEORY §3.

**Heat is at least \(kT\) times the net learning rate.** Under a memory/world split and local detailed balance, or the Einstein relation for additive-noise diffusions, in a steady state or a long-run average, \(\dot Q_M\ge kT\,\dot I_M\), where \(\dot I_M\) is the net information flow of the memory's own transitions. This is **KNOWN** for bipartite jump processes (Horowitz and Esposito 2014, doi 10.1103/PhysRevX.4.031015; Proposition JP-heat in `theory/GENERALITY_2026-11-22.md`; `theory/GENERAL_FLOOR_ATTEMPT_2026-11-23.md` §3, T57) and **PROVED (in-model)** for additive-noise diffusions (Proposition EQ-heat). Opposing jumps at equilibrium cancel: the bound is on the net flow, not on unsigned traffic (`theory/FREEZE_OR_HOLD_FLOOR_2026-11-23.md` §1, T59; `theory/GENERAL_FLOOR_FINITE_2026-11-23.md` §3, T60). The general form of the floor is that thermodynamic step composed with the world's causal learning-rate–distortion function \(\Phi\): \(\dot Q_M\ge kT\,\Phi(d)\) is exact wherever the thermodynamic step holds, for every such world (T57 §0, §3–§4). Closed-form, design-independent lower bounds on \(\Phi\) exist only in named classes.

**A world-only positive floor, when the world is connected.** For an exogenous finite-state world in a steady state, the net learning rate equals a flux-weighted sum of KL divergences of the memory's conditionals (the stationary Horowitz–Esposito / Barato–Hartich–Seifert identity; T60 §3). If the occupied flux graph is strongly connected — in particular, if the world is irreducible — then a vanishing net learning rate implies that the Bayes error is at chance: any memory that beats chance has a strictly positive, world-only floor. That implication is **PROVED (in-model), one direction only** (T60 §0, §3). The converse fails for three or more states: a memory can sit at chance Bayes error while still distinguishing non-mode states. Two explicit world-only bounds then hold for any finite memory (T60 §4): \(\dot I_M\ge(\varepsilon_0-\varepsilon)^2/K_X\), with \(K_X\) computed from the world's rates alone, and a pairwise \(L\) bound that diverges as the error goes to zero when a well-occupied pair shares flux. Then \(J\ge kT\,\varphi(\varepsilon)+w\varepsilon\), with \(\varphi\) the pointwise maximum of those bounds (T60 §5).

**On the telegraph the floor is tight:** \(\dot Q_M\ge kT\,r\,L(\varepsilon)\), with \(L(\varepsilon)=(1-2\varepsilon)\ln((1-\varepsilon)/\varepsilon)\), for any finite memory, with equality at the symmetric two-state copy (T60 §4; T59 §0–§1 for the two-state record; already the proved telegraph infimum in `theory/GENERAL_FORM_PHYSICS_2026-11-22.md` §4). A leak cancels and does not change the floor. The design that approaches it is a fast copy at the minimal affinity (T60 §5; IP3 in `theory/GENERAL_FORM_PHYSICS_2026-11-22.md` §4).

**On a drifting continuous world,** one Einstein spring tracking a random walk has learning rate \(\sigma^2/(2\varepsilon)\). A Kalman-matched memory, built the textbook way with matched noise, pays exactly twice the minimum heat. A fast spring at the same error reaches the infimum \(kT\,\sigma^2/(2\varepsilon)\). Both are **PROVED (in-model)** for this family (`theory/DRIFT_HEAT_LIFT_2026-11-23.md` §0, §3–§4, T58). The factor two is the same accounting already **KNOWN** for a path-sufficient or capacity-one device (Sandberg, Delvenne, Newton, and Mitter 2014; Hartich, Barato, and Seifert 2016). No general prefactor is claimed.

**Frozen or disconnected worlds allow zero ongoing heat.** A static record in a frozen world, or a memory that holds only the label of a disconnected class, can beat a no-memory guess at vanishing ongoing memory-bath heat after a one-time write (T57 §0, §4; T59 §0, §4; T60 §0, §3). The stake on error remains. Storage is cheap. Tracking a changing, connected world is what costs. That is an open boundary of the general floor, stated as such, not a hidden loophole.

**When the memory also steers the world.** The thermodynamic step still holds under feedback, and in a steady state the net learning rate equals the information used to steer (Horowitz and Esposito 2014). The T60 identity and the exogenous floor are not inherited: the occupied flux is then part of the design, and the net learning rate can fall below \(r\,L(\varepsilon)\) or change sign. A design-independent positive floor on memory heat alone, in terms of a task error, is false once the memory may act. A conservative trap of the plant, or a joint conservative coupling, has vanishing ongoing heat at a Boltzmann residual (T57's loophole, now on the plant). In the linear class with additive feedback at fixed process noise, a compensated Kalman–Bucy implementation keeps the exogenous learning rate and a factor-two memory-bath heat, both independent of the gain; the world-bath heat is the actuation cost and diverges as the regulated variance is pushed down to the estimation error. Acting does not reduce that tracking floor. An exogenous coordinate the memory tracks, while it slaves a different plant, keeps the T59 floor. These are **PROVED (in-model)** in the named classes (`theory/FEEDBACK_FLOOR_2026-11-23.md`, T61; independent review reported in [the T61 row of the retired work list](https://github.com/Chad-Mitchell/theory-of-adaptive-organization/blob/1eed183f3888e8e3eead5956bbf1e8cb4233510a/OPEN_WORK.md#L112); no review record committed). T61 prices heat for a passive plant at fixed noise. It is not the co-adaptation statement F3/F4. Dual control, and a closed-form infimum of total heat at fixed variance over all physical designs, stay open.

**Still open:** a closed form of the learning-rate–distortion function for a general world (T57 §4; T60 §0, §6); which memories attain the floor outside the telegraph (T60 §5); worlds the memory itself moves, beyond the named classes of T61 (T60 §6; T61); and the remaining items of CX1 (CLAIM_MAP PD8). In a world with no stationary law the information floor is already a flow, in its class: for a linear world with independent shocks, any design must take in new information at least as fast as the drift wears the old away, and for a random walk the error obeys \(D\ge q/(e^{2\bar r}-1)\), met with equality by the Kalman filter (`theory/THEOREM_P_DRIFT.md` §0; CLAIM_MAP PD1; re-confirmed 23 November 2026). The continuous-information A6 extension (T48) is in `theory/CONTINUOUS_A6_2026-11-23.md`. The live work list is [`README.md`, Ranked gaps](README.md#ranked-gaps). The goal is a floor for every adaptive system. None is claimed for every class here.

## 4. The worked class and what its minimum buys

**The class.** An exogenous equilibrium Ornstein–Uhlenbeck signal, independent signals of that kind, or the two-state telegraph. The memory does not move the signal. The loss is the mean squared error of an action of the current memory, or Hamming error on the telegraph. Proofs: `theory/INFO_PHYSICS_ATTAINABILITY.md`, `theory/INFO_PHYSICS_GENERAL.md`, consolidated in `theory/GENERAL_FORM_PHYSICS_2026-11-22.md`.

**PROVED in that class.**

- One OU signal, rate \(a\), variance \(v\), decision error \(\varepsilon\): the heat infimum is \(kT\,a(v-\varepsilon)/\varepsilon\), approached by a fast linear memory at finite heat.
- Above the stake \(kT a/v\) the minimizing cost is \(2\sqrt{kT\,a\,v\,w}-kT\,a\). At or below it the design is blind: heat \(0\), error \(v\), cost \(wv\).
- Equal-variance OU modes on a geometric ladder, each scored on its own: the number tracked is \(\lfloor\log(wv/(kT a))/\log\rho\rfloor+1\).
- The error in a sum of modes: one rank-one memory, gains \(1/(a_k+\mu)\), heat \(kT\mu\). Separate memories of \(K\) identical modes cost \(K\) times that heat.
- The telegraph: flow \(\gamma(1-2\varepsilon)\ln((1-\varepsilon)/\varepsilon)\) nats per unit time. The heat infimum is \(kT\) times that flow.

**When memory pays.** The curve's threshold \(x=1\) is the physical form of a result the theory already had for discrete memories. On the predictive information-bottleneck curve the first bit pays only if its price is below the loss it saves (CT4, `THEORY.md` Appendix B.1), and with that price at its thermal floor the condition reads as a stakes-to-thermal ratio above 1; where real costs sit far above the floor, the niche price sets the threshold instead (`core/bridge_2026-11-21/FLOOR.md`). The same minimum has a frozen corner and a lookup edge. For one item on the five-term sum, freezing is best while the drift stays below \(q_f=2c/(W\tau_{\mathrm{life}}^2)\), and looking up beats holding once it exceeds \(q_c=(\mu u-h)^2/(2cW)\) (CT11, `THEORY.md`; freezing as a process in `theory/FREEZE_PROCESS_2026-11-21.md`). At the ends of the physical scale these corners place atoms at the zero-model corner and black holes at the extreme of the frozen class, given published physics and without deriving any of it (`theory/SCALE_ENDS.md` §0).

Six uses. The first five are proved in the worked class or beside it, and their synthesis is `theory/ACTING_LOOP_2026-11-22.md` §8. Of the sixth, stay, pooling onset, alignment, the leave cliff and the re-form band are proved in the classes of `THEORY.md` Appendix B (CT9, CT14, CT29, CT31, Lemma B1 and the CL19 band), not in the worked class above; the merge condition and remove-or-reprice are conjectures. The tamper condition is proved when the part shares the whole's full stay-versus-leave cost, including collateral and any priced ruin term (`theory/SWARM_RULE_2026-11-23.md` §4). One theorem that produces all six from the action in the drift is not on this page.

**Layers.** A stack is the cheapest design when one store cannot reset every component. On the ladder above, the count has no free constant per layer. Each layer's lifetime comes from the same trade: a lumpy store with mean interval \(T^*=\sqrt{6k/(w\sigma^2)}\) under the band rule balances its reset cost \(k\) against the drift loss, and one such store is already optimal for a single process, so a stack appears only when layers are specialized (`theory/LAYER_LIFETIME_2026-11-21.md`). An application of the hold rule and this refresh time to one assistant's working memory is `applications/GROK_BOT_MEMORY_2026-11-23.md`. The older placement view, [`figures/face.png`](figures/face.png), draws at each time scale the cheapest of local, shared, both, or neither; the master picture is `figures/master_curve.png`. Under the closed loop the conjecture is that the relevant reversion is \(a+bK\), and that multi-rate plant remains open.

**Holographic memory.** In the storage sense (many items in one shared state), a shared memory costs less when the decision is a combination of items, ties or costs more when every item is read on its own, and a random code is beaten by an aligned readout when the decision is known (`theory/HOLOGRAPHIC_MEMORY_2026-11-22.md`). In the field sense, at a fixed per-site error the heat scales with the volume, and scales with a cut only when the decision names a cut: about \(13.61\) per cut site on the worked square lattice, against a lower bound of \(13\) (`theory/HOLOGRAPHIC_2026-11-22.md`). Holographic memory is not a separate assumption: the one cost equation predicts when it wins. On the five-term sum, in the upkeep-free power class, the same economy appears as pooling: one tracker of a common driver with pooled stake \(nW\) costs \(n^{1/\alpha}\) times one tracker, less than \(n\) separate trackers (CT28, `THEORY.md`).

**Boundary.** For a decision outside a region, the blanket is sufficient and the interior is not (`theory/BOUNDARY_SUFFICIENCY_2026-11-22.md`). On the path of three the exterior variance given the blanket differs from the variance given the region alone (\(3/5\) against \(1/2\)). The matching blanket channel costs at most the Schur trace more than the channel it matches. Screening needs a local precision and a Markov world. A continuous-time heat kernel is positive at every distance, so no finite shell screens. Through a boundary of finite-capacity channels the tracked learning rate is capped by the boundary's capacity, so it scales with area when capacity per area is bounded; frozen storage is not capped, and this is not the holographic bound (T62, `theory/AREA_LAW_PROBE_2026-11-23.md`; review fixes recorded at d79e766; independent review reported by T63 §1; no standalone review record).

**Rebuild, and gaining from shocks.** The cost predicts when a system gains from disorder and when it does not. That is a use of the same cost, not a separate principle. The canonical statement is `theory/ANTIFRAGILITY_CONJECTURE_2026-11-23.md`. What is proved is proved in its own class. What would make a general gain from closing the loop is an open theorem, stated there as a conjecture (T50).

In the linear-Gaussian class, with the ruin cap slack, the minimized cost \(J^*\) is concave in volatility, so a spread of volatility lowers expected cost if the system can redesign per regime (Jensen). With one fixed design there is no such gain. That is the Oi–Hartman–Abel effect, read inside this cost (`theory/ROBUSTNESS.md` §6, CT40; Oi 1961, doi 10.2307/1907687; Hartman 1972, doi 10.1016/0022-0531(72)90105-6; Abel 1984, doi 10.1016/0165-1889(84)90047-2). Taleb and Douady 2013 (doi 10.1080/14697688.2013.800219) define antifragility as convexity in the stressor; a concave cost is that definition applied to \(J\). The option to redesign is worth buying only when redesign is faster than the regime and cheaper than the Jensen gain. The cost of redesign is still open. A binding ruin cap can break the concavity. A naked option, premium 1 against \(\mathbb{E}[\max(X,0)]=\sqrt{\theta/(2\pi)}\), drops in level with no channel and no heat. The sign bet is a capped loss, and the financial name for that bet adds no comparison the cost does not already make.

A second route is learning, and it is open-loop. Fixed observation noise, a probe price that is not proportional to the variance, and a loss that at fixed fraction depends on the variance only through that fraction: the probe price of the learned fraction falls as \(1/\theta\), and that fall is the derivative of the minimized sign cost (`theory/ACTING_MEMORY_2026-11-22.md`). In that same model \(J^*\) is strictly decreasing in \(\theta\). At the scored point \(\theta=1\) it is convex, so a spread of volatility around that point raises the cost. The world equation contains no action. The construction of \(\alpha\) places the operating point; it is not the source of the \(1/\theta\) identity. This level drop is not evidence from closing the loop.

Under the ruin cap, replacing identical units can lower the long-run cost: at rate 1, cost 15.473978 with ruin probability 0.020940, against the inadmissible zero at cost 16 (`theory/antifragility_checks/RESULTS.md`). The unconstrained grid minimum is inadmissible. The heterogeneous arm minimizes at no shock, cost 13. The open miss at this cap: heat is proportional to precision, and the admissible points keep the more precise units. A failure time independent of the world adds nothing about the world. A failure that depends on the state cuts the variance, in the one Cox comparison that was scored, and the part is then gone (`theory/ACTING_LOOP_2026-11-22.md` §6).

The barbell is not a general result of this cost. Where the cost-versus-ruin frontier is convex, mixing two extremes gains exactly zero. A discrete mix can shave a little (6.555 mean copies against 7 at \(\varepsilon=5\times 10^{-4}\)) only if the cap binds before the draw. A system that then drew 6 copies has ruin probability \(10^{-3}\), which exceeds \(\varepsilon\). That is randomization, not a safe-plus-speculative barbell (`theory/DYNAMICS.md`; the condition is ex-ante against ex-post binding).

Closing the loop, in the scored linear-quadratic-Gaussian model, shrinks the information stock memory must carry and does not shrink the learning-rate heat. At unit stake and \((a,\theta)=(1,1)\), with heat and probe charged, the loop is dearer than sensing at every fraction, and the minimum is the blind cost \(q\theta\), which rises with volatility. At stake 4 the loop is slightly cheaper, by 0.033889928154 at the minimizing fraction (`theory/ACTING_LOOP_2026-11-22.md`). The spread of the minimizing closed loop was not scored. The conjecture — a bet, not a result — is that antifragility falls out of minimizing the cost once the predict-act-correct loop is closed. The joining theorem is open.

**Acting.** World \(dX=-aX\,dt+b u_a\,dt+\sqrt{2a\theta}\,dW\). With the channel unpriced, the closed loop is cheaper than sensing alone by \((q-2aP(a))\theta\). At unit stake, unit drift, and unit variance that gap is 0.171572875254, against a sensing-alone cost of 1. With the heat floor and the probe price charged, that gap is negative at every fraction when the stake is 1, and at stake 4 the gap at the minimizing fraction is 0.033889928154 (`theory/ACTING_LOOP_2026-11-22.md`). Feedback shrinks the stock \(\tfrac12\log(v/p)\) and does not shrink the learning-rate heat. The separated gain is certainty equivalence for unconstrained linear-quadratic-Gaussian control. A tight variance cap, and a capped loss, each move the gain. An open-loop probe makes the minimized sign cost fall with volatility. The slope equals minus the probe price of the fraction over \(\theta^2\) (`theory/ACTING_MEMORY_2026-11-22.md`). The derivation that puts the action in the drift is merged as [PR #203](https://github.com/Chad-Mitchell/theory-of-adaptive-organization/pull/203) (T47). Theorem R1′ is a representation theorem, the cubic ordering is a conjecture, and the proposal diff is not applied to THEORY.

## 5. The evolutionary route

Where no closed form exists, a search scored by the cost finds the minimum. Weak-mutation selection with fitness linear in the cost has the stationary law \(\pi\propto\exp(-\beta J)\), with \(\beta\) set by selection strength, and near a quadratic minimum in \(d\) coordinates its predicted excess is \(\mathbb{E}[J]-J^*=d/(2\beta)\) (`core/bridge_2026-11-21/BRIDGE.md`, `core/bridge_2026-11-21/SELECTION.md`, `theory/SELECTION_AXIOMS.md`, `theory/DRIFT_SELECTION.md`). This is the link between the mathematics and the hypothesis: it says how real systems could reach the minimum without solving anything, and the same rule is a design method for evolutionary algorithms, with the floor bounding what is left to gain. It holds only under four conditions a designer must check, each with an in-repo counterexample when violated:

1. **Strong selection.** T27 P1's high-\(\beta\) mode held while its low-\(\beta\) prediction P3 failed (`core/bridge_2026-11-21/RESULTS.md`).
2. **A reachable minimum with no uncrossable valley.** EVO1 W1/W2 and SIM-1 converged in clean worlds, but EVO1 W4 stalled behind a creation valley (3/8) and every W4b seed stopped at the no-sharing rival (8/8) (`tests/EVO1/RESULTS_EVO.md`, `core/sim1_2026-11-21/`).
3. **A cost that includes the relevant terms.** EVO1's store-noise arms were INCONCLUSIVE on the cost band (`tests/EVO1/RESULTS_EVO.md`).

Two proved classes do. A beta-potential game settles near a Nash point, the joint optimum when cost shares match stakes (CT31; `theory/COADAPTATION.md` CA1). A strongly monotone game that is not a potential game is stable, with an excess bound (CA5). Exact Boltzmann reversibility holds if and only if the beta-scaled game is an exact potential, which is narrower than convergence. Opposed coupling can cycle (CT32, F1). In the linear copy-gain model a gain above one runs away, and a gain of one loses confinement (F3). Delayed push-back, with gain below −1, can make stronger selection destabilize (F4). A cost of its own is not by itself a joint potential. The world is a party we chose not to model as a player; the one-way limit of this file's heat floors, outside T61's named classes, is that choice.

## 6. The hypothesis, and how a miss is read

The full rule is THEORY §3. A class qualifies with a preregistered cost-to-fitness map, a population proxy, a band selection can resolve that excludes a named rival, and a redesign time with at least ten of them in the same world. Cities, nation-states, global economies, and the biosphere wait for a proxy. The class fixes the cost terms, the world's spectrum and noise, and the prices before measuring.

**Join, stay and leave (swarms).** The same cost says when parts come together, hold, break apart and re-form. That is a use of the cost, not a separate principle. The canonical statement is `theory/SWARM_RULE_2026-11-23.md`. A shared layer forms once enough parts face a common driver (CT14). A part keeps following the whole iff \(\lambda s c<w\Phi\): how fast the whole's signal changes, times the part's share of the price of following, must stay below the part's own stake in following times what the signal is worth to it. This is CT9 with the part's net share \(sc/w\) as the read price. Above that line, leaving is the part's cheapest move, a pricing failure and not malice. As the signal's change rate rises, following effort first rises and then falls steeply to zero (Lemma B1). The part's choice is the whole's iff \(s=w/W\), shares in proportion to stakes (CT29, CT31). Shared-driver play reaches the Nash point, and that point equals the joint optimum only when the shares match the stakes. The stay clause assumes the part does not move the signal. The five terms multiply, so two partial levers can do what one full lever does. Tampering with the whole's signal or its enforcement is one more action. Its gain is zero at \(s=w/W\) when the part shares the whole's full stay-versus-leave cost, including collateral and any priced ruin term, so an aligned part never tampers in that case; if the whole enforces because of unshared collateral or a ruin cap, the gain can be positive even at that share (the swarm rule §4). Corrupting a shared signal the part itself reads costs it the signal's worth, and \(N\) independent checks deter a gain \(D\) iff \(D<Nt+(1-(1-q)^N)P\). A grouping is re-formed when its gap from the best grouping crosses the CL19 band, so group lifetimes scale as the square root of the re-forming cost. These pieces are proved in their classes. The merge condition (CX2, CX3) and remove-or-reprice are conjectures. Reading them onto cells, AI agents, people or biofilms is an interpretation. The cases are `applications/CANCER_DEFECTION_2026-11-23.md` and `applications/AI_AGENT_OVERSIGHT_2026-11-23.md`, and the open tests are T51–T53. A purpose-aligned swarm whose parts are free to join and leave is the design the rule selects when each part pays the share of the cost of judgment that matches its share of the stake.

A qualifying design outside the preregistered band is a miss for that class. The allowance, the diagnosis, and the next test are fixed with the preregistration. A cost the list omitted is a miss of the same kind. So is a design that does not move with a measured change in its inputs, except where the gap that remains sits inside the redesign band at the lower bound of the preregistered redesign cost (CT41, `theory/DYNAMICS.md` §2). A field result with no recorded qualification check is not a test of the hypothesis. The bet is untested. The directional published-record test `DIR1` (`tests/DIR1/`) was INCONCLUSIVE (29/42); published coding bounds such as Palmer et al. 2015 and Mattingly et al. 2021 are outside context only, not tests of this hypothesis.

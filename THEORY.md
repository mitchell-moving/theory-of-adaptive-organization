## 1. Judgment and its cost

Everything runs down. The second law lets order last only where something pays to keep it, exporting disorder as it goes. An atom or a crystal pays almost nothing: nothing it does changes whether it lasts, so a model of the world would buy it nothing. A star or a flame pays a steady flow of energy and regulates itself, but anticipates nothing. Cells, colonies, people, firms and cities are different. They must stay far from equilibrium in a world that keeps changing and can ruin them, and they cannot wait for that world to arrive. Once acting late is costly, it pays to carry a model and act ahead.

That is **judgment**, and judgment has one cost: holding memory, being wrong or late, and buying and sharing the information that keeps memory fresh, all without going broke. The theory adds no physics to the second law. Physics enters only as a floor under the prices (§3), which above the cell is slack; the theory prices what staying alive takes, and says the price sets the design: where the world changes on several time scales, build in layers matched to the time scales of the world, as many as the stakes can pay for and faster looking cannot replace (a rhythm counts two, by degree). The same cost decides when a model should be shared, who must be in charge, and how far one tracker can reach.

The bet is that strongly selected systems (defined operationally in §3), at every scale, sit near that cost's minimum. The hypothesis is refuted, for a substrate class, when qualifying systems, with their worlds and costs measured before the comparison, put their designs outside the preregistered band around the minimum more often than chance allows at the preregistered coverage (for a small class, once), or need a cost not on the preregistered list; it is refuted across scales when two classes at different scales miss (§3, §8).

## 2. The claim

**The claim is that one cost sets how adaptive systems should be built at every scale: in layers matched to the time scales of the world, as many as the stakes can pay for and faster looking cannot replace.**

*Matched* means what the theory proves and no more. The count follows the world: a layer for each separated real time scale the stakes can afford, counted by degree, so a rhythm (one period) is two layers by degree (with sensing fixed and ruin not binding; when reads can stand in for a layer the count can fall as stakes rise, while total judgment spend still rises if ruin is slack, CT33). The timing does not copy the world: each layer's memory is set by its own mode **and** the sensing noise, and at the full optimum (one layer per separated mode, K equal to the world's degree) every layer runs faster than its mode, between the mode's own rate and the next mode's (the filter's poles interlace the world's, L2; interlacing is proved only there, and with fewer layers than modes a store does not track a single mode): a 90-day mode gets a store of 6–12 days in one worked case, 7–15× faster, so layers are *matched to* the time scales in number and order, not *set at* them. That timing has a premise (Model S+, `theory/STORE_NOISE.md`): it holds when the noise a store makes itself is small next to the sensing noise, as in Model S. When a store's own noise dominates, with input-referred variance ∝ τ^−(2−2s), the best one-store memory is λτ\* = (1 − s)/s at low signal-to-noise and shorter at any positive signal-to-noise (λ the mode's rate, τ\* the memory; E1-a, `theory/store_noise/threshold_check.py`). So the store is faster than its mode at any positive signal-to-noise when s > 1/2, matches its mode at low signal-to-noise when s = 1/2 (λτ* = 1), and is slower at low signal-to-noise when s < 1/2; a price on speed (upkeep h0 + h1/τ, h1 > 0) lengthens it further (E2, `theory/store_noise/checks.py` §4). Post hoc, E. coli's measured s = 0.31 [0.24, 0.37] gives λτ\* ≈ 2.2 [1.7, 3.2] at low signal-to-noise, a store slower than its mode (measured λτ ≈ 8.5). None of this is new: that the optimal integration time depends on which noise limits is the ten Wolde line of work (closest, Malaguti & ten Wolde 2021), and Tjalma & ten Wolde 2024 give a store-noise account of E. coli's adaptation time (`theory/STORE_NOISE.md` §6). *Physics* supplies the reason upkeep exists and a floor under every price (kT per nat, §3); above the cell those floors are slack (§9), so the claim above is a claim about economics of judgment, not about thermodynamics, and the second law does no further work in it.

## 3. The master equation

A system that must act ahead of a world it does not control chooses its design 𝒟 to

$$
\min_{\mathcal D}\; J(\mathcal D) \;=\; \underbrace{\kappa\, I_{\text{mem}} + h\,K}_{\text{holding memory}} \;+\; \underbrace{w\,\mathbb E[\ell_{\mathcal D}]}_{\text{being wrong or late}} \;+\; \underbrace{A(\mathcal D) + L(\mathcal D)}_{\text{acquiring and sharing information}} \qquad \text{subject to}\quad P(\text{ruin}) \le \varepsilon_r .
$$

- **𝒟**, the design: the number of layers K, what each tracks, how and how often it senses, and what it shares.
- **κ·I_mem + h·K**: the price κ per nat held per unit time, and the upkeep h of each layer, store or shared level.
- **w·E[ℓ]**: the stake times the expected loss at the moment of use. Lateness counts as error, because a prediction that arrives after the moment to act buys nothing.
- **A(𝒟)**: information-acquisition spend, priced per read (Σ c_k f_k, read price times read rate), per nat acquired (λ), or per unit of precision (c′·r^{−θ}).
- **L(𝒟)**: link, telling and lookup costs: κ_b per member–level link, d per member told, u per lookup at use rate μ. This is what puts pooling and sharing inside J.
- **Ruin**: a reserve that starts at buffer B, earns income y and pays J hits zero; ε_r is the tolerated chance.
- **The world** is exogenous and enters only through its measured pattern of change: its spectrum or Markov kernel, the sensing noise N and the lead k at which actions meet it. The drift level q and spectral slope α are the scale-free special case.
- **One currency per system** (heat, attention, money, compute). Physics sets floors on the prices (at least kT per nat acquired or erased by a measuring tracker, and kT·(I_mem − I_pred) for memory that does not predict: Landauer 1961, Still et al. 2012), not exchange rates between systems. In each system's own units the equation is the same problem at every scale: only dimensionless ratios matter, and the optimal cost shares are currency-free (CT24); the distance from the floor is one measured number per system.
- **Open lever, and where the world responds.** Effort g spent making the world drift less (homeostasis, buffers, contracts) belongs in the problem, but it makes the world depend on the design. Except for the coupled-party results of B.10 (CT29–CT32), the theorems of this file hold the outer world fixed at this g = 0. Modelling a responding world as one more party is available. A potential or reversibility theorem applies only when the beta-scaled game is an exact potential: the linear copy-gain model is one, and an arbitrary pair of costs need not be. That model has a confining potential only for a copy gain below one; a gain above one runs away, and a gain of one loses confinement (`theory/COADAPTATION.md` F3). Delayed push-back is a different statement and can destabilize strong selection (F4, gain below −1). Strongly monotone coupling can settle without being a potential game (CA5). Results with the action in the world's state, including the heat floor when the memory steers, are in `theory/ACTING_LOOP_2026-11-22.md` and `theory/FEEDBACK_FLOOR_2026-11-23.md` (T61). T61 and F3/F4 are related two-way structures and are not the same result. Drift-intensity reduction itself stays open (CL11).

The equation is assembled from known parts; the repository already wrote it as Axiom 5 of `reviews/night1/formal/AXIOMS.md`. It is the theory's statement of the problem, not a theorem. When that form is forced, and when it is not, is `theory/REPRESENTATION.md`: on a product of intervals the cost is additive up to a positive affine transformation, nonnegative prices need the further assumption that each concatenating coordinate is a bad, and a discrete layer count is not part of the cited Debreu theorem. Storing, predicting, acting and staying solvent do not force it. Whether the equation is a limit of growth selection is `theory/GROWTH_BRIDGE.md` (CLAIM_MAP section X). On a gapped finite menu, weak selection recovers the ranking only. The diffusion limit keeps \(\mu-\sigma^2/2\). An interior stake separates the argmaxes. The term list and the cap are inputs. Whether dissipation forces those terms is `theory/INFO_PHYSICS.md` (CLAIM_MAP section Y). It does not: a free-energy rate is bounded below by an information flow, the bound is not the cost, and the term list, the cap, and the linear prices stay assumptions. **Note (22 November 2026; not an edit of the display above).** Inside the class where the heat floor is proved, the physical cost is a different object: J_phys = Q̇ + w·E[ℓ] under the ruin cap, which stays an assumption: the heat of the memory's bath plus the stake on the loss (`theory/GENERAL_FORM_PHYSICS_2026-11-22.md`). The claim that the display above is the physical frontier is false (CLAIM_MAP IP5). Theorem R1 (`theory/REPRESENTATION.md`, axioms A1–A7) gives an additive priced sum on an assumed coordinate list; it does not choose these five terms, nonnegative prices need A8, and h·K needs an extra separability assumption. Reconciling the two objects in this section is open work T40 ([`README.md`, Waiting on Chad](README.md#waiting-on-chad)). Until a reviewed proposal is adopted, the display above stands as the definition.

**The law of adaptive organization (hypothesis): strongly selected systems minimize the cost of judgment.**

Here *minimize* means: sit within a preregistered band of the minimum of J, given the system's measured world. This is a bet, untested and not yet earned.

**Strongly selected (operational; every item preregistered before any measurement).** A substrate class qualifies for a test of the hypothesis only if all five hold, with the evidence recorded:
(a) *a cost-to-fitness map:* σ, the fractional change in the class's growth, survival or reproduction rate per fractional change in J (for cells, the measured growth-rate cost of the design variable; for firms, the elasticity of survival or profit margin to J, with its source); no map, no qualification;
(b) *a population proxy:* N_e, the number of independent lineages competing in the same world per redesign time (cells and microbes: measured; firms: independent firms in one market over one redesign time, with entry and exit); **cities, nation-states, global economies and the biosphere are excluded until a class-specific proxy is preregistered**, because for them N_e is not defined;
(c) *selection resolves the band:* ε = 10/(σ·N_e), the smallest fractional difference in J that selection can resolve (the drift barrier is s·N_e ≈ 1, with s = σ·ε, Lynch et al. 2016, doi 10.1038/nrg.2016.104; the factor of 10 above it is a conservative choice made here, not Lynch's); the predicted band around argmin J is at least ε wide whatever the measurement error, so a worse measurement cannot make qualification easier;
(d) *power:* the band, with the measurement error added, must exclude the design of at least one named rival (R1–R3, `measurement/FRAMEWORK.md` §9); a band that contains every rival's design can neither refute nor support anything;
(e) *a defined redesign time:* T_red is the time the class's design takes to close 1 − 1/e of the gap to its new argmin J after a step change in the prices or in the world's statistics, measured or preregistered from the class's turnover (where units adjust in lumps, on the class average, `theory/DYNAMICS.md` CL19 (d)); the lineage must have faced the same world for at least 10 T_red (N54 is the separate, longer record a redesign learns from).
A class that does not qualify is not evidence either way. Misses below the drift barrier are excluded by construction and cannot be invoked afterwards.

**Refutation conditions.** Fixed before the test, per substrate class (cells, firms, cities, …): (1) a closed list of J's cost terms, the world's drift spectrum and noise, and the prices, each measured independently of the design they predict, with the measurement protocol and error budget (`measurement/FRAMEWORK.md`, "Preregistered refutation conditions"); (2) the design's predicted optimum with its error band; (3) how the system's layers are counted; (4) the band's coverage and the number of systems (below). Then a **preregistered miss** in a qualifying system, meaning the layers (count, memories, read rates) fall outside the band around argmin J, counts toward refuting the hypothesis *for that substrate class*; **misses in two classes at different scales** refute it as a cross-scale hypothesis; a needed cost that is not on the preregistered list counts as a miss unless it was added publicly before the test; and **a design that fails to move with a measured change in its inputs, by the preregistered share, counts as a miss**, unless the post-change gap is inside the redesign band at the lower bound of the class's preregistered redesign cost (CT41, `theory/DYNAMICS.md` §2). A band exists only where adjustment is lumpy, and only if the class preregisters, before data, the redesign cost on the closed term list (point estimate and error budget, measured independently of the design), the curvature w, the input-process type (step or drift) and T or σ_d. A gap between the bands at the cost's lower and upper bounds is INDETERMINATE. If the band at the upper bound covers the predicted move, the move test is NOT A TEST. Without these preregistered quantities the band is zero and the rule is unchanged. Re-measuring the world is allowed once, before the comparison, never after it. To count as *support* the minimum must also beat a named rival (a fixed budget share; a universal exponent of ½). Until then, what is established is a principle (§4), theorems proved in-model (§5), and field results, which are first gated on qualification under the pre-written definition and then reported as passed or failed on proxy. A field claim with no recorded pre-test qualification check is not a test of the hypothesis (status: NOT A TEST, with the proxy result kept in the label). Where the world responds to the design, the input measured independently of the design is that response, under an intervention protocol fixed before the data. Holding it fixed instead requires CT34 (iii): cost F(x)+G(y)+εΦ(x,y), G μ-strongly convex, Φ L-Lipschitz in the team's design, regret at most (εL)²/(2μ), and that bound inside the preregistered error budget. CT34 (iii) is not a general small-gain theorem. The pre-data gate is unchanged.

*From a cost band to a design band.* The band lives in J but a miss is judged in design space, so each class preregisters the map: the band is {𝒟 : J(𝒟) ≤ (1 + ε + δ_meas)·J\*}, with ε the resolvable fractional difference in J (item (c)) and δ_meas the fractional error in J propagated from the input errors. J is flat at its minimum, J(𝒟) ≈ J\* + ½ δᵀHδ, so a fractional gap ε in J allows a displacement of about √(2εJ\*/H_ii) in each continuous design variable (log memory, log read rate): it grows as √ε, not ε. The class preregisters this band in design units, with H from its own J. A layer count is discrete: a count differs from the predicted one as a miss only if the counted design's J lies outside the band.

*Coverage and how many systems.* One chance miss must not refute, and the number of systems tested must not be choosable after seeing results. Each class therefore preregisters (i) the band's **coverage** c, the probability that the band contains the design the cost implies, given the measurement error (for example 99%); (ii) the **minimum number n_min of qualifying systems**, all of which are tested and reported, with a class below n_min excluded and not evidence either way; and (iii) a significance level α. The hypothesis is refuted for the class when the number of misses m is larger than chance allows at coverage c: P(Bin(n, 1 − c) ≥ m) < α. For a small class this can be one miss (c = 99.9% and n ≤ 10 give a chance probability of about 1% for a single miss); for a large class at coverage 99% it is several. The "two classes" rule for refutation across scales is unchanged.

*Counting layers in a real system (v2.5.4; method finalized in `measurement/LAYER_COUNT_RULE.md`, T15, draft; to be preregistered per class before any test).* Layers are counted by one operational rule fixed before measurement, not by judgment afterwards. (1) Fix per class the input/output pair and the band of time scales (table in `figures/face_placements.md` §4). (2) Measure the response kernel over that band by a fixed protocol, on isolated units and pooled. (3) Fit sums of exponentials (complex pairs allowed): Fit degrees 0 through K_max. Degree 0 is always eligible. Accept degree K>0 only if it lowers BIC by at least 10 against every lower degree, and select the largest such degree. A rhythm may enter as a jump of 2. Ignore poles outside the band. (4) Classify each pole *before* any merging: within each unit, match isolated-unit in-band poles to same-kind pooled in-band poles by a maximum-cardinality matching under the factor (for a complex pair, both the decay time and the period must lie within the factor; among those matchings, the smallest sum of log-ratios, with remaining ties broken by the sorted pooled decay times, then by the sorted periods (a real pole has no period and sorts before any positive period)); a pooled pole is local if it matches in every unit (or, fixed in advance, in the per-unit response to unit-specific drives), and shared otherwise; then merge only within a class, and only among poles that the unit records matched to the same partners, poles less than a factor 3 apart. A local and a shared pole are never merged, so "both" is observable. (5) Count poles by degree: a real pole counts 1 and a complex pair counts 2 (the exponential-sum fitter has no pole-at-0 integrator basis). (6) Relay-only test: bypass the candidate level or replace it with a pure delay of the same latency; it is a layer only if a pole disappears or moves by at least the resolution factor (ratio ≥ factor), otherwise it relays (CT16). (7) Assign a pole to a dot if its time constant lies in the dot's τ range widened ×3 (nearest range centre if several); a pole matching no dot is a miss. Only degree-1 components are scored, because B.11 covers one store per design. (8) The observed design passes if it has ≥ 50% of the dot's input draws, misses if it has < 10%, and is inconclusive in between. (9) The prediction is `figures/face_systems.py` as frozen on 20 Nov 2026 (SHA-256 in `figures/face_placements.md` §4, checked by the tests); a later change voids the comparison for that class. Count only what the rule returns; counting is never redone after a miss. A management level counts only if it holds a state with its own measured time constant. The prediction side, in the same note, is: K* is always argmin J, which equals the lower-convex-hull increment test (`core/KEYSTONE_NOTE.md` §4); on a finite domain, the from-0 count K*_0(R) = #{K ≥ 1 : R d_K > 1} equals the leftmost argmin for all R ≥ 0 if the decrements of ε_K are non-increasing, while the converse holds under positive, pairwise distinct decrements (distinctness with the threshold decrement positive is enough; see theory/LAYER_CONVEXITY.md; d = (−2, −1) is a counterexample to a bare iff; Fox 1966; PR #145, N64), and is not used when convexity fails, as it can when S/N has a peak. A pair-count shortcut is not a substitute. A raw count difference from K* is a miss only if the observed design's J lies outside the band, as above. No system has yet been counted by this rule (E. coli's published two-pole kernel is degree 2 and was not selected by it).

## 4. What kind of theory this is

The theory has three parts. A **definition**: the cost J. A **principle**: strongly selected, persistent adaptive systems sit near the design that minimizes J, to within the tolerance ε their selection can resolve. Under the selection note's A1 (persistence is costly) and A2-L, which are not Appendix B's A1–A5, together with weak mutation and SA1–SA1b (`theory/SELECTION_AXIOMS.md`), the derived in-model result is that note's excess formula. The excess is small only when the note's \(\sigma N_e\) is large compared with the number of soft coordinates. The empirical bet is the note's §10: qualifying systems meet those assumptions with parameters that make the remainder small. SA-P1 tests that excess. It is not locked. The principle has the form of a variational principle, as least action does, with one difference that matters: least action has no free prices and J does, so the principle says something only through structure that holds for every price (§5 (ii)–(iii)) and through prices measured independently of the design they predict. Whether real systems obey it is untested, not refuted. If strongly selected systems across scales are found near the cost's minimum, that cross-system regularity would earn the hypothesis of §3 its name in the scientific sense; until then what is established is a principle, not a law. **Theorems**: what minimizing J implies, proved in stated classes (§5).

The world's pattern of change is a **measured input**, not part of the theory, so there are two kinds of wrong. Where the world responds, the input is that response, measured the same way, or CT34 (iii) shows it is weak enough to hold fixed under the assumptions in §3 and §6. **A wrong picture of the world** means re-measuring and getting a new design; it costs little, because regret is second order in the picture error (CT23: misjudging drift 2× costs about 4–6%), except near cliffs such as the viability edge or the lookup cliff, where a small error can flip survival. **A wrong or missing cost breaks the theory**: no regret bound covers a cost J cannot book.

## 5. The theorem

**Master Theorem** (proved in-model in the linear-Gaussian class 𝓜_LG, Appendix B.9: exogenous Gaussian world, stationary or with stationary increments, sensed through white noise or read exactly; layers are first-order stores with rates in a compact set; squared error at lead k; ruin imposed through the Lundberg bound. Parts (ii) and (iii) were restated in v2.4 after the independent review of v2.3 found them too wide; the restatements are not yet reviewed.)

- **(i) Existence** (CT20). If any design survives, a best design exists, and its layer count is at most J(𝒟₁)/h for any surviving one-layer design 𝒟₁.
- **(ii) Monotone comparative statics** (CT20, CT33), **with the ruin constraint slack at the stakes compared (B ≥ B_slack) or absent**. *With sensing fixed* (reads not chosen), the best layer count never falls as stakes rise relative to upkeep (w/h), in any world and for any loss, and a single layer's best read rate never falls as w/c rises. *With read rates chosen*, the layer count **can** fall as stakes rise, because reads substitute for layers (a counterexample is in B.9, and the many-scale map's federation boundary is another case); what stays true in every design class, ruin slack, is that **total judgment spend** (upkeep plus sensing, J\* − w·E\*) never falls and the error never rises as w rises at fixed prices (CT33). **When the ruin constraint binds (ii) fails:** the feasible set then depends on the stake (the reserve pays J, and the variance of realized loss scales with w²), so a costlier, lower-error design can become uncertified as w rises and spend falls while error rises (a numerical case is in `scope_checks.py` §1b). Scoped this way (ii) is a robust, price-free prediction: with survival not at issue, more at stake means no less is spent on judgment.
- **(iii) The world caps the layers, counted by degree** (CT21). The best layer count is at most the number of dimensions of the world's optimal predictor (its McMillan degree), where a layer is one state. A world with one real time scale (an OU mode, a random walk) needs one layer, **including the self-similar random walk** (L1): K\* ∈ {0, 1}, never more (K\* = 0, acting blind, can win when stakes are small). A world with J separated real time scales needs at most J, exactly J (at zero lead) once R = w·E_∞/h is large enough, and layers beat one flat tracker iff R > R\* = min_K (K−1)/(Δ₁ − Δ_K), with Δ_K the relative excess error of K layers (L2). At the full optimum (K equal to the world's degree) every layer is faster than its mode (the filter's poles interlace the world's; proved only there, not for fewer layers than modes). This assumes exogenous noise and speed-free upkeep: when a store's own noise dominates, with exponent s, the best one-store memory is λτ\* = (1 − s)/s at low signal-to-noise and shorter at any positive one, so it is faster than the mode when s > 1/2 (at s = 1/2, λτ* = 1 at low signal-to-noise, equal, not faster) and slower at low signal-to-noise when s < 1/2, and any price on speed lengthens it (Model S+, `theory/STORE_NOISE.md` E1-a, E2; prior art in its §6). **A rhythm is a two-state mode:** a lightly damped oscillation needs two layers by degree, and real first-order stores approximate it only with a count that keeps growing as stakes rise (excess error at least 68%, 45%, 30% for 1, 2, 3 stores in the worked case, best of many optimizer starts). A continuum of time scales has no exact finite design (L3).
- **(iv) The price of survival, on the scalar diffusion path of Lemma B9 (CT22).** Below a minimum buffer B_min the Lundberg bound certifies no design. That is a sufficient bound, not a proof that every uncertified design ruins. Above B_slack the ruin constraint is slack and the expected-cost rules are exactly optimal. In between, on that path, it binds with a multiplier μ_s > 0, the price of survival, which moves the check interval from the expected-cost optimum (μ_s = 0) toward Theorem L's survival-optimal interval (the μ_s → ∞ end). The movement is not claimed for a general design set. In the diffusive-reset class the constraint is the temperature: per-window ruin ≤ ε_r means z = B/(σ√τ) ≥ z_ε, so the buffer needed grows as shock × √(time to fix). The square root is the Brownian case: where the error since the last fix is self-similar with exponent H (fractional Brownian motion; H = 1/α_s for a stable-law world of index α_s), the group is z = B/(σ·τ^H), the buffer needed is z_ε·σ·τ^H, and the largest survivable shock falls as τ^{−H}, so faster correction raises the ceiling (CT25, proved in a model: exact by self-similarity, checked on simulated fBm; first passage is Black & Cox 1976, not Merton's terminal default). The spread's time growth is all this shares with Drăgulescu & Yakovenko's (2000) money-gas "temperature": an analogy only. That stress *strengthens* a system is not implied; it needs an adaptation rule and stops at B_min (K7, open).
- **(v) Special cases.** Each named result is J under a stated restriction, and `theory/meq_checks/meq_derive.py` checks each derivation:

**Theorem L, stated correctly.** Theorem L maximizes the survival exponent. Its best interval does not depend on the buffer, and it reduces to the expected-cost rule when the reserve's own noise dominates, not when the buffer is large. The large-buffer statement is (iv).

**Scale-free cost shares (CT24, proved).** For any design problem whose cost is a price-weighted sum of price-free terms **and whose feasible set does not depend on the prices** (no ruin constraint, or one that is slack), J\* is homogeneous of degree 1 in the prices, so by the envelope and Euler theorems (the duality of Hotelling 1932 and Shephard's lemma) each term's share of the optimal cost equals the elasticity of J\* in its price, and the shares sum to 1. **A binding ruin constraint breaks this**: its feasible set depends on the prices (the reserve pays J) and on income and buffer, which are not prices of J's terms, so Euler runs over all of them and elasticities exceed shares (in the worked case 0.584 and 0.563, summing to 1.147, against shares 0.528 and 0.472; slack, all 0.500). A share is currency-free and measured twice, as a spend ratio and as a price response, an over-identified test. For one tracker (CT12) with exact (noise-free) reads of a self-similar (power-law) world, the sensing share is (α−1)/α and the share lost to being wrong is 1/α: **looking costs α − 1 for every 1 lost to being wrong, in any currency, at any scale** (α = 2 is the classical equal costs of the order-quantity rule, Harris 1913). It does not hold with read noise (for a random walk read through noise the ratio falls from 1 to 0.89, 0.62, 0.50 as the noise grows) or for a world with a time scale of its own (one OU mode: 0.99 down to 0.58 as c/w rises), so any test must first measure that read noise is negligible. Independent trackers each obey it with their own α; for coupled layers the split is not derived (conjecture). It is a cross-scale prediction (N57, open) for trackers with negligible read noise: α from the measured drift spectrum, the ratio from spend and measured loss, the elasticity from an exogenous price shift (a cell's cost of sensing, a firm's cost of monitoring); three readings of one number must agree. It needs the cost of error in the same currency, so it inherits the measurement gap G3. The same homogeneity makes w, h and c a system's shadow prices when it allocates a binding budget: the multiplier of a read budget is 1/w, and a K-way allocation gives K − 1 testable equalities of marginal value (KKT, convex single-tracker class); that observed allocations satisfy these K − 1 equalities is the testable statement (CX5; the identity itself, that multipliers are shadow prices, is a definition, so the content is that real systems allocate optimally). With the floor priced at β·kT per nat, J\* rises by β^{(α−1)/α} and the shares do not move: the gap to the floor changes the size of a system's cost, not the shape of its trade-off.

## 6. The spine

1. Judgment has one total cost, holding memory plus being wrong or late plus acquiring and sharing information, with the risk of ruin as a constraint, and physics puts a price floor under each part at every scale.
2. The theory is that cost and the design that minimizes it; how the world changes is an input, measured on each system, never assumed. Where the world responds to the design, that response is the input, measured under an intervention protocol fixed before the data, not the observed marginal drift alone. Holding the response fixed is allowed only when CT34 (iii) applies and its bound sits inside the preregistered error budget: the cost splits as F(x)+G(y)+εΦ(x,y), G is μ-strongly convex, and Φ is L-Lipschitz in the team's design, so the regret is at most (εL)²/(2μ). That bound is not a general small-gain theorem. The pre-data gate and the independent-measurement gate are unchanged.
3. A world that changes on one time scale is tracked best by one layer (or none, when the stakes are small), even when that change is a self-similar random walk.
4. A world with several separated time scales is tracked best by one layer per scale once the stakes outweigh a layer's upkeep (R > R\*), each layer's memory set by its scale and the sensing noise and, when a store's own noise is small next to the sensing noise, faster than the scale it tracks (a store whose own noise dominates can be slower, as at s < 1/2 and low signal-to-noise; Model S+, §1); with sensing fixed the count keeps rising with stakes over upkeep, and with a continuum of scales it has no finite end; with reads chosen it is total judgment spend that rises.
5. The architecture inherits the world's scale structure: where change is scale-free the layers come out roughly log-spaced (exactly geometric for shared clocks, CL12), and where it has rhythms (days, quarters, seasons) there is one two-state (resonant) layer per rhythm, counted by degree, in the linear-Gaussian class (deterministic seasonality is not a stationary Gaussian mode at all).
6. Across members the same cost decides when to centralize: a shared level forms once enough members face a common driver and telling is cheap, cheap lookup dissolves levels that only hold facts, lateness caps a level's reach so that beyond L_max a system federates, and a part that bears only some of the whole's loss under-watches, so expect someone in charge (below).
7. A wrong picture of the world costs second-order regret and is fixed by re-measuring before the test, but the hypothesis is refuted, for a substrate class, at the preregistered number of misses in strongly selected systems (for a small class, one), with inputs measured independently of the design, or if some level needs a cost not on that class's preregistered list.

**When to centralize: the equation decides.** Whether a system is run from the middle or from its parts is an output of J, not an assumption: one comparison, a shared tracker's cost (upkeep, telling, lateness) against each member tracking alone. These results assume the parts cooperate; whether self-interested parts reach them is CX2 (§9).
- *Pooling.* A shared level beats separate trackers once N > N\*(ρ) members face a common driver (CT14), and telling pays iff d/c < 1/N\* − 1/N (CT16).
- *Dissolving.* Cheap lookup removes levels that only hold facts (CT11).
- *Reach.* A central tracker holds a tolerable error only within L_max, beyond which a system federates unless a shortcut raises v (CT19).
- *Authority.* A part that bears only Λ_i of the whole's loss reads at (Λ_i/Λ_whole)^{1/α} of the right rate, so the part under-reads (CT17); that an enforcer or shared fate follows, unless shared upkeep is privately rewarded, is an interpretation, not part of what CT17 proves. Pooling, dissolving, reach and authority are proved in a model.
- *Coupling.* Federation pays iff Σᵢ Jᵢ\* + J_coupling < J\* of one flat tracker: that is a definition of the comparison, not a theorem (CX3). It becomes a conjecture only once J_coupling's functional form is specified and the boundary it predicts is stated (open). The question has a direct literature: Coase (1937) and Williamson on when to organize; team theory (Marschak & Radner 1972); hierarchies and polyarchies (Sah & Stiglitz); the organization of knowledge (Garicano 2000).
- *Join, stay and leave.* A part keeps following a whole iff λ·s·c < w·Φ, CT9 with the part's net share s·c/w of the price of following; CT29's alignment clause makes its choice the whole's iff s = w/W; joining is CT14 and re-forming is the CL19 band. The canonical statement of this swarm rule, with what is proved and what is interpretation, is `theory/SWARM_RULE_2026-11-23.md`.

**Ants and the trail (CT30; proved in a model).** Ants are the decentralized limit: where every signal is cheap and local, a trail deposited as a by-product of use means no central tracker pays. For a random walk seen through noise with quadratic loss, the steady-state optimal tracker is exponential smoothing (Muth 1960; known), x ← (1−λ)x + λ·signal: *reinforce by use* (deposit λ) plus *evaporate at rate λ*, with λ\* = (−ρ + √(ρ² + 4ρ))/2 for ρ = q/r, about √(q/r) per unit time (evaporation is set by drift over noise; with precision priced, by q, w, c′ and θ together, CT15's form). When trails drift independently, local updates equal the centralized Kalman filter exactly (checked to 10⁻¹⁴); a shared driver is what a pooled level (CT14) buys. It fails when use is endogenous: if choice follows the trail, the trail measures choice share × relevance and can lock onto the worse path (19 of 20 runs). **Proved in a model:** the update rule is optimal and decentralizable. **Still a hypothesis:** that real ants and synapses implement it because selection favored it. Dorigo, Maniezzo & Colorni (1996) build the same update into ant-colony optimization, Deneubourg et al. (1990) model Argentine-ant recruitment, and the Lasius niger trail lifetime (about 47 minutes, Beckers, Deneubourg & Goss 1993) has never been compared with q/r. The one nonlinear ant-like readout simulated failed (Lemma B7).

**Two axes, one rule.** Stores lie across time scales and levels across members, from one comparison of a layer's saving with its upkeep. Levels copy the world's shared-drift structure, one per shared driver whose pooled stakes pay (CL13, CL14), as stores copy its time scales. In a nested tree, levels fill from the broadest scope down iff shared drift falls slowly enough with scope (χ < χ\* = (1−a)/(1−b), formal Theorem 7; CL15). That the architecture inherits the world's scale structure is T7 (CL16).

**Pooling and nesting (CT28, CT34; proved in a model, with conditions).** *(1) Homogeneity under pooling* (upkeep-free power class): J\*(nW) = n^{1/α}·J\*(W), so pooling n members costs n^{a−1} of n separate trackers (a = 1/α), Eppen's (1979) square-root rule at α = 2; it does **not** survive the term that creates layers, since with hK in J, J\*(W) = min_K (hK + W·E_K) is not homogeneous in W. *(2) Exact nesting* (CT34): the team's optimized cost J\*_team enters the parent's problem exactly as upkeep h **if the parent sees the team only through quantities fixed before the team optimizes** (and, generically, only if) (stake passed down, noise, drift); if what is passed down depends on the parent's design, J\*_team is a value function of it, still exact, no longer a constant (partial minimization, the principle of hierarchical decomposition: Dantzig & Wolfe 1960; Geoffrion 1970). Under weak coupling ε, freezing the team at its stand-alone optimum costs at most (εL)²/(2μ), second order in ε (checked, slope 2.1); a shared driver is not weak coupling, and the independent-team accounting then misses the pooling gain (CT14), about ρ² at first. *Conjecture, not shown:* that real teams coarse-grain to a single member whose upkeep is the team's J\*; CT34 gives conditions and a bound, not that real teams meet them. Time-scale separation is the usual heuristic for weak coupling: it makes the fast variable quasi-static, which does not by itself make the coupling coefficient ε small, so the bound applies only once ε is estimated (Borkar 1997, cited not proved; near-decomposability, Simon & Ando 1961); it breaks for heavy-tailed or non-additive costs (S4, Lemma B12) and where levels shape one another's worlds (CX2). Redesign is the slowest layer inside J (N54). Decoupled layers are nearly optimal only when scales separate (`theory/THEOREMS.md` T3c). Loosely coupled parts giving fast dynamics inside and slow between is Simon's (1962); higher brain levels tracking slower causes is Kiebel, Daunizeau & Friston's (2008).

**Judgment spend (CT33; proved, no model class needed).** With J = S(𝒟) + w·E(𝒟), S the spend on judgment and E the error per unit stake, adding the optimality inequalities for w₁ < w₂ gives (w₂ − w₁)(E₂ − E₁) ≤ 0 and S₂ − S₁ ≥ w₁(E₁ − E₂) ≥ 0: **error never rises and spend never falls as stakes rise**, J\* concave with slope E\*. It holds for any design set, loss and world, with reads chosen, **provided the ruin constraint is slack at both stakes compared or absent** (when ruin binds, spend can fall: `scope_checks.py` §1b). It does not say the layer count rises (reads substitute for layers); nor does it hold when only the upkeep h falls at a fixed stake. This is what survives of (ii) with sensing optimized.

**Sizes.** On the time axis a layer buys modest gains: 0.2–5% of the error cost in scale-free bands, about 20% at most in the scans, and at most 29% for one extra clock. The large gains come from pooling (about N reads saved for one), the lookup cliff and ruin margins.

## 7. Uses: design and diagnosis

**Design.** Measure the world's pattern of change, price the reads, upkeep and stakes, and solve J for the number of layers, their memories, the read rates and what to share. For monitors, caches, sensor fleets, inventory and forecasting this is a calculator today (`theory/MASTER_EQUATION.md` §2). The seven design rules of the 12 November post (archived in [`history/`](https://github.com/Chad-Mitchell/theory-of-adaptive-organization/tree/1eed183f3888e8e3eead5956bbf1e8cb4233510a/history)) came first, as requirements; pushing on them led to the master equation, and now they are its corollaries, so the derivation runs from J to the rules. Their status follows; an earlier sentence called all seven solutions of J, which was too strong for rules 1 and 6, and rules 3 and 4 are known or near-tautological rather than new theorems.

**Four results, not seven.** Rules 2 and 4 are one decision, what to hold (keep while the value rate beats upkeep: CT4, CT9, CT11, CT26); rules 3 and 5 are one estimator (a belief moved by reliability and acted on from the current model: CT21, CT8, CT19); rules 1 and 7 are one read rate (watch as drift × stakes rises, early when stake × chance beats the cost: CT10, CT12, CT19); and rule 6 with rule 1's teaching term is one information purchase and update (CT27, CT30). The minimal set is hold, estimate, watch, learn: each derived from J in a model, with rules 1 and 6 only partly (the teaching value is model-specific, the update rule is derived in CT30) and rules 3 and 4 known or near-tautological; that real systems learn this way is the hypothesis.

**Diagnosis.** The same equation reads an existing organization (a firm, a team, a city, a cell) against its costs:
- which layers the costs say should exist, given the time scales its world changes on;
- layers that do not pay their upkeep;
- layers that cheap lookup should have dissolved;
- missing layers for time scales the stakes can afford;
- reads that are mistimed for the drift and stakes they face, and parts that under-watch because they bear little of the whole's loss.

Much of this works from signs and ratios, without exact stakes (§5 (ii) holds in every world; only price ratios are identified). Prices can also be inferred from an observed design (revealed costs), a test and not a circle only when observed features outnumber the free price ratios: several exogenous drift levels, several items under one clock, or one technology across layers.

**Applicability is general; the bottleneck is measurement.** Firms, teams and cities fall under the theory as machines do; what differs is how hard stakes and upkeep are to measure (scope: §9).

## 8. Predictions, and refutation conditions

**The refutation test** (preregistrable; the operational definition of *strongly selected* and the refutation conditions are in §3, the protocol in `measurement/FRAMEWORK.md` §9): in a qualifying system, measure the world's drift spectrum and the sensing noise and the prices, independently of the design, then compare the system's layers (count, memories, read rates) with argmin J. Misses outside the preregistered band refute the hypothesis for that substrate class once they exceed the allowance set by the preregistered coverage and minimum number of systems (§3; for a small class, one miss), misses in two classes at different scales refute it across scales, a needed cost not on the preregistered list counts as a miss, and a design that fails to move with a measured change in its inputs by the preregistered share counts as a miss unless the post-change gap is inside the redesign band at the lower bound of the class's preregistered redesign cost (CT41, `theory/DYNAMICS.md` §2). A band exists only where adjustment is lumpy, and only if the class preregisters, before data, the redesign cost on the closed term list (point estimate and error budget, measured independently of the design), the curvature w, the input-process type (step or drift) and T or σ_d. A gap between the bands at the cost's lower and upper bounds is INDETERMINATE. If the band at the upper bound covers the predicted move, the move test is NOT A TEST. Without these preregistered quantities the band is zero and the rule is unchanged. Support needs a named rival to lose (a fixed budget share; a universal exponent of ½). Re-measuring is allowed once, before the comparison (CLAIM_MAP N13, J6, J16, N56).

## 9. Scope and caveats

- **When to compete, collaborate, or organize (CX2): classified, because no general theorem is true.** One story in two halves. The proved half says *when organizing pays if the parts cooperate* (one J governs the group): a shared level pays above N\*(ρ) and pooling n members costs n^{a−1} of n separate trackers (Result 4, CT14–CT16), the layer count K\* and threshold R\* (L2, CT21), and the reach L_max beyond which a system federates (LAT-1, CT19). The other half asks *whether self-interested parts reach it*. Parts that each minimize their own J_i while shaping one another's worlds compete (Nash); one J for the group is an organization; partial sharing is collaboration. Organizing is worth Σᵢ Jᵢ(Nash) − J\*(joint), the price-of-anarchy gap (Koutsoupias & Papadimitriou 1999; Roughgarden & Tardos 2002), and parts merge iff it exceeds J_coupling (links, telling, a shared layer): the federation inequality of CX3 read in reverse. Each merger creates the next level, so levels are outputs of the cost, as in the major evolutionary transitions (Maynard Smith & Szathmáry 1995; a conjecture). The answer by class (CT29, CT31, CT32, proved in models): **(a) potential coupling converges.** Symmetric shared-driver coupling is a weighted exact potential game (Monderer & Shapley 1996) with weights wᵢ; Nash equilibria are the minimizers of its potential, sequential best response and small-step adaptation converge, the price of anarchy is ½(√(a/W) + √(W/a)) ≤ (N+1)/(2√N), tight at equal stakes and growing as √N/2, and stake-proportional cost shares make every party's cost proportional to the joint cost, so Nash is the joint optimum (public-good pricing, Samuelson 1954; the incentive problem of revealing stakes, Groves 1973, is open here). **(b) General convergence is false.** With opposed coupling (pursuit and evasion, J₁ = x₁x₂, J₂ = −x₁x₂) continuous-time adaptation orbits forever and discrete simultaneous steps spiral outward, and fictitious play in a Shapley (1964)-type game cycles, so no theorem covers arbitrary coupling. **(c) Time-scale separation is not shown to restore convergence:** in a linear two-party model it makes gradient play settle iff the slow party's speed ε is below b/m, but at a point that is not a Nash equilibrium (the slow party's cost is concave), and with convex own costs the speed ratio never changes stability (CT32 (iii)); whether separation helps inside J's class is open (Borkar 1997 proves convergence to the slow system's attractors, not to Nash). **Still open:** which real couplings are potential games, how stakes are revealed, and non-aggregative or stochastic coupling. Every other proof holds the outer level fixed (g = 0); a world that responds is one more party we chose not to model as a player (F3 below, whose g is the world's copy gain, not the effort of §3), and drift reduction g is one case (CL11). Under selection (the selection note's A1, A2-L, weak mutation on connected party move graphs with equal clocks), the joint substitution chain is reversible (and then uniquely Boltzmann, π ∝ e^{−Ψ}) iff the β-scaled game is an exact potential game (CA1); when a confining quadratic potential exists (integrable e^{−Ψ}, violated when unbounded below as in F3 at g > 1), per-party expected regret is d_i/(2β_i), which is small relative to operating cost only when σN_e ≫ d, while strongly monotone non-potential games are stable at all speeds with excess controlled by the radial part of a Hodge split (CA2, CA5) and strong-selection multi-basin trapping selects by resistance trees (Young 1993) rather than lowest cost (F6); which real couplings are β-potential games stays open, and CX2 stays OPEN: `theory/COADAPTATION.md` (CLAIM_MAP U1–U12; reviewed, `reviews/coadaptation_2026-11-21/REVIEW.md`, CONFIRM2 READY at `93add78`, merged as `b16193d`).
- **The layer results (L1–L3, CT21) are linear-Gaussian, squared-error, and counted by degree** (a rhythm is two layers; deterministic seasonality is not covered). The count rises with stakes only with sensing fixed (§5 (ii)). Nothing is proved for jump worlds, Bayes trackers or other losses. Whether layers must be separate *agents* or can be stores inside one agent is open.
- **Robustness, redundancy and antifragility (`theory/ROBUSTNESS.md`; CT37–CT40, CL18, CX7, CX8; independent review 30 READY-WITH-FIXES, fixes a self-check; mostly known in substance, placed inside J).** Where the ruin constraint binds, the cost-minimizing design is certified with zero margin, and no risk outside the model is covered (CT39). Put a risk into the model and J makes its own defences: copies of a layer under component failure, and more copies, never fewer, when ruin binds (CT37); more reserve and faster checking than the fitted-model optimum when the world's parameters are uncertain (CT38). Identical copies cannot beat a common-mode failure, and a shared uncertain failure rate needs more copies than its plug-in (CL18, prior art). Copies and buffers add no layers at new speeds; the layer count under binding ruin is open (CX7). In a fixed world more volatility never lowers J\*, but with redesign per regime the cost is concave in volatility, which is antifragility to its variation in Taleb & Douady's sense (CT40); stress strengthening stays open (K7). A preregistered, untested prediction: selected systems hold reserves above the plug-in minimum, rising with their record's uncertainty (CX8).
- **Dynamics: redesign cost, lineages, time averages (`theory/DYNAMICS.md`; CT41–CT43, CL19–CL21, CX9, CX10; one independent review, READY-WITH-FIXES, fixes a self-check).** The master equation is unchanged; three extensions a class may preregister. A lump-sum redesign cost makes not moving optimal inside a band ((6kσ²/w)^{1/4} for a drifting optimum, √(k/(wT)) for a step; Dixit 1991), so a non-move is excused only when the post-change gap is inside the band at the redesign cost's preregistered lower bound, a gap between the lower- and upper-bound bands is indeterminate (CT41), and K should show hysteresis (CX9, open). Where shocks are shared within a generation, selection on lineage growth favours a mixture of designs (bet-hedging, Cohen 1966), which no per-unit argmin J gives; with independent shocks the two agree (CT42); any E. coli test must name its objective first. For additive stakes, or stakes on the log of the reserve, J is already the time average; for lumpy losses on returns it is not, and a ruin threshold fixes the sign of growth but not the ranking (CT43).
- **One currency per system.** "Every scale" means the same problem solved with each system's own measured inputs and prices, not one exchange rate. Physics sets only price floors (kT per nat; a synapse pays 10⁵–10⁶ times the floor, Laughlin et al. 1998). Whether the second law does more than that above the cell is open; Chad's position is that the physics is not decorative.
- **Further limits.** Not covered: regime shifts (N16), goals beyond tracking (exploration, play), indivisible reads (formal Prop 9), and costs that do not add (S4, Lemma B12). **Conjecture, not proved:** beyond the linear-Gaussian class the layers are the components of the world's predictive state worth their upkeep, capped by the dimension of its predictive-state space, the number of causal states (Crutchfield & Young 1989), with predictive information (Bialek, Nemenman & Tishby 2001) bounding what they can carry; a two-state switching world is the first test (CX4). Federation with a coupling cost is CX3 (§6).
- **"Should be built", not "are built",** until a same-system test exists. That selection holds designs near the optimum is the prior-art assumption of Alchian (1950). The in-model excess is the selection note's formula (§4); the field claim is untested. Below the drift barrier misses are expected (Lynch et al. 2016).
- **Ruin is two different things.** The master equation's constraint is lifetime ruin of a reserve with income (the Lundberg bound); the temperature (CT25) is a per-fixing-window chance constraint, and over a horizon T_h the buffer needed is B ≥ σ·τ^H·z(ε·τ/T_h) (survivable-shock elasticity −0.46, not −0.5, in the checked case). The qualitative ceiling survives.
- **Theorem P** (when judgment pays) is proved for stationary worlds; most results use drifting ones (CX1, open).
- **No valid measurement of a tracked world's drift spectrum exists yet** (GAPS G1). The NYC 311 data are scale-free across complaint categories, after post hoc dequantization; in time the hourly series breaks near 22 hours. The 10-year Treasury yield (DGS10) is a random walk on proxy (α = 2.00 ± 0.03), so it needs one layer.
- **Change is not "fractal, period".** The theory does not say physical time is fractal; that framing is withdrawn (N46). What it says is about inputs: whether the world a system tracks changes self-similarly over a band is measured, and if it is, the layers come out roughly log-spaced. The literature shows broad scale-free bands broken by cycles and cadences, a mixed spectrum, which is what multi-layer design needs.
- **Where it stands.** No field test has yet measured the theory's quantities validly, so the bet is untested, not refuted: 30 field rows are NOT A TEST (10 passed on proxy and 20 failed on proxy); none is support or refutation. - **Ends of the scale.** Atoms are frozen because nothing they do changes whether they last (E1); stars regulate themselves and anticipate nothing outside; the cosmic horizon caps memory. The theory does not derive black holes, does not claim a torus universe, dark matter or energy, or that wormholes exist (`theory/REACH.md`).

## Appendix A. The model and notation

### A.1 The model

Every result in Appendix B is a theorem about this model (the Master Theorem's class 𝓜_LG, B.9, narrows it), and each states which parts it uses. CT29–CT32 (B.10) relax the World item below: parties shape one another's costs.

- **World.** A state X_t that the agent does not change (exogenous). Two classes appear, and each result names its class. **(W-a)** stationary, ergodic Markov: the world fluctuates around fixed statistics. **(W-b)** drifting: Gaussian with stationary increments, E(X_{t+τ} − X_t)² = q·τ^{α−1}, with **spectral slope** α = 1 + 2H between 1 and 3 (a random walk is α = 2) and **drift level** q.
- **Agent.** A memory M_t updated from observations, and actions A_t = π(M_t) committed a lead time k before they meet the world.
- **Persistence.** A reserve starts at a buffer B, earns income y and pays every cost; ruin is the first time it reaches zero.
- **Selection.** Under the selection note's A1 (persistence is costly) and A2-L, which are not Appendix B's A1–A5, together with weak mutation and SA1–SA1b, the derived in-model result is that note's excess formula (`theory/SELECTION_AXIOMS.md`), not a primitive assumption (§4). The excess is small only when the note's \(\sigma N_e\) is large compared with the number of soft coordinates. The empirical bet is the note's §10: qualifying systems meet those assumptions with parameters that make the remainder small. SA-P1 tests that excess. It is not locked.
- **Φ**, the **lifetime value of a fresh reading**: how much one exact reading lowers expected loss, summed over the time until the world has drifted away from it.
- **Regime first.** Every number names its world class, its α and its premises. A result proved for α = 2 is not assumed at other α.

### B.1 When judgment pays

**CT1 (P0, the persistence bound).** *World W-a; A1, A2, A5.* Let D(R) be the distortion-rate function of the stationary marginal of X under ℓ, D(R) = min{E ℓ(A, X) : I(A; X) ≤ R}, and I_pred = lim (1/T) Σ_t I(M_t; X_{t+k}). Then every agent's long-run mean loss is at least D(I_pred). If y − c̄ < D(I_pred), ruin is certain.

*Proof.* A_t is a function of M_t, so I(A_t; X_{t+k}) ≤ I(M_t; X_{t+k}) (data processing). By the definition of D and because D is nonincreasing, E ℓ(A_t, X_{t+k}) ≥ D(I(A_t; X_{t+k})) ≥ D(I(M_t; X_{t+k})). D is convex, so averaging over t and applying Jensen gives mean loss ≥ D(I_pred). The reserve increments are stationary and ergodic with mean at most y − c̄ − D(I_pred) < 0, so R_t → −∞ almost surely (ergodic theorem): ruin is certain. ∎

*At equality* (mean margin exactly 0), ruin is still certain if the increments are nondegenerate (positive asymptotic variance, as for i.i.d. or mixing increments), by recurrence. A bounded coboundary is the exception. *Derivation:* `theory/GENERAL_THEOREM.md` §2; checks `theory/theorem-v2-checks/c6_persistence_bound.py`. Ingredients known (Shannon's rate–distortion; the ergodic theorem).

**CT2 (P1, when a model is necessary).** *As CT1.* Call a policy π₀ blind if I(M_t; X_{t+k}) = 0. Blind action is viable (survival with positive probability for a large enough B) iff some blind policy has D(0) < y − c̄(π₀). A model is necessary iff every blind policy has D(0) ≥ y − c̄(π₀), with nondegenerate increments at equality.

*Proof.* A blind agent's action is independent of X_{t+k}, so its mean loss is at least min_a E ℓ(a, X) = D(0). If D(0) ≥ y − c̄(π₀) for every blind π₀, CT1 (with I_pred = 0) makes ruin certain for every blind agent, so a persistent agent must have I_pred > 0. Conversely, if a blind policy using the minimizing action has margin m = y − c̄(π₀) − D(0) > 0, then R_t − B → +∞ almost surely, so inf_t (R_t − B) is finite almost surely and P(inf_t R_t > 0) → 1 as B → ∞. ∎

The inequality is strict and c̄ depends on the policy, since a blind agent pays no memory or read cost (math-attack review G3, applied 19 Nov 2026). *Counterexample* to "every self-maintaining system pays for a model": a bet-hedging lineage in an i.i.d. world grows at ln 1.5 per generation with zero predictive information (Kussell & Leibler 2005; `c7d`).

**CT3 (P2, the lead-time cap).** *As CT1, X Markov.* I(M_t; X_{t+k}) ≤ I(X_t; X_{t+k}), and the right side is nonincreasing in k. So persistence requires k ≤ k_max, the largest k with I(X_t; X_{t+k}) > R(y − c̄).

*Proof.* M_t depends on the world only through X_s, s ≤ t, and X is an exogenous Markov chain, so M_t → X_t → X_{t+k} is a Markov chain and data processing gives the first inequality. X_t → X_{t+k} → X_{t+k+1} is Markov, so I(X_t; X_{t+k+1}) ≤ I(X_t; X_{t+k}). By CT1, persistence needs D(I_pred) < y − c̄, i.e. I_pred > R(y − c̄), which is impossible once I(X_t; X_{t+k}) ≤ R(y − c̄). ∎ *Example:* a binary world flipping with probability 0.05 per step, income 0.30: k_max = 7 (`c6a`).

**CT4 (P3, the first bit must pay).** *As CT1, with memory priced κ per bit per step.* Let IB(I) = sup{I(M; X_{t+k}) : I(M; past) ≤ I}, the predictive information-bottleneck curve (concave, nondecreasing). Then L(I) = D(IB(I)) + κI is a convex lower bound on cost at memory size I, and its minimizer is positive iff κ < −(D∘IB)′(0⁺). For a Gaussian AR(1) world X_{t+1} = aX_t + noise with stationary variance Var, lead 1 and squared-error loss, a model is carried iff κ < 2 ln 2·a²·Var, and then I\* = ½ log₂(2 ln 2·a²·Var/κ).

*Proof.* D is convex and nonincreasing and IB is concave, so D∘IB is convex and L is convex on [0, ∞). A convex function's minimizer is positive iff its right derivative at 0 is negative: (D∘IB)′(0⁺) + κ < 0. In the Gaussian case the best memory is a noisy copy of X_t; with I bits about X_t the error variance of X_t is Var·2^{−2I}, so the prediction loss is Var[(1 − a²) + a²·2^{−2I}]. Its derivative at 0 is −2 ln 2·a²·Var, and setting the derivative of the total to zero gives 2^{−2I} = κ/(2 ln 2·a²·Var). ∎

The bound is tight in the Gaussian and binary-symmetric cases (`c6d`: 2.0014 vs 2.0018 bits by brute force). The Lagrangian (loss plus a price on information) is prior art (Sims 2003; Tishby & Polani 2011); the persistence operating point is the addition. In physical systems that meet Still et al.'s conditions, dissipation is at least kT(I_mem − I_pred), and deriving the information bottleneck from that bound is known (Still 2020).

**CT5 (E1, no decision, no model).** *Hypotheses of CT9.* If no action's payoff depends on the world (one effective action), then Φ = 0 and holding never beats fading, at any drift and any positive read price.

*Proof.* With one effective action, the Bayes risk of a reading of any age equals the blind risk: r(u) = r_∞ for all u, so Φ = ∫(r_∞ − r) = 0, and CT9's condition λc < 0 fails for every c > 0. ∎ Partly definitional. `theory/SCALE_ENDS.md` E1 (CLAIM_MAP N25).

**CT6 (I5, upkeep is a flow).** *World exogenous: M_{t−1} → S_{t−1} → S_t is a Markov chain for the world's causal state S; M_t is a function of (M_{t−1}, Y_t).* Let η be the strong data-processing constant of the channel S_{t−1} → S_t and I_obs = I(Y_t; S_t | M_{t−1}). In steady state, **I_obs ≥ (1 − η)·I(M; S).**

*Proof.* I(M_t; S_t) ≤ I(M_{t−1}, Y_t; S_t) = I(M_{t−1}; S_t) + I_obs (data processing, then the chain rule). By the strong data-processing inequality, I(M_{t−1}; S_t) ≤ η·I(M_{t−1}; S_{t−1}). In steady state both mutual informations equal I, so I ≤ ηI + I_obs. ∎

For a binary world flipping with probability q, η = (1 − 2q)², so I_obs ≥ 4q(1 − q)·I (Polyanskiy & Wu). For unconstrained Gaussian kernels η = 1 and the bound is empty; the jointly Gaussian contraction is used instead, and the Kalman filter meets it with equality (known: Mitter & Newton 2005; Horowitz & Sandberg 2014). `theory/INTERNAL_MODEL.md` §2, checks `i2`. Self-checked only.

**CT7 (O1, the tracking floor).** *A random walk of variance rate σ², not affected by the tracker; a tracker whose learning rate is bounded by its entropy production (Barato, Hartich & Seifert 2014).* Holding a mean squared error D dissipates heat at a rate of at least kT·σ²/(2D). With squared-error stakes w, the least total cost is **J\* = σ√(2kT·w)**, at D\* = σ√(kT/(2w)).

*Proof.* With the estimate fixed, drift destroys information about the walk at rate σ²/(2D) nats per unit time: ½ ln((Var X + σ²dt)/(D + σ²dt)) − ½ ln(Var X/D) → −σ²dt/(2D) as Var X grows. Holding D steady therefore needs a learning rate of at least σ²/(2D), and so dissipation of at least kT·σ²/(2D). Minimizing kT·σ²/(2D) + w·D over D gives D\* and J\* = 2√(kT·σ²·w/2). ∎

This is the same function as the inventory optimum σ√(2c·w) with c replaced by kT per nat. With one cost scale, one drift and one quadratic stake, dimensional analysis forces any optimum to be ∝ σ√(c·w), so only the √2 prefactor is informative. The bound needs no feedback from tracker to signal (otherwise Horowitz & Esposito 2014). `theory/MATH_ATTACK.md` §1.2; cross-checked against Duncan's theorem for the Kalman–Bucy filter (`objective_checks.out` §1).

### B.2 Keep, check or fade

*Setting for CT8–CT11 and Lemma B1.* A stationary Markov world (W-a), any loss, renewal checks (A3). r(u) is the Bayes risk of acting on a reading of age u (in drift-time units), with r(∞) = r_∞; the world's drift speed is λ (time rescaled as X_{λt}).

**CT8 (G1, staleness is monotone).** r(u) is nondecreasing in u, for every loss; a chain of copies with independent noise at each hop has risk nondecreasing in the number of hops.

*Proof.* By stationarity, r(u) is also the risk of predicting X_{u+s} from a reading at time s, while r(u + s) is the risk of predicting X_{u+s} from a reading at time 0. Since X_0 → X_s → X_{u+s} is Markov, the reading at 0 is a garbling of the reading at s with respect to X_{u+s}, and a garbling never lowers Bayes risk (Blackwell 1953). So r(u + s) ≥ r(u). A copy chain with independent hop noise is a garbling chain, so the same argument applies to hops. ∎

*Fails* for non-Markov observed coordinates: for an MA(2) signal a two-step-old reading has squared error 1.5 against 2.0 for a one-step-old one; seasonality does the same. Known in substance (Sun & Cyr, mutual-information version). CLAIM_MAP K6.

**CT9 (G2, hold or fade).** Checking every T, with s = λT, costs J(s) = λc/s + A(s) per unit time, where A(s) = (1/s)∫₀ˢ r(u) du; fading costs r_∞. **Some periodic schedule beats fading iff λ·c < Φ := ∫₀^∞ (r_∞ − r(u)) du.**

*Proof.* J(s) < r_∞ ⇔ λc < s(r_∞ − A(s)) = ∫₀ˢ (r_∞ − r(u)) du =: Ψ(s). By CT8 the integrand is nonnegative, so Ψ is nondecreasing with supremum Φ. Hence some s satisfies Ψ(s) > λc iff λc < Φ. ∎

The critical drift speed is λ_c = Φ/c. State-dependent intervals can only help (Φ/c ≤ λ_c ≤ sup_x Φ\*(x)/c), and retained history (filtering instead of renewal) only lowers holding cost, so λ < Φ/c remains sufficient. Checked: a two-state chain with 0–1 loss has Φ = ¼ and λ_c = 5 at c = 0.05, holding at 4.9 and fading at 5.1 (`core_checks.py`; `c1b`, `c1d`). Generalizes Cho & Garcia-Molina 2003; novelty unconfirmed.

**CT10 (G4, effort rises with stakes).** With check frequency f and stake w, J(f; w) = c·f + w·A(λ/f). The minimizing f is nondecreasing in w.

*Proof.* A is nondecreasing (a running average of a nondecreasing r), so A(λ/f) is nonincreasing in f. For f′ > f, J(f′; w) − J(f; w) = c(f′ − f) + w[A(λ/f′) − A(λ/f)] is nonincreasing in w: J has decreasing differences in (f, w), and the minimizer is nondecreasing in w (Topkis 1978). ∎ Known in substance; `c1c`.

**CT11 (T8, freeze, hold or look up).** *One item; squared-error loss at stake W; drift q at α = 2; read price c; upkeep h; lifetime τ_life; use rate μ; lookup price u per use, with μu > h.* Holding costs min_{T ≤ τ_life}[c/T + WqT/2] + h, freezing is the corner T = τ_life, and looking up costs μu. Then:
1. Freeze iff q ≤ q_f = 2c/(W·τ_life²).
2. Look up rather than hold iff q > q_c = (μu − h)²/(2cW).
3. A holding band exists iff μu·τ_life > 2c + h·τ_life.
4. A store shared by N users (stakes NW, lookups Nμu) multiplies q_c/q_f by N² at h = 0.
5. Effort just below q_c is (μu − h)/(2c) reads per unit time, and 0 above it: a cliff.

*Proof.* The interior optimum is T\* = √(2c/(Wq)), with cost √(2cWq) + h. (1) T\* ≥ τ_life ⇔ q ≤ 2c/(Wτ_life²). (2) √(2cWq) + h > μu ⇔ q > (μu − h)²/(2cW). (3) q_f < q_c ⇔ 4c² < τ_life²(μu − h)² ⇔ 2c < τ_life(μu − h). (4) With W → NW and μu → Nμu at h = 0, q_f is divided by N and q_c is multiplied by N. (5) The read rate 1/T\* = √(Wq/(2c)) at q = q_c equals (μu − h)/(2c). ∎

The cliff is known (Cho & Garcia-Molina 2003). The band condition and the N² widening appear to be new. If lookups return stale values or carry their own lag, the edge moves (not derived). `reviews/night1/formal/THEOREMS.md` Theorem 8; `reviews/night1/formal/checks/s5_phase.py`; CLAIM_MAP F13.

**Lemma B1 (G3, rise then fall).** Watching effort f\*(λ) rises from 0 as λ → 0 (as √(λ·r′(0)/(2c)) when r′(0) is finite), is positive on (0, λ_c) and is 0 for λ ≥ λ_c. With fallback at blind risk and r approaching r_∞ exponentially, it falls toward 0 only logarithmically (a near-cliff). With a bounded fallback F < r_∞ (read the source at use), holding stops at λ_c = Φ_F/c, with Φ_F = ∫₀^{s_F}(F − r), and effort jumps from λ_c/s_F to 0: a true cliff. If Φ = ∞ (a random walk under squared error with no fallback), effort rises without limit. *Proof:* CT9 applied with the fallback's cost in place of r_∞; `theory/GENERAL_THEOREM.md` §3, `c1e`.

### B.3 How often to look

*Setting for CT12–CT13.* A drifting world (W-b): Gaussian with stationary increments, E(X_{t+τ} − X_t)² = q·τ^{α−1}, 1 < α < 3. Exact reads at price c that reset the copy; the last value is held in between; squared-error loss at stake w.

**CT12 (the read-rate rule).** The optimal read interval is T\* = [α·c/((α − 1)·w·q)]^{1/α}, so the read rate is **∝ (w·q/c)^{1/α}**, and the minimized cost scales as c^{(α−1)/α}(w·q)^{1/α}: stakes and drift elasticities a = b = 1/α = 1/(1+2H). Equal gaps are optimal.

*Proof.* A copy of age s has squared error q·s^{α−1}. Over a cycle of length T the mean error is (1/T)∫₀^T q·s^{α−1} ds = q·T^{α−1}/α, so the cost rate is J(T) = c/T + w·q·T^{α−1}/α. J is strictly convex for α > 1, and J′(T) = −c/T² + w·q·(α − 1)·T^{α−2}/α = 0 gives T\*. Substituting gives the cost scaling. For n reads over a fixed horizon with gaps T_k, the cost nc + (w·q/α)·Σ T_k^α is minimized at equal gaps, by convexity of T^α (Jensen). ∎

At α = 2 this is T\* = √(2c/(w·σ²)): the economic order quantity (Harris 1913) and the inattention rule (Reis 2006). The exponent holds for hold-last-value and for the optimal linear predictor (any predictor class closed under time rescaling), but not for a predictor with a fixed time constant. For H ≠ ½, hold-last-value is a restricted technology (a predictor that uses the path does better). For the Bayes tracker of a non-Gaussian world, more of the world's statistics can enter: in a two-level switching world, jump rate and jump size enter separately (CLAIM_MAP N16). Formal Lemma 1(i); CLAIM_MAP N7.

**CT13 (T1c, robustness).** Reading at k·T\* instead of T\* raises the cost by the factor [(α − 1)/k + k^{α−1}]/α, which is (k + 1/k)/2 at α = 2. Misjudging c/(w·q) by a factor m sets k = m^{1/α}: at α = 2 that costs +6% at m = 2 and +74% at m = 10.

*Proof.* At T\* the first-order condition gives c/T\* = (α − 1)·E\* with E\* = w·q·T\*^{α−1}/α, so J\* = α·E\*. Then J(kT\*) = (α − 1)E\*/k + E\*·k^{α−1}. ∎ CLAIM_MAP M3.

**Lemma B2 (the rest of the spectrum map; known, stated with credit).** For a Gaussian world or the best linear tracker, with white observation noise of density σ_n²: the error of noisy continuous sensing is q^{1/α}σ_n^{2(1−1/α)}/sin(π/α) (Afterman et al. 2022; Kleptsyna et al. 2020); memory time ∝ (σ_n²/q)^{1/α} (Wiener/Kalman bandwidth); the optimal forgetting kernel puts weight ∝ τ^{−(1+α/2)} on an observation of age τ and is exponential only at α = 2 (Nuzman & Poor 2000; Gripenberg & Norros 1996); precision effort with a linear price ∝ w^{α/(2α−1)}q^{1/(2α−1)}; and the number of stores grows with the span of time scales and with stakes over upkeep away from α = 2 (rate conjectural). Premises and credits: `reference/THEORY_v1.4.29.md` §2.2.

### B.4 When to share a model

*Setting for CT14–CT16 (architecture class 𝒜₁).* N members, each tracking x_i = g + u_i, with g and the u_i independent and drifting with the same α at levels q_g and q_u; ρ = q_g/q_u. A sensing technology has minimized cost φ(w, q) = K·p^{1−a}w^a q^b with 0 ≤ a < 1 and 0 < b < 1 (the class P(a, b)). *Flat:* every member tracks x_i, at cost N·φ(w, q_g + q_u). *Level:* one tracker of g with the pooled stake N·w, plus a tracker of each u_i, at cost φ(Nw, q_g) + N·φ(w, q_u) + h. Equal prices unless stated.

**CT14 (pooling onset).** In units of K·p^{1−a}w^a q_u^b, the gain of the level is G(N) = N[(1+ρ)^b − 1] − N^a ρ^b − h′. G crosses zero exactly once, so the level is optimal iff N > N\*. At h = 0, **N\* = [ρ^b/((1+ρ)^b − 1)]^{1/(1−a)}**. As ρ → 0, N\*·ρ^{(1−b)/(1−a)} → b^{−1/(1−a)}, so the onset scales as ρ^{−(1−b)/(1−a)}. For the square-root technology (a = b = ½), **N\* = (√(1+ρ) + 1)²/ρ**.

*Proof.* Drift levels of independent components add, so flat costs N·K p^{1−a}w^a(q_g + q_u)^b; the pooled stake gives the level K p^{1−a}(Nw)^a q_g^b. Write A = (1+ρ)^b − 1 > 0 and C = ρ^b > 0, so G(N) = NA − N^a C − h′. Then G″ = a(1 − a)N^{a−2}C > 0 for a > 0 (G is linear if a = 0), G(0) = −h′ ≤ 0 and G → +∞. A convex function that starts at or below 0 and diverges crosses 0 exactly once from below; at h = 0 and a > 0, G < 0 near 0 because N^a dominates N. Setting G = 0 at h′ = 0 gives N^{1−a} = C/A. Since (1+ρ)^b − 1 = bρ + O(ρ²), C/A·ρ^{1−b} → 1/b. At a = b = ½, N\* = ρ/(√(1+ρ) − 1)² = (√(1+ρ) + 1)²/ρ, since (√(1+ρ) − 1)(√(1+ρ) + 1) = ρ. ∎

Within 𝒜₁ the condition is exact. A joint design outside 𝒜₁ can only do better, so for real choices **N > N\* is sufficient** for some shared design to beat flat, not a cutoff. With upkeep h > 0 the small-ρ exponent stays −(1−b)/(1−a) if a > b and becomes −1 if a ≤ b. With a cost linear in stakes (a = 1) no level ever pays. If the level and the members use different technologies, the **level's** (a_L, b_L) sets the exponent. `reviews/night1/formal/THEOREMS.md` Theorem 1; `s2_onset.py`, `s9_attack.py`; CLAIM_MAP B1–B3, B9. The onset −1 is prior art in substance (Veldkamp & Wolfers 2007, Prop. 2; Van Zandt & Radner 2001).

**CT15 (the level's technology sets the exponent, independently of α).** For exact reset reads the onset exponent is **−1 at every α**. For continuous sensing with precision priced as precision^θ it is **−(1 + θ) at every α**.

*Proof.* By CT14 the exponent is −(1−b)/(1−a). Reset reads have a = b = 1/α (CT12), so it is −1. For priced precision: with white observation noise of intensity r_n, the steady-state error of the best linear tracker is P = C_α·q^{1/α}·r_n^{(α−1)/α}. This is the form of Lemma B2; it is also forced by units, since q has units x²·t^{1−α}, r_n has units x²·t, P has units x², and the scale-free model has no other parameter. Minimizing W·C_α q^{1/α} r_n^{β} + c′·r_n^{−θ} over r_n, with β = (α − 1)/α, gives a cost ∝ (W q^{1/α})^{θ/(θ+β)} c′^{β/(θ+β)}. So a = θ/(θ + β) = αθ/(α(θ+1) − 1) and b = a/α = θ/(α(θ+1) − 1), and (1 − b)/(1 − a) = 1 + θ. ∎

*Premises:* the same α for shared and private drift; exact reads at the level (read noise steepens the exponent toward −2 at small ρ); no broadcast cost; h = 0 or a ≥ b; small ρ. What does depend on α: vigilance growth (∝ N^a), the mixed-technology private-drift exponent, and every constant. Refereed (`reviews/night2/N12_REFEREE.md`). CLAIM_MAP N12 is labelled MATH-CORRECTED because the original statement's scope claims were false; the exponent itself stands.

**CT16 (the telling price).** *𝒜₁ at a = b = ½ (square-root rule), zero latency.* A read costs c and telling one member costs d. Reading g centrally and telling all N members, plus reading each u_i locally, costs √(2(c + Nd)·N·w·q_g) + N√(2c·w·q_u). All-local costs N√(2c·w·(q_g + q_u)). **The hybrid is cheaper iff d/c < 1/N\*(ρ) − 1/N.**

*Proof.* The central tracker pays c + Nd per update for N stakes, so its cost is √(2(c + Nd)·Nw·q_g). Divide both sides of hybrid < all-local by N√(2c·w·q_u): √((1 + Nd/c)ρ/N) + 1 < √(1+ρ) ⇔ (1 + Nd/c)/N < (√(1+ρ) − 1)²/ρ = 1/N\*(ρ) ⇔ d/c < 1/N\*(ρ) − 1/N. ∎

At d = 0 this is CT14; as ρ → ∞ it becomes d/c < 1 − 1/N. Latency ℓ on the shared part adds N·w·q_g·ℓ to the hybrid. Like CT14 it compares one feasible hybrid with all-local, so it is sufficient for centralizing the shared part. It is a rule about any price of dissemination, and it is not conditional on shortcuts (CLAIM_MAP N41 is its shortcut instance; `theory/WORMHOLES.md` §2, 180/180 brute-force cases).

**Lemma B3 (shared and private drift push the onset in opposite directions).** ∂N\*/∂q_g < 0 < ∂N\*/∂q_u at every ρ, for a common technology (CLAIM_MAP C1; formal Theorem 3).

**Lemma B4 (vigilance at birth).** In the reset class at α = 2, the new level's effort over a flat member's effort at onset is V(N\*) = ½(1 + (1+ρ)^{−1/2})(1 + √(1 + 4h′(√(1+ρ) − 1)/(r_pρ))), where r_p is the level's read price over a member's and h′ is upkeep in member-cost units. Its floor (h′ = 0) is **1 + (1+ρ)^{−1/2}**, strictly between 1 and 2. The earlier "V ≥ 2" is false as stated (CLAIM_MAP E5, E6, E7).

**Lemma B5 (clocks).** With K independent components read separately at additive cost, in the technology class P(γ, γ), separate clocks save at most 1 − K^{−(1−γ)}: 29% for K = 2 at γ = ½. With indivisible reads they never save (formal Proposition 9; known).

**Lemma B6 (selection resolution).** In 𝒜₁ a level's largest possible fractional saving (N → ∞) is 1 − (1+ρ)^{−b} ≤ bρ (Bernoulli's inequality). So if selection resolves only relative differences larger than ε, no level is selected at any N when ρ ≤ (1 − ε)^{−1/b} − 1 ≈ ε/b: 0.020 at ε = 1% and 0.108 at ε = 5% (b = ½). Formal Proposition 10; CLAIM_MAP F15.

**Lemma B7 (√N fade).** Pooled deposits by N independent users on a drifting target fade faster as √N; on a stable target use preserves. Known (Kalman averaging), linear readouts only; an ant-like nonlinear readout failed in simulation (CLAIM_MAP K9, G15).

### B.5 Who must be in charge

**CT17 (one clock, and the externality).** (i) K parts at α = 2 with read prices c_i, stakes w_i and drifts σ_i² cost Σ σ_i√(2c_i·w_i) on their own optimal clocks, and √(2·Σc_i·Σw_iσ_i²) on one shared clock. The shared clock is never cheaper, with equality **iff c_i/(w_iσ_i²) is the same for all i**. (ii) If the whole loses Λ_whole when a part fails but the part counts only Λ_i, the part's chosen read rate is (Λ_i/Λ_whole)^{1/α} of the whole's optimum.

*Proof.* (i) One clock with interval T costs Σc_i/T + Σw_iσ_i²·T/2, minimized at √(2Σc_iΣw_iσ_i²). By Cauchy–Schwarz, (Σ√c_i·√(w_iσ_i²))² ≤ Σc_i·Σw_iσ_i², with equality iff √c_i ∝ √(w_iσ_i²). (ii) By CT12 the read rate scales as (stake)^{1/α}, and the part sets it with stake Λ_i instead of Λ_whole. ∎

With heterogeneous read prices the shared-clock penalty is unbounded (51× in random draws, 500× when cost sits in one part and drift in another). A worker sharing relatedness r_rel of its colony's fate reads at r_rel^{1/α} of the optimum; at r_rel = ¾ and α = 2 that is 0.87, so it under-watches by about 13%. The externality half holds when the failure cost is not internalized by incentives, shared fate or a controller, and it counts against "no one in charge" as a general claim. `theory/MATH_ATTACK.md` §3.4 (the analogue of Gorman's 1953 condition).

### B.6 Buffers, failure and the temperature

**CT18 (the temperature identity).** *The error accumulates from each fix as σ times a standard Brownian motion, and fixes act on one time scale τ (periodic resets, Poisson resets at rate 1/τ, or proportional restoring at rate 1/τ). Failure occurs when the error reaches B (one- or two-sided).* Then the hazard is **ν = F(z)/τ with z = B/(σ√τ)**, for some function F fixed by the fixing mechanism. Its elasticities satisfy **ε_B = −ε_σ and ε_τ = ε_σ/2 − 1**. Slow fixing raises the hazard iff ε_σ > 2, and at a fixed observed spread s = σ√τ, ε_τ = −1.

*Proof.* The inputs are σ (units x·t^{−1/2}), τ (t) and B (x), and ν has units 1/t. The only dimensionless group is z, so ν·τ = F(z) (Buckingham π). With g = d ln F/d ln z: ε_B = g, ε_σ = −g, and ε_τ = −g/2 − 1 = ε_σ/2 − 1. At fixed s, ν = F(B/s)/τ, so ε_τ = −1. ∎

**CT18-H (the same identity for self-similar error; proved in a model; new in v2.3).** *The error since each fix is σ times a self-similar process of exponent H, meaning X(λt) has the law of λ^H·X(t): fractional Brownian motion with Hurst exponent H, or a stable Lévy process of index α_s with H = 1/α_s; fixes act on one time scale τ.* Then ν = F_H(z)/τ with **z = B/(σ·τ^H)**, and **ε_B = −ε_σ, ε_τ = H·ε_σ − 1**. Slow fixing raises the hazard iff ε_σ > 1/H, and at a fixed observed spread s = σ·τ^H, ε_τ = −1 for every H. *Proof:* the units of σ are x·t^{−H}, so z is the only dimensionless group (Buckingham π), as in CT18; per-window ruin P(sup_{t≤τ}|X| ≥ B) = P(sup_{t≤1}|X| ≥ z) exactly, by self-similarity. The Brownian case is H = ½; H > ½ (persistent error) makes slow fixing worse than √τ, H < ½ (anti-persistent) better. Checked on simulated fBm (H = 0.3, 0.75): windows with different (σ, τ, B) and equal z have equal ruin probability (a code check: one seed and rescaled Cholesky factors are reused, so equality holds by construction, self-similarity being exact), and z_ε(H) is one number per H (`theory/meq_checks/rules_scaling_checks.py` §1, §5). Not covered: error not self-similar over the window (bias drift, a second group, the Péclet number), fixes that do not fully reset, or heavy-tailed fix times.

z is the inventory safety factor (SS = z·σ·√(lead time)). The √τ index fails once errors are biased (a second group, the Péclet number μ_b·B/σ² for a bias drift μ_b, enters), under heavy-tailed jumps and long memory (the group becomes σ·τ^H/B, and for a strictly stable Lévy error it is the τ^H group of CT18-H that holds, not the √τ group), under jumps that are not self-similar (compound Poisson with finite variance, where single big jumps dominate and no one group exists), and when fixes do not fully reset. How fixing works sets the tail of F (fixed delay e^{−z²/2}; proportional restoring e^{−z²}; Poisson fixing e^{−√2·z}; heavy-tailed fix times a power law). `theory/MATH_ATTACK.md` §2; `theory/GENERAL_THEOREM.md` R0–R5, which writes z = σ√τ/B.

**Lemma B8 (exact per-window ruin; known).** For periodic full resets and a one-sided barrier, the per-window ruin probability is P = erfc(z/√2) = 2Φ̄(z), the reflection-principle first-passage probability, which is Black & Cox's (1976) default probability at zero drift. Merton's (1974) terminal-default version is off by a factor of 2. The calendar hazard is ν = −ln(1 − P)/τ. Two-sided and biased cases have known series (`theory/GENERAL_THEOREM.md` R1–R2). DOIs: 10.1111/j.1540-6261.1976.tb01891.x; 10.1111/j.1540-6261.1974.tb03058.x.

**CT25 (temperature as a per-window chance constraint; proved in a model; it is not the master equation's lifetime-ruin constraint, CT22).** In CT18's setting with per-window ruin P = erfc(z/√2) (Lemma B8), P ≤ ε_r iff z ≥ z_ε := Φ̄⁻¹(ε_r/2), which is 2.576, 3.291 and 4.892 for ε_r = 10⁻², 10⁻³ and 10⁻⁶. So, for each fixing window, the buffer must satisfy B ≥ z_ε·σ√τ: it grows as shock × √(time to fix). Over a horizon T_h the number of windows grows as τ falls, so the buffer needed is B ≥ σ·τ^H·z(ε_r·τ/T_h) (a stricter per-window budget for shorter windows); the survivable-shock elasticity in τ is then −0.46, not −0.5, over τ ∈ [0.01, 1] at ε_r = 10⁻³, T_h = 100 (`theory/meq_checks/scope_checks.py` §6), and the qualitative ceiling (faster correction raises the survivable shock) survives. With B and ε_r fixed, the largest survivable shock is σ_max = B/(z_ε√τ) ∝ τ^{−1/2}, so faster correction raises the ceiling. **General form (CT18-H):** for self-similar error of exponent H, B ≥ z_ε(H)·σ·τ^H and σ_max ∝ τ^{−H}; for fBm at ε_r = 10⁻² the simulated z_ε (the 99% quantile of the window's maximum) is 2.89 at H = 0.3, 2.54 at H = ½ (exact 2.576; the grid slightly underestimates) and 2.35 at H = 0.75. The square root is the H = ½ special case. *Proof:* erfc is strictly decreasing; Monte Carlo of the closed form within 5% (`theory/meq_checks/scale_free_checks.py`, §6). Not implied: that stress strengthens a system, which needs an adaptation rule (redesign runs on tens of the slowest scale, B.9) and stops at B_min (CT22); that is K7, open. Coarse-graining rules (pooling cools, z′ = z√(N/(1+(N−1)ρ)) up to z/√ρ; the weakest link heats, z′ ≈ √(z² − ln N)) are in `theory/MATH_ATTACK.md`, numerical, not proved here.

**Lemma B9 (Theorem L, survival versus cost).** *Reserve income y with noise s₀; a check costs c every τ; squared-error loss w·e² accrues between checks; diffusion approximation, ruin checked at cycle ends.* (L1) P_ruin ≤ e^{−ϑB}, with ϑ the Lundberg root of E e^{−ϑΔ} = 1 for the cycle increment Δ. (L2) The survival-optimal interval is shorter than the cost-optimal one, because the loss variance grows like τ³; they agree when the reserve's own noise dominates. (L3) The survival-optimal interval does not depend on B, and the marginal value of raising ϑ, ∝ B·e^{−ϑB}, peaks at **ϑB = 1**. So rule 7's "watch most where shocks are large relative to buffers" holds for the value of extra effort, not for the cost-optimal interval. **Correction (v2.1):** the survival-optimal interval reduces to the cost-optimal (EOQ) one when the reserve's own noise dominates (policy-insensitive variance; checked: 0.314 against 0.316), not when the buffer is large. That the expected-cost rules are exact for a large enough buffer is a different statement, CT22. Cramér–Lundberg is known (Asmussen & Albrecher 2010). `theory/GENERAL_THEOREM.md` §5, `c3c`.

**Lemma B10 (finite horizon).** If a blind structure's reserve increments have conditional mean ≤ −δ < 0 and centred increments bounded by b, then P(it survives to T) ≤ exp(−(δT − B)²/(2b²T)) for T > B/δ (Azuma–Hoeffding). CLAIM_MAP N33.

**Lemma B11 (the internal-model bound).** The forecast miss of a memory equals the predictive information it fails to carry, δ = I(Past; Fut) − I(M; Fut). With causal states separated by γ_L, P(the memory's decoded state ≠ the world's causal state) ≤ √(2δ)/γ_L, and a solvent agent's memory must nearly copy the world's causal states. Parts are probably folklore (Shalizi & Crutchfield at δ = 0). `theory/INTERNAL_MODEL.md` §2. Self-checked only.

### B.7 How far one tracker reaches

**CT19 (LAT-1).** *W-b; a tracker at distance L from its target receives each reading after latency ℓ = L/v and holds the last value; reads every T.* Its mean squared error is (1/T)∫₀^T q(ℓ + s)^{α−1} ds ≥ q·ℓ^{α−1}, with the bound approached as T → 0. So a tolerable error D\* can be held only if q·ℓ^{α−1} ≤ D\*, i.e. L ≤ **L_max = v·(D\*/q)^{1/(α−1)}**.

*Proof.* At use, the held value is ℓ + s old, where s ∈ [0, T) is the time since the last arrival, so its error is q(ℓ + s)^{α−1} ≥ q·ℓ^{α−1}. Averaging preserves the bound, and reading more often only removes the s part. Inverting q(L/v)^{α−1} ≤ D\* gives L_max. ∎

*The three shortcut cases.* (a) **None:** v is light speed, and beyond L_max a system must federate. (b) **Finite shortcuts**, each spanning at most Λ_hop at hop latency at least τ_hop: a path covering distance D with n hops and light-speed travel time t_light has D ≤ n·Λ_hop + (light speed)·t_light and latency ≥ n·τ_hop + t_light, so its effective speed is at most max(Λ_hop/τ_hop, light speed), and LAT-1 holds with that v. (c) **Open shortcuts**, any point to any other at fixed latency: latency no longer grows with distance, so there is no distance limit, and CT16 decides the design. The theory does not say which case nature provides, and does not claim that shortcuts exist. Prior art in substance: Simon 1962; Tatikonda & Mitter 2004; consistency-latency trade-offs (Abadi 2012). `theory/SCALE_ENDS.md` §5; `theory/WORMHOLES.md` §4b. The real-system form is CLAIM_MAP N27 (open).

### B.8 Scale

**Lemma B12 (Theorem S, covariance and its limits).** (S1) If the objective is homogeneous of degree 1 in one currency, rescaling every cost leaves the optimum unchanged; (S2) rescaling time rescales optimal frequencies. (S3) With loss w|e|^p and self-similar drift e ∼ σ·t^H, the optimal interval is T\* = [c(pH + 1)/(pH·m_p·w·σ^p)]^{1/(1+pH)}: its exponents depend only on (p, H), not on the currency. (S4) Covariance fails when two cost components scale differently with size or currency; the optimum then shows crossovers, not one power. Proofs: S1–S2 by substitution, S3 by the first-order condition, S4 by example (a fixed plus a size-proportional read cost slides the local exponent from −1 to −½). That real systems sit in the generic class (p = 2, H = ½, by Taylor expansion and the central limit theorem) is a conjecture. `theory/GENERAL_THEOREM.md` §6, `c5a`–`c5c`.

### B.9 The master equation: the Master Theorem

The derivations, checks and worked example are in `theory/MASTER_EQUATION.md`; the review that produced them (a separate executor, same bot family) is `reviews/master-equation_2026-11-19/REVIEW.md`. **New, proved here or in the cited notes; not yet independently reviewed as a whole. CT24 and CT25 are new in v2.2, and CT18-H and CT26–CT29 in v2.3. The independent review of v2.3 found CT20 (ii), CT21 (iii), CT24, CT28 and parts of CT25 and CT29 stated too widely; v2.4 restates them (below), adds CT33 and CT34, and none of the restatements has been reviewed.**

*Setting 𝓜_LG.* World exogenous (g = 0): a zero-mean Gaussian process, stationary with spectral density S(ω) or with stationary increments, observed through white noise of density N > 0 or read exactly at price c. Design 𝒟 = (K; an LTI estimator realizable with K first-order stores whose rates lie in a compact set [b_min, b_max]; read rates f_k ∈ [0, F]; a sharing graph). Costs h > 0, c_k ≥ 0, κ ≥ 0, w > 0 in one currency; squared error at lead k ≥ 0. Ruin as in Lemma B9's diffusion reserve, imposed through the Lundberg bound θ(𝒟) ≥ ln(1/ε_r)/B, a sufficient condition.

**CT20 (existence; monotone comparative statics with sensing fixed; restated in v2.4).** (i) If any design is feasible, a minimizer exists, and K\* ≤ J(𝒟₁)/h for any feasible one-layer 𝒟₁. (ii) *With the sensing (read rates, noise) held fixed and the ruin constraint slack or absent*, and E_K the least expected loss over nested design classes for any world and any loss, K\* is nondecreasing in w/h; for one layer whose error grows with the age of its reading, f\* is nondecreasing in w/c.

*Proof.* (i) J ≥ h·K, so only finitely many K can beat 𝒟₁; for each K, J is continuous on a compact parameter set (Weierstrass). Without the compact rate set the infimum may only be approached. (ii) −J = −h·K − w·E_K has increasing differences in (K, w/h) because E_K is nonincreasing in K; Topkis (1978) and Milgrom & Shannon (1994) apply. Likewise −J(f) = −c·f − w·Ē(1/f) with Ē(1/f) decreasing in f. ∎ Check: K\* = 1, 1, 1, 2, 2, 2 at R = 1 … 1000 (`meq_derive.py`).

*Why (ii) needs fixed sensing.* If the read rates f are also chosen, J_K = hK + G_K(w) with G_K(w) = min_f [w·E_K(f) + c·f]; then ∂G_K/∂w = E_K(f\*_K(w)) must be nonincreasing in K, a single-crossing condition the nesting of classes does not give. **Counterexample** (the referee's): E₁(f) = 1/f, E₂(f) = min(1/f, 0.5 + 0.01/f) ≤ E₁, c = 1, h = 0.05 (a trend store that helps only while reads are rare): K\* = 2 for w/h ≤ 250 and K\* = 1 for w/h ≥ 260 (the two cost the same at w/h ≈ 255), so layers fall as stakes rise, while spend rises (0.42 → 4.5) and error falls (0.53 → 0.22): reads replaced the second layer (`scope_checks.py` §1). What remains true, with ruin slack, is CT33.

**CT21 (layers; L1–L3).** *Model S: s = Σ_j independent OU modes (rates a_j, variances v_j), the random walk as the a → 0 limit; noisy sensing; J_K = h·K + w·E_K; R = w·E_∞/h; Δ_K = E_K/E_∞ − 1.* (L1) One pole (one OU mode or a random walk): E_1 = E_∞ at every lead, so K\* ∈ {0, 1}, never more (K\* = 0, acting blind at cost w·Var, can win when stakes are small; for the random walk, whose blind error is unbounded, K\* = 1 at every w and h). (L2) J modes with distinct rates: the MMSE predictor has degree ≤ J at every lead, so K\* ≤ J; at k = 0 its degree is exactly J, so K\* = J for R large enough; layers beat flat iff R > R\* = min_K (K−1)/(Δ₁ − Δ_K). (L3) A non-rational spectrum (fBm with H ≠ ½, a continuous mixture of time scales) has E_K > E_∞ for every finite K; if also E_K → E_∞, K\*(R) → ∞.

*Proof.* (L1) The Kalman–Bucy filter of a scalar first-order state is one exponential store. (L2) Factor S + N = N·Π(ω² + b_j²)/Π(ω² + a_j²); P(−a_k²) ≠ 0, so no b equals an a, and the Wiener filter H = 1 − √N/G⁺ has no pole–zero cancellation: degree exactly J; the causal MMSE estimator is unique. (L3) If H were rational, G⁺ and so S + N would be rational. Full proofs and numerics: `theory/LAYERS.md` §2. ∎ The content of L1 is KNOWN (Kalman–Bucy 1961); L3 restates "finitely many stores are exact iff the spectrum is rational" (KNOWN). Each layer's time constant is 1/b_j, set by its mode **and** the noise, and faster than the mode. *Interlacing (proved in v2.4):* in x = ω² the roots of N + Σ_j c_j/(x + a_j²) = 0, c_j > 0, are the −b_j²; the function runs from +∞ to −∞ between each pair of consecutive poles −a_j², so there is a root in each gap, plus one to the left of the fastest pole (where it runs from N > 0 to −∞): a_1 < b_1 < a_2 < … < a_J < b_J. Checked on 200 random worlds (`scope_checks.py` §8). *Scope (restated v2.4):* all of this is for real-pole worlds (sums of OU modes). A lightly damped oscillator (ω₀ = 1, ζ = 0.05, signal-to-noise 0.1) has a Wiener filter with complex poles, McMillan degree 2, which no sum of real first-order stores equals: the best 1, 2, 3 real stores have excess error at least 68%, 45%, 30% (more stores keep helping), so counted by degree a rhythm is **two** layers, and counted as real stores its count grows without bound as stakes rise (`scope_checks.py` §4). Deterministic seasonality is not a stationary Gaussian mode.

**CL12 (L4, geometric clocks at every α).** Items with natural intervals log-uniform, K shared clocks over contiguous log-bands, penalty p(u) = [(α−1)e^{−u} + e^{(α−1)u}]/α − 1 (CT13): equal log-widths are optimal for every α in (1, 3). *Proof:* p is strictly convex, so a band's cost ψ(Δ) is strictly convex in its width (envelope theorem), and Jensen gives equal widths. `theory/LAYERS.md` Proposition L4; `theory/layers_checks/t7b_alpha.out`. Not the store model, where spacing is only roughly logarithmic.

**CT22 (survival; the price of survival).** Let θ(𝒟) be the Lundberg exponent, B_min = ln(1/ε_r)/max_𝒟 θ and B_slack = ln(1/ε_r)/θ(𝒟_E), with 𝒟_E the expected-cost optimum. For B < B_min no design is feasible. For B ≥ B_slack the constraint is slack and 𝒟_E is optimal (multiplier μ_s = 0). In between the constraint binds with μ_s = −m′/θ′ > 0, the marginal expected cost of survival exponent, and the design lies between 𝒟_E and the survival-optimal design of Lemma B9 (the μ_s → ∞ end). *Proof, on the scalar diffusion path of Lemma B9.* Growth of the feasible set in B orders the buffers and makes the optimal cost nonincreasing in B. It does not produce the multiplier, the uniqueness, or the monotone movement. Those are proved here.

Let the design coordinate be the check interval τ > 0, with c > 0 and α = wσ²/2 > 0. Then J(τ) = c/τ + ατ, so J″(τ) = 2c/τ³ > 0: J is strictly convex, and τ_E = √(c/α) is its unique minimizer. The reserve margin is m = y − J, so m′ = −J′. The diffusion variance rate is s²(τ) = s₀² + βτ³ with β = w²σ⁴/3 > 0, so (s²)″ = 6βτ > 0 for τ > 0: s² is strictly convex. On {m > 0} the certificate is θ = 2m/s², and the constraint is θ(τ) ≥ θ_req with θ_req = ln(1/ε_r)/B. Assume m(τ_E) > 0, so the expected-cost design has positive drift. (If it does not, τ_E is never certified and there is no slack regime.)

Let φ = m′s² − m(s²)′. Then θ′ = 2φ/(s²)², so θ′ has the sign of φ. Differentiating, φ′ = m″s² − m(s²)″. Both terms are negative on {m > 0}: m″ = −J″ < 0 and (s²)″ > 0. So φ is strictly decreasing where the certificate is positive. At the left end of that interval, m ↓ 0 with m′ > 0, so φ > 0. At τ_E, m′ = 0 and (s²)′ > 0, so φ(τ_E) < 0. Hence φ has exactly one zero τ_S, and τ_S < τ_E. θ is strictly unimodal: θ′ > 0 on the left of τ_S and θ′ < 0 on the right. Every superlevel set {θ ≥ θ_req} is a closed interval. It is nonempty if and only if θ_req ≤ θ(τ_S).

Set B_min = ln(1/ε_r)/θ(τ_S) and B_slack = ln(1/ε_r)/θ(τ_E). Strict unimodality gives θ(τ_S) > θ(τ_E) > 0, so 0 < B_min < B_slack.

(i) If B < B_min, then θ_req > θ(τ_S) and no design is certified. "Infeasible" means not certified by the Lundberg bound. The bound is sufficient (ruin is checked at cycle ends), not tight, and it does not say every uncertified design ruins.

(ii) If B > B_slack, then θ(τ_E) > θ_req, so τ_E is strictly feasible. Strict convexity puts the unique minimizer at τ_E. The constraint is slack and the multiplier is μ_s = 0.

(iii) If B = B_slack, then τ_E is feasible and optimal, and θ(τ_E) = θ_req. Stationarity holds at μ_s = 0 because J′(τ_E) = 0. The constraint holds with equality. θ′(τ_E) ≠ 0, since τ_E ≠ τ_S, so this multiplier is the only one.

(iv) If B_min < B < B_slack, then θ(τ_E) < θ_req < θ(τ_S). The feasible interval is [τ_L, τ_R] with τ_L < τ_S < τ_R < τ_E and θ(τ_R) = θ_req. The unique minimizer of a strictly convex function on an interval that lies entirely to the left of τ_E is the right endpoint, so τ\* = τ_R. Thus τ_S < τ\* < τ_E and the constraint binds.

*Constraint qualification.* θ′(τ\*) < 0, so the gradient of g = θ_req − θ does not vanish (LICQ on this one constraint). It fails only in the limit τ\* → τ_S, that is B ↓ B_min.

*Multiplier.* The Lagrangian is J + μ(θ_req − θ). At the binding point, J′(τ\*) − μ θ′(τ\*) = 0, so μ_s = J′(τ\*)/θ′(τ\*). Both derivatives are negative (τ\* < τ_E, and θ is decreasing there), so μ_s > 0. Since m′ = −J′, this is μ_s = −m′/θ′. Necessity is the one-variable Lagrange identity under the qualification above. Uniqueness of the minimizer is the projection of τ_E onto the feasible interval. That uses strict convexity of J and convexity of the feasible set. It does not need θ to be concave. θ is not concave on the whole domain: the second derivative of a ratio of this shape changes sign, and a convex-program argument that required a concave θ would be assuming more than the path has. The scalar geometry replaces it.

(v) On [τ_S, τ_E], θ is continuous and strictly decreasing, so it has a continuous inverse. θ_req(B) is continuous and strictly decreasing in B. Therefore τ\*(B) is continuous and strictly increasing, from τ_S at B = B_min to τ_E at B = B_slack. As B ↓ B_min, θ′(τ\*) → 0 while J′(τ_S) < 0, so μ_s → +∞. As B ↑ B_slack, J′(τ\*) → 0 while θ′(τ_E) < 0, so μ_s → 0+. The μ_s → ∞ end is Lemma B9's survival optimum. The μ_s = 0 end is the expected-cost optimum.

*What the hypotheses are for.* If J is not quasiconvex, a feasible local minimum can have J′ = 0, hence μ_s = 0, while the global unconstrained minimizer is uncertified. If θ is not unimodal, the feasible set need not be an interval and the minimizer need not lie between τ_S and τ_E. The check script exhibits both. Off a scalar path the projection step does not apply. The claim is this path.

*Check* (Lemma B9 point, ε_r = 10⁻³): B_min = 0.486, B_slack = 0.508, τ_S = 0.236, τ_E = 0.316, and τ\* moves 0.267 → 0.299 → 0.310 → 0.316 as B goes 0.49 → 0.50 → 0.505 → B_slack; μ_s = 0.0052 per unit θ at B = 0.5. `theory/representation_checks/binding_multiplier.py` (pytest `tools/test_representation_checks.py`) checks the sign identity for φ′, the multiplier identity, the monotone path, and the two obstructions. μ_s carries the system's own units. Risk-sensitive control has the same direction (Jacobson 1973).

**CT23 (local regret).** If J is C² in log design variables with a nondegenerate minimum and the design is computed from a picture of the world off by δ in log parameters, the design error is O(δ) and the regret is ½δᵀHδ + o(δ²). For one layer, underestimating drift by a factor m (so the interval T is too long) costs exactly [(α−1)m^{−1/α} + m^{(α−1)/α}]/α − 1 ≈ (α−1)(ln m)²/(2α²): 3.8% at α = 1.3 and 6.1% at α = 2 for m = 2 (overestimating drift by 2× costs more at α = 1.3, 4.9%). *Proof:* implicit-function and envelope theorems. **Exceptions:** a wrong layered design can be worse than no layering (7.0% against 2.2%); fitting α far from the decision's scales (up to 29%); and near feasibility edges (B_min, the lookup cliff) regret is not smooth, so a picture error can flip survival. A missing cost term is not a perturbation and has no such bound.

*Notation:* H is the Hurst exponent of the world (α = 1 + 2H); in CT18-H it is also the self-similarity exponent of the error since the last fix, and the two coincide for hold-last-value in the power-class tracker.

**Dynamics (elementary; predictions N54, N55).** Estimating the slowest rate a to relative precision δ needs a record of about 2/(aδ²) of that time scale (Monte Carlo: records of 10, 30, 100 slowest time constants give 0.55, 0.30, 0.15), so the record a redesign learns from is tens of the slowest tracked scale long (two-time-scale learning, Borkar 1997); that fixes the record **length**, not the update cadence, since a system can redesign daily on a sliding window (a cadence would come from a switching cost, with the hysteresis of Dixit 1989). The relative curvature of J in ln T at the optimum is exactly α − 1, so under noisy selection balanced against it the spread of ln(read interval) scales as 1/√(α − 1); that premise is standard for stabilizing selection and not derived for a specific selection model. Rates re-tune smoothly; K changes only at thresholds, with hysteresis under switching costs (Dixit 1989).

**Dimensionless groups.** Scaling time by T₀ = (c/(w·q))^{1/α} and cost by J₁ = c^{(α−1)/α}(w·q)^{1/α}, a parametric world's design is fixed by about 5–7 groups: the spectrum's shape, h/J₁, ℓ/T₀, μu/J₁, y/J₁, θB, and (N, ρ). Equal groups give the same design up to units (Theorem S); a general world's shape is a whole function. `theory/meq_checks/part2.out`. With the same R and mode ratio but a noise share of 0.005, 0.05 and 0.5, R\* is 135, 45 and 21, so the shape carries extra groups.

**CT24 (cost shares and the currency-free accounting; proved in a model).** *General form (v2.3):* let J(x; p) = Σᵢ pᵢ·φᵢ(x) with price-free terms φᵢ ≥ 0 and a feasible set that does not depend on p, and J\*(p) = min_x J. Then J\* is concave and homogeneous of degree 1, so at a differentiable optimum ∂J\*/∂pᵢ = φᵢ(x\*) (envelope) and Σᵢ pᵢ ∂J\*/∂pᵢ = J\* (Euler): **share of term i = elasticity of J\* in pᵢ, and the shares sum to 1**, for any number of terms and any design, provided the feasible set does not depend on the prices (no ruin constraint, or a slack one). *A binding ruin constraint is a counterexample (v2.4):* θ(𝒟) ≥ ln(1/ε)/B depends on the prices because the reserve pays J, and on income y and buffer B, so Euler runs over (c, w, y, B) jointly; in Lemma B9's setting at B = 0.5 the shares of reads and error are 0.528 and 0.472 but the elasticities are 0.584 and 0.563, summing to 1.147; at B = 5, slack, all are 0.500 (`scope_checks.py` §3). Sources: this is Hotelling's (1932) and Shephard's duality; the equal-cost property at α = 2 is Harris's (1913). A discrete choice such as the layer count K is covered between its switch points. If each term scales as a power x^{aᵢ} under one rescaling of the design, Σᵢ aᵢ·shareᵢ = 0 at an interior optimum (the virial identity); with two terms it fixes the split, so (sensing) : (error) = (α−1) : 1 for the single tracker. With more terms it does not, and for coupled layers the split is not derived. Independent trackers each obey it with their own α. *Setting of CT12 (hold-last-value, exact reset reads of a power-law world, J(T) = c/T + w·q·T^{α−1}/α, 1 < α < 3); the ratio (α − 1) needs the exact reads and the pure power law (it fails with read noise or a world with its own time scale, `scope_checks.py` §5).* The optimum is T\* = T₀(α/(α−1))^{1/α} and J\*/J₁ = (α/(α−1))^{1−1/α}, functions of α alone. J is degree-1 homogeneous in (c, w), so the optimum is invariant under any rescaling of the currency, and by the envelope theorem ∂ ln J\*/∂ ln c = (c/T\*)/J\* = (α−1)/α and ∂ ln J\*/∂ ln w = 1/α; they sum to 1 (Euler). Hence (spend on reads) : (cost of error) = α − 1. *Shadow price:* minimizing mean error subject to c/T ≤ B gives T = c/B; its multiplier λ = −de\*/dB satisfies λ·w = 1 when w is the stake that makes T\* = c/B optimal, and with several trackers under one budget the marginal error saved per currency unit is equal across them (KKT). *Floor:* with c = β·kT per nat, J\*(β)/J\*(1) = β^{(α−1)/α} (σ√(2βkT·w) at α = 2) and the shares are independent of β; J\* is nondecreasing in every price (envelope), so any actual price ≥ kT gives J\* ≥ the CT7 floor. Checked to 10⁻⁷ across 18 decades of currency (`theory/meq_checks/scale_free_checks.py`). It does not give the same numbers across systems (the world's shape and S4 still differ), and it makes no claim that any system pays in joules.

### B.10 Rules 4 and 6, pooling and nesting, the free-riding toy, the ant trail, the CX2 classification, judgment spend and exact nesting (CT26–CT34; new in v2.3 and v2.4; all proved in a model, none independently reviewed)

*Checks:* `theory/meq_checks/rules_scaling_checks.py` (CT26–CT29; run by `tools/test_rules_scaling_checks.py`; output in `rules_scaling_checks.out`), `theory/meq_checks/ants_cx2_checks.py` (CT30–CT32; `tools/test_ants_cx2_checks.py`; `ants_cx2_checks.out`) and `theory/meq_checks/scope_checks.py` (CT33, CT34 and the scope fixes of the v2.3 review; `tools/test_scope_checks.py`; `scope_checks.out`).

**CT26 (keep the superseded version; rule 4).** *A correction replaces a model. Keeping the old version costs upkeep k per unit time (κ·I_mem for a frozen version, with no refresh because it no longer drifts). Proof that the correction is right arrives after a time D ~ Exp(ν); the correction is wrong with probability p; if it is wrong the loss is L_r when the old version is still held and L_f > L_r when it is not. Keep the old version for a window t.* Then J(t) = k·(1 − e^{−νt})/ν + p·[L_r(1 − e^{−νt}) + L_f·e^{−νt}], and dJ/dt = e^{−νt}·[k − p·ν·(L_f − L_r)]. The sign does not depend on t, so the optimum is bang-bang: **keep the old version until proof iff k < p·ν·(L_f − L_r), otherwise drop it at once.** Checked on four parameter sets. The earlier objection ("symmetric loss, no recoverability term") is answered by letting the loss depend on whether the old version is held, which J allows because what to keep is part of 𝒟. It is close to a tautology (keep what is cheaper than the loss it prevents) and it needs recoverability (L_r < L_f) and proof that arrives (ν > 0).

**CT27 (outcome records as information; rules 6 and 1).** *Relevance r ~ U(0,1) is unknown; an item is returned iff the posterior mean is at least θ; the loss when wrong is |r − θ|; n outcome records (used or not) are Bernoulli draws of r.* The Bayes regret R(n) is nonincreasing (an extra record can be ignored). The marginal value of a record is not monotone: it is zero at n = 2 at θ = 0.4 (the decision does not change), so the value of information is not concave. Hence **n\*(price) = argmin R(n) + price·n is nonincreasing in the price per record** (monotone comparative statics, Topkis; n\* is a set, ties such as n = 2 against n = 4 at price 0.005 are resolved in the strong set order; the non-concave value of information is Radner & Stiglitz 1984, Chade & Schlee 2002), and records that cost only storage, because use creates them, are worth keeping in larger numbers. The first record is worth 0.033 against 0.0002 for the twentieth: this is rule 1's "what the outcome would teach". At θ = 0.4: n\* = 0, 2, 7, 22 at prices 0.05, 0.01, 0.002, 0.0002. Hebbian reinforcement (raise a link on use, let it decay otherwise) is an exponentially weighted average, the linear tracker of L1; in a simulation of a drifting relevance the best gain was interior (0.02 of 0.005–0.3), as L1 says. That nature implements rule 6 this way is a hypothesis, untested; the update rule itself is derived in CT30.

**CT28 (homogeneity of the single-tracker cost under pooling; proved in the upkeep-free power class; restated in v2.4).** *Power-class single tracker: J\*(W) = min_T c/T + W·q·T^{α−1}/α.* Substituting T = (c/(W·q))^{1/α}·u gives J\*(W) = c^{(α−1)/α}(W·q)^{1/α}·min_u(1/u + u^{α−1}/α), so **J\*(nW) = n^{1/α}J\*(W)**: one tracker of a common driver with pooled stake W = Σwᵢ·Bᵢ² (members' losses quadratic in the common error) costs n^{a−1} of n separate trackers, a = 1/α, and reads n^{−1/α} as often. Checked for α = 1.5, 2, 2.6 and n up to 40. It is one line from CT12 and at α = 2 is Eppen's (1979) square-root rule of pooling. It does **not** survive the term that creates layers: with hK in J, J\*(W) = min_K (hK + W·E_K) is not homogeneous in W. The earlier title, "every scale is a closure property of the cost", claimed more than this; that a team coarse-grains to a single member whose upkeep is its own J\* is **a conjecture**, for which CT34 gives conditions and a bound. *Nesting:* if J = J_low(x) + J_high(y; quantities fixed before x is chosen), the minimization separates and solving level by level equals the joint optimum exactly (CT34). If what is passed up depends on the lower level's design, level-by-level solving is a bilevel approximation: with J = J₀(x, y) + κ·J₁(x, y) and strictly convex J₀, solving y first at κ = 0 and then x leaves x and y off the joint optimum by O(κ) and the cost off by O(κ²), because the gradient vanishes at the joint optimum; numerically, with a product feedback of strength κ, the regret was 1.9×10⁻⁵, 7.4×10⁻⁵, 2.8×10⁻⁴ at κ = 0.05, 0.1, 0.2 (log-log slope 1.95). Time-scale separation is the usual heuristic for small κ (a fast layer's output hardly moves a slow layer); it makes the fast variable quasi-static but does not by itself make the coupling coefficient small, so the bound applies once κ is estimated (Borkar 1997 proves the two-time-scale adaptive version; not reproduced here). Conditions: stakes add (Gaussian or quadratic losses), the power class, smooth strictly convex J. Fails for heavy tails, costs that do not add (S4, B12) and mutual shaping of worlds (CX2).

**CT29 (compete, collaborate, organize: the free-riding toy; α = 2; the textbook private provision of a public good).** Known forms: only the largest-stake party contributes (Bergstrom, Blume & Varian 1986; the best-shot case, Hirshleifer 1983; Olson 1965's exploitation of the great by the small); stake-proportional cost shares are Lindahl pricing (Samuelson 1954); the damped-best-response threshold is the Cournot-stability condition (Theocharis 1960). *N parties track one shared driver. Party i has stake wᵢ and pays c·fᵢ for reads at rate fᵢ; a read helps everyone, so the error cost to i is wᵢ·q/(2F) with F = Σfⱼ.* **Joint** (one J, F\* = √(Wq/2c), W = Σwᵢ): J\* = √(2cqW). **Nash** (each minimizes its own J_i): party i alone would read at Fᵢ = √(wᵢq/2c), so only the largest stake a = max wᵢ reads, F = F_a, and every smaller party free-rides; the total cost is ½√(2cq)·(√a + W/√a). The **price of anarchy** is ΣJᵢ(Nash)/J\* = ½(√(a/W) + √(W/a)) ≥ 1 (AM–GM), equal to 1 iff one party has all the stake, and (N+1)/(2√N) for N equal parties (1.06 at N = 2, 2.1 at N = 16, 5.05 at N = 100). With separate trackers costing N·J₁ (J₁ one party alone), free-riding costs (N+1)/2·J₁ and pooling √N·J₁, so **free-riding captures (√N+1)/(2√N) of the pooling gain, tending to a half.** *Collaboration:* if party i pays the share sᵢ = wᵢ/W of the reads, its preferred total is √(wᵢq/(2sᵢc)) = F\* for every i, so cost shares proportional to stakes implement the joint optimum; equal shares do not. Revealing the stakes is not solved here (mechanism design). *Adaptation:* with **N equal parties** and the update f ← f + λ(best response − f), reads clipped at 0 (a negative read rate is outside the model), the deviation from the Nash total multiplies by 1 − Nλ per step, so it settles iff λ < 2/N, as checked for N = 2, 3, 5 (above 2/N the total oscillates) and from 30 random starts at 0.95·(2/N) and 1.05·(2/N) (all converge below the threshold, none above). With unequal stakes the active set changes and the threshold is **not** 2/N: for stakes (1, 2, 4) the iteration settles at λ = 0.6 and 0.9 (above 2/3) and not at 1.5 or 1.9; the threshold is not derived (`scope_checks.py` §7). *Not proved:* the general convergence of decentralized adaptation (false for arbitrary coupling, CT32), and merging iff the gap exceeds J_coupling (CX2, CX3; the inequality is a statement of the definition, not a theorem about real parts). The class results are CT31.


**CT30 (the ant-trail update rule; proved in a model; known in substance: Muth 1960, Kalman 1960).** That the steady-state optimal tracker of a random walk seen through white noise is exponential smoothing with a gain set by q/r is **Muth (1960)**, doi 10.1080/01621459.1960.10482064 (and the steady-state Kalman filter); what is new here is the reading as deposit and evaporation, the priced-precision rate, and the mapping onto trails. *A relevance s_t drifts as a random walk of variance q per step and is read through white noise of variance r (a use or no-use signal, Gaussian-approximated); the loss is quadratic.* The steady-state Kalman gain solves rλ² + qλ − q = 0, so **λ\* = (−ρ + √(ρ² + 4ρ))/2 with ρ = q/r**, and the steady-state tracker is x ← (1−λ\*)x + λ\*·y, which equals x + λ\*·y − λ\*·x: a deposit of λ\* on each use and evaporation of λ\* of what is there. Its error variance is V(λ) = ((1−λ)²q + λ²r)/(1 − (1−λ)²), minimized at λ\*; with intensities q·dt and r/dt, λ\*/dt → √(q/r). So the evaporation rate follows from drift over noise. *Prices:* the loss weight w multiplies V and drops out of the argmin at fixed noise; if precision is priced, J(r) = w√(q·r) + c′·r^{−θ} gives r\* = (2θc′/(w√q))^{1/(θ+½)} and an evaporation rate √(q/r\*) that rises with w. *Decentralized:* M trails with independent drift, each updated from its own signal only, equal the joint M-state Kalman filter because the joint problem is block-diagonal (a trivial decoupling, not a surprise), checked to 7×10⁻¹⁵, with Monte Carlo error within 2% of V; with a shared driver (q_g = 0.01, q_u = 0.0005, r = 0.5) a joint filter has MSE 0.030 against 0.067 for local smoothing, which is what a pooled level (CT14) buys. *Which parameter is predicted:* in a real colony the deposit d and the evaporation e are separate; the update is smoothing with gain e scaled by d/e, so only the evaporation rate e = λ\* is predicted, not d. *Endogenous use:* if the signal is generated by choices that follow the trail, the trail settles at (choice share) × (relevance) (0.357 against 0.51 × 0.7 under uniform choice), and with positive feedback a better path that starts weaker is abandoned in 19 of 20 runs (the classic lock-in under nonlinear positive feedback: Deneubourg et al. 1990; Goss, Aron, Deneubourg & Pasteels 1989, doi 10.1007/bf00462870; Arthur 1989 for technologies, doi 10.2307/2234208). *Not proved:* that real ants or synapses implement the rule because selection favored it; that the use signal is exogenous; Bernoulli (non-Gaussian) noise, where smoothing is the best *linear* tracker only. `theory/meq_checks/ants_cx2_checks.py` §1.

**CT31 (potential games and cost sharing; proved in a model; α = 2 shared-driver class).** *Party i pays cᵢ·fᵢ for reads at rate fᵢ and bears wᵢ·g(F), F = Σfⱼ, g decreasing and convex (g = q/2F in the toy).* Then Φ(f) = Σᵢ cᵢfᵢ/wᵢ + g(F) satisfies Jᵢ(fᵢ′, f₋ᵢ) − Jᵢ(f) = wᵢ·[Φ(f′) − Φ(f)] for every unilateral deviation, so the game is a weighted exact potential game (Monderer & Shapley 1996), checked to 8×10⁻¹⁵ over 2,000 random deviations with unequal read prices. Φ is convex, so its minimizers are the Nash equilibria: for equal read prices the largest-stake party reads alone, at the total F_a = √(a·q/2c), and sequential best response lowers Φ every step and converges. *Price of anarchy:* with x = W/a ∈ [1, N], PoA = ½(√x + 1/√x), increasing in x, so **PoA ≤ (N+1)/(2√N)**, equal to it iff stakes are equal (checked on 4,000 random stake vectors); no bound independent of N exists in this class. A bound for general g would come from smoothness (Roughgarden 2015) and is not derived here. *Alignment:* if party i pays the share wᵢ/W of all reads, Jᵢ = (wᵢ/W)·J_joint(F) exactly (checked to 9×10⁻¹⁶), so every best response minimizes the joint cost, Nash equals the joint optimum, and the game is an exact potential game with Φ = J_joint. This needs the stakes to be known (revealing them is mechanism design, Groves 1973, not solved here). *Not proved:* anything outside the aggregative, shared-driver class. `ants_cx2_checks.py` §2.

**CT32 (no general convergence theorem; what time-scale separation does and does not do; proved in models; restated in v2.4).** (i) *Opposed coupling:* J₁ = x₁x₂, J₂ = −x₁x₂ with continuous-time gradient adaptation ẋ₁ = −x₂, ẋ₂ = x₁ conserves x₁² + x₂², a closed orbit that never converges (checked by RK4 over 200 time units, radius 1.000000 → 1.000000); with simultaneous steps of size η the radius grows as (1 + η²)^{t/2} (4.45 after 300 steps at η = 0.1). (ii) *A Shapley (1964)-type 3×3 game* (the payoff table as commonly tabulated, taken from a secondary source and not checked against Shapley's chapter, whose existence was checked on Crossref; start at (a, B)): fictitious play does not converge to the mixed equilibrium; **checked numerically, a simulation and not a proof:** the empirical play stayed at a distance 0.86–1.03 from (⅓, ⅓, ⅓) over 200,000 rounds. So a convergence theorem for arbitrary coupling is false, and CX2's general case cannot be proved. (Both examples sit outside J's class: (i) is zero-sum with costs unbounded below, while J's terms are ≥ 0.) (iii) *Time-scale separation changes which stationary point gradient play reaches; it is not shown to restore equilibrium.* J₁ = x₁x₂ − (m/2)x₁², J₂ = −x₁x₂ + (b/2)x₂², with the first party adapting at speed ε and the second at speed 1, has Jacobian [[εm, −ε], [1, −b]], trace εm − b and determinant ε(1 − mb); for mb < 1 it is stable iff ε < b/m, checked at six speeds. **But the rest point (0, 0) is not a Nash equilibrium:** J₁ is concave in the slow party's own action (m > 0), so J₁(x₁, 0) = −(m/2)x₁² < J₁(0, 0) and party 1 gains by deviating in either direction; the point is a stationary point of gradient play (closer to a local-minimax or Stackelberg-type point). **With convex own costs separation does nothing:** for J₁ = (a/2)x₁² + c₁x₁x₂ and J₂ = (b/2)x₂² + c₂x₁x₂ with a, b > 0 the Jacobian [[−εa, −εc₁], [−c₂, −b]] has trace −εa − b < 0 and determinant ε(ab − c₁c₂), so stability holds iff ab > c₁c₂ for every ε (20,000 random games, none depended on ε; `scope_checks.py` §9). Whether separation restores convergence to equilibrium inside J's class (costs bounded below) is therefore **open**. Borkar (1997) proves convergence of two-time-scale stochastic approximation to the slow system's attractors given a stable fast equilibrium, which is not Nash convergence. `ants_cx2_checks.py` §2d.

**CT33 (judgment spend rises with stakes; proved, no model class needed; new in v2.4).** *Any design set 𝒟 with J(𝒟; w) = S(𝒟) + w·E(𝒟), S the spend on judgment (layers' upkeep, memory, reads, links, telling) and E the expected error per unit stake; stakes w, prices fixed.* For w₁ < w₂ with optimal designs 𝒟₁, 𝒟₂: S₁ + w₁E₁ ≤ S₂ + w₁E₂ and S₂ + w₂E₂ ≤ S₁ + w₂E₁. Adding gives (w₂ − w₁)(E₂ − E₁) ≤ 0, so **E\*(w) is nonincreasing**; then S₂ − S₁ ≥ w₁(E₁ − E₂) ≥ 0, so **S\*(w) is nondecreasing**. J\*(w) = min over designs of affine functions of w is concave with slope E\* (envelope). Nothing about the world, the loss, the design class, whether sensing is chosen, or whether K is discrete is used (ties are resolved by any selection), **but the design set must not depend on w: the ruin constraint must be slack at the stakes compared (B ≥ B_slack, CT22) or absent.** The master equation's constraint θ(𝒟)·B ≥ ln(1/ε_r) has a Lundberg exponent θ = 2(y − J)/σ² in which the reserve pays J and the variance of realized loss scales with w², so the feasible set shrinks with w and the step "𝒟₂ is feasible at w₁, 𝒟₁ at w₂" fails. *Counterexample (the referee's):* y = 20, θ ≥ 1; 𝒟₁ has S = 1, E = 1, σ² = 0.01w², 𝒟₂ has S = 3, E = 0.5, σ² = 0.2w²: 𝒟₂ is optimal for 4 < w ≤ 10, but for w ≥ 11 it is uncertified and 𝒟₁ is the optimum, so spend falls from 3 to 1 and error rises from 0.5 to 1 as stakes rise (`scope_checks.py` §1b); the same mechanism can break Master Theorem (ii) with sensing fixed once ruin binds. It is the revealed-preference inequality of comparative statics. It does **not** say the layer count rises, which fails when reads substitute for layers (the referee's counterexample, B.9), nor that spend falls when only h falls: at a fixed stake cheaper layers can lower S = hK\* (E_K = 1/K, w = 100: S = 10, 5, 2.5 as h = 1, ¼, 1/16). In units of h with price ratios fixed, raising w/h is the same problem as raising w, so S\*/h is nondecreasing in R = w/h. Checked on the counterexample, on 300 random design menus, and along rising stakes on the map of designs in 300 random worlds (`scope_checks.py` §1, §1c; `figures/face_map.py`, B.11).

**CT34 (when the team's optimized cost enters the parent exactly as upkeep, and the error when it does not; proved; new in v2.4).** *Parent design x, team design y; J(x, y) = F(x) + G(y; θ(x)), where θ is what the parent passes the team (stake, noise, drift); J\*_T(θ) = min_y G(y; θ).* (i) **Exactness (partial minimization):** min_{x,y} J = min_x [F(x) + J\*_T(θ(x))], and the minimizers agree; so the parent's problem is exactly F(x) plus the team's optimized cost, as a function of what it passes down. (ii) J\*_T is a **constant, that is, an upkeep h, if θ does not depend on x** (and, generically, only if: J\*_T can be flat in the direction θ moves), that is, if the parent sees the team only through quantities fixed before the team optimizes; then the parent's argmin ignores the team entirely. With θ(x) it is a value function with derivative ∂J\*_T/∂θ = the team's own error per unit stake (envelope; checked 0.134907 against q·T\*^{α−1}/α). Equivalently the parent may see the team through its aggregate cost–accuracy curve C_T(a) = min{G : E_T ≤ a}, exact for any x that only uses the accuracy a. (iii) **Error bound when the team is only weakly coupled.** Let J = F(x) + G(y) + ε·Φ(x, y) with G μ-strongly convex and Φ L-Lipschitz in y, y⁰ = argmin G, and freeze the team at y⁰: m₀(x) = F(x) + G(y⁰) + ε·Φ(x, y⁰) ≥ m(x) = min_y J. Then 0 ≤ m₀(x) − m(x) ≤ (εL)²/(2μ), and the parent's regret from optimizing m₀ is at most (εL)²/(2μ): **second order in the coupling.** *Proof:* G(y) ≥ G(y⁰) + (μ/2)d² and Φ(x, y) ≥ Φ(x, y⁰) − L·d with d = |y − y⁰| give G + εΦ ≥ G(y⁰) + εΦ(x, y⁰) + (μ/2)d² − εLd ≥ … − (εL)²/(2μ); m(x) ≤ m₀(x) because y⁰ is feasible; for the regret, m(x₀) ≤ m₀(x₀) ≤ m₀(x\*) ≤ m(x\*) + (εL)²/(2μ). Checked: worst gap 1.5×10⁻¹, 3.3×10⁻², 7.6×10⁻³, 1.8×10⁻³, 4.4×10⁻⁴ at ε = 0.4 … 0.025 against bounds 2.5×10⁻¹ … 9.8×10⁻⁴, slope 2.10. (iv) **A shared driver is not weak coupling:** N = 4 units whose drift is shared with weight ρ, treated as independent upkeeps, miss the pooling gain (CT14): per-unit error 0.0905 locally against 0.0902, 0.0878, 0.0799, 0.0410 pooled at ρ = 0.1, 0.3, 0.6, 1 (gain 0.0003, 0.0027, 0.0106, 0.0495: about ρ² at first, then large). *Not shown:* that real teams satisfy (i)–(iii); that the parent's problem is of this form. This is the precise form of the coarse-graining conjecture (CT28): conditions and a bound. Credits: partial minimization is the principle behind hierarchical decomposition (Dantzig & Wolfe 1960; Geoffrion 1970) and dynamic programming; near-decomposability is Simon & Ando 1961; the pooling form is Eppen 1979. `scope_checks.py` §2.

### B.11 The map of designs (new in v2.5; restated in v2.5.1–v2.5.3 after independent reviews 26 and 28; the restatements are self-checks)

*Setup.* n units; for one component of the world, a layer in each unit (gain G_L per unit, upkeep n·h_l), one shared layer pooling the n units' reads a delay ℓ late (G_S, h_c), both (G_B ≥ max(G_L, G_S)), or none; stake w_u per unit. Let X_L = w_u·G_L/h_l, X_S = n·w_u·G_S/h_c, X_B = n·w_u·G_B/(n·h_l + h_c), ν = n·h_l/h_c, κ_S = (G_B − G_L)/G_S, κ_L = (G_B − G_S)/G_L.

*Identities (any model, any loss).* Values over none in units of n·h_l are local = X_L − 1, shared = (X_S − 1)/ν, both = X_L − 1 + (κ_S X_S − 1)/ν = (1 + 1/ν)(X_B − 1), so the cheapest design is the argmax of (0, X_L − 1, (X_S − 1)/ν, (1 + 1/ν)(X_B − 1)); and (1 − κ_S)G_S = (1 − κ_L)G_L, so κ_S > 1 iff κ_L > 1.

**CT35 (the map theorem).** *Premises: G_L, G_S > 0 (so κ is defined), and gains that do not depend on the stake or the upkeeps (true when reading is fixed; with reading priced and chosen per design the optimized net gains move with w_u, so CT35 does not apply there). Fix the gains. The quadrant rule (none iff X_L ≤ 1 and X_S ≤ 1; local if X_L > 1 ≥ X_S; shared if X_S > 1 ≥ X_L; with both > 1, both iff κ_L X_L > 1 and κ_S X_S > 1, else local iff ν(X_L − 1) > X_S − 1) gives the cheapest design for every stake and upkeep if and only if the value is subadditive, G_B ≤ G_L + G_S.* *Proof.* If κ ≤ 1, both − local = (κ_S X_S − 1)/ν ≤ 0 where X_S ≤ 1, both − shared = κ_L X_L − 1 ≤ 0 where X_L ≤ 1, and X_B = (νX_L + κ_S X_S)/(ν + 1) ≤ 1 where both are ≤ 1. If κ_S > 1, choose upkeeps with X_L = X_S = 1 − ε: X_B → (ν + κ_S)/(ν + 1) > 1, so both beats none in none's quadrant (and likewise beats local just below X_S = 1). ∎

**CT36 (joint value is a common-mode reference).** *Linear-Gaussian, fixed rates: for two estimates with correlations r_L, r_S to the target and r_LS to each other, (G_B − G_L − G_S)/Var = r_LS[(r_L² + r_S²)r_LS − 2r_L r_S]/(1 − r_LS²).* With each estimate's sign chosen so that r_L, r_S ≥ 0 (no loss of generality), it is positive iff r_LS < 0 or r_LS > 2r_L r_S/(r_L² + r_S²). The condition is the statement. The intuition is only rough: a shared store whose link to the local estimate runs mostly through shared noise rather than the target can cancel that noise when subtracted. A store correlated more with the local noise than with the target is not enough (r_L = r_S = 0.5, r_LS = 0.8 gives excess −0.22). Estimates independent given the target (r_LS = r_L r_S) give excess r_L²r_S²(r_L² + r_S² − 2)/(1 − r_L²r_S²) ≤ 0. Prior art: the suppressor variable (Horst 1941; Conger 1974), adaptive noise cancelling with a reference channel (Widrow et al. 1975), team decision theory (Radner 1962; Marschak & Radner 1972), complementary signals (Börgers, Hernando-Veciana & Krähmer 2013). New here is only its consequence for layer design. *At optimized rates the formula is a necessary condition, not a classification:* CT35's gains are each at their own optimal rates, and at the rates b* optimal for both, G_L(b*) ≤ G_L^opt and G_S(b*) ≤ G_S^opt, so G_B* − G_L^opt − G_S^opt ≤ the fixed-rate excess at b*. A positive fixed-rate excess at the joint optimum is necessary, not sufficient, for superadditivity at optimized rates.

*Which classes are subadditive (CX6, `figures/face_subadd.py`; sampled, not proved).* One OU component per read channel, independent unit noise, unpriced reading: no violation in 500 + 3000 worlds and 20 adversarial maximisations (max κ_S = 0.99). Not subadditive: read noise common to the units (28/500 worlds), a fast common and a slow idiosyncratic part on one stream (8/500; review 26's counterexample B), reading priced at c/N and chosen per design (net worths superadditive in 119/1500 worlds, κ_L up to 49; counterexample A, where the reads are paid once by "both"; here CT35's fixed-gain premise also fails). Both counterexamples are regression tests in `tools/test_face_map.py`.

*OU closed forms (THEORY L1).* G_L = v − P(v, a, N), G_S = e^(−2aℓ)(v_m − P(v_m, a, N/n)), v_m = v(ρ + (1 − ρ)/n), P = Na(√(1 + 2v/(Na)) − 1); hence X_S = ν·π·e^(−2ℓ/τ)·X_L at equal read quality.

*One read stream.* Components sharing a channel are not separable; the cheapest store set is found by exhaustive search over small store counts, which is the rule. Adding stores one at a time (greedy) is exact when marginal worths are submodular, which κ > 1 breaks (counterexample B: greedy stops at none, local + shared pays). **Tested scope only:** in 800 two-scale worlds with ≤ 2 local and ≤ 2 shared stores (ρ = 1 in half, drawn per component in half), greedy matched exhaustive search in 99.75% (worst regret 2.2×10⁻⁵ of total cost) and greedy with a pairwise look-ahead in 100% (numerical only). Outside that scope the look-ahead misses too: in review 26's three-scale world (seed 40, ≤ 3 + 3 stores) the optimum is 2 local + 3 shared stores, while greedy and the look-ahead reach 3 local + 2 shared (regret 2.8×10⁻⁴ of total cost), because the optimum has fewer local stores than the greedy path, which additions cannot undo. The optimizer keeps stores of one kind ≥ 1% apart in rate, uses a truncated solve when the read-out matrix is ill-conditioned (cond > 10¹⁰), and confirms every optimum with the Lyapunov solution at rounded rates (review 26 found near-coincident stores fake a low error). The v2.5 claim of 600/600 used ρ = 1 throughout and is withdrawn.

*The figure.* Shading shows X_L > 1 and X_S > 1 (which single layer pays on its own); the marker shape is the exact rule; they agree under CT35's condition. Ruin is a gate: all of this holds where the ruin constraint is slack (as CT33).

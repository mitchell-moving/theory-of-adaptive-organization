## 0. Bottom line

**A repair floor exists, and it is proved in a clean class.** Keep a record of a fair bit that is gone — nothing else in the world still points at it — in carriers that each leak at rate at least \(\Gamma\) in a medium that does not know the bit. To hold the decoded error at or below \(\varepsilon\) at every moment of a horizon \(\tau\), the heat delivered to the bath, averaged over the horizon and over the bit, is at least

\[
\dot Q\;\ge\;kT\,\Gamma\,g(\varepsilon)\;-\;\frac{kT\,S_{\max}}{\tau},\qquad 4\bigl(\ln 2-h(\varepsilon)\bigr)\;\le\;g(\varepsilon),
\]

for every code, every amount of redundancy, every decoder and every corrector, with \(h\) the binary entropy in nats and \(S_{\max}\) the log of the number of joint states of the record and its corrector (**PROVED (in-model)**, Proposition RF below). "Every corrector" means every corrector modeled inside the finite joint Markov process, feedback included, that acts through its own mechanisms and leaves the carriers' energies and leak rates alone. A corrector that biases the carriers energetically makes record and corrector one cooperative medium, which RF-general and §5 treat. Idealized external measurement and feedback, priced by the Sagawa–Ueda second law, is outside the class and is credited with a lower floor in §2 (Zhao 2026). The constant \(4(\ln 2-h(\varepsilon))\) rises to \(4\ln 2\approx 2.7726\) as \(\varepsilon\to 0\). For one carrier the exact value is \(g=L(\varepsilon)=(1-2\varepsilon)\ln\frac{1-\varepsilon}{\varepsilon}\), the same function as the telegraph tracking floor of T59/T60. Holding a record of a vanished bit against a leak \(\Gamma\) costs, per unit time, what tracking a world that flips at rate \(\Gamma\) costs.

**The floor does not grow like \(\ln(1/\varepsilon)\) once redundancy is allowed.** With one carrier the floor diverges as \(\varepsilon\to 0\). With a repetition code it does not: at \(\varepsilon=10^{-9}\) the numerical floor falls from \(20.72\) (one carrier) to \(9.2378\) (21 carriers) and \(8.8335\) (41 carriers), in units of \(kT\Gamma\) (**NUMERICAL**). A diffusion-limit argument suggests the repetition value tends to \(8\) as the code grows and \(\varepsilon\to 0\) (**CONJECTURE**, heuristic). Small error is bought with room, not with heat.

**The self-correcting loophole is real for classical records in two and three dimensions.** The floor is \(kT\) times the modified log-Sobolev constant \(\rho\) of the medium's passive dynamics, times \(\ln 2-h(\varepsilon)\). Independent carriers have \(\rho=4\Gamma\) whatever the block size. A cooperative medium with an energy barrier that grows with block size has \(\rho\le 2\lambda\), with \(\lambda\) its spectral gap, and for a low-temperature Ising block in \(d\ge 2\) the gap shrinks like \(\exp(-c\,m^{(d-1)/d})\) in the block's carrier count \(m\) (**KNOWN**, Thomas 1989). Then this lower bound collapses, and, by passive metastable retention rather than by the inequality itself, a record can be held passively for any finite horizon at zero ongoing heat, with block size growing only polylogarithmically in the horizon. For quantum memories the picture is different: there is no such medium in two dimensions for stabilizer codes (Bravyi–Terhal), only partial self-correction is known in three (Haah's cubic code, up to a temperature-set size), and full self-correction is known in four (the 4D toric code). A record of a vanished bit is classical, so the classical case decides the question for records.

**So the area cap follows only for media that cannot self-correct.** For unprotected carriers, and a stated input that the region sheds heat at most \(q\) per unit boundary area, the number of records kept at error \(\varepsilon\) over a long horizon is at most

\[
N\;\le\;\frac{q\,A}{kT\,\Gamma\,g(\varepsilon)}\;\le\;\frac{q\,A}{4\,kT\,\Gamma\,(\ln 2-h(\varepsilon))},
\]

which scales with area at fixed \(q\), \(\Gamma\), \(T\) and \(\varepsilon\) (**PROVED (in-model)**, Proposition RF-cap). The cost of judgment supplies the price per record. The area comes from the heat-shed input, not from the cost. A classical cooperative medium in 2D or 3D evades the cap, restoring volume scaling up to a polylogarithmic block-size factor. The cap has no Planck coefficient, it does not touch static records, and it is not the Bekenstein or Bousso bound. It is the thermodynamic floor under a known engineering fact: dense memory that needs refresh is limited by how fast you can cool it.

## 3. Class RF and the repair floor

**Class RF.** A fair bit \(b\in\{0,1\}\) is written at time \(0\) and then the source is gone: from then on no degree of freedom outside the record and its corrector depends on \(b\). The record is \(n\) two-state carriers \(M=(m_1,\dots,m_n)\). A decoder is any set \(D\subset\{0,1\}^n\) read as "\(b=0\)". The medium's passive dynamics, the *leak*, flips carrier \(i\) at rate \(\Gamma_i\ge\Gamma>0\) in both directions, as a mechanism of its own with local detailed balance with a bath at temperature \(T\) (the two carrier states are degenerate, because the medium does not know \(b\)). The leak rates are fixed by the medium and are not modulated by anything else. Any corrector \(Y\) (finite, any size) may act on \(M\) and on itself through further mechanisms, each in local detailed balance with the same bath and with any work or chemical-fuel reservoirs. The joint process is a finite Markov jump process. The error constraint is \(P(M\notin D\mid b=0)\le\varepsilon\) and \(P(M\in D\mid b=1)\le\varepsilon\) at every time in \([0,\tau]\), with \(\varepsilon<1/2\).

For a distribution \(p\) on \(\{0,1\}^n\) write the leak entropy production

\[
\sigma_{\Gamma}[p]=\sum_i\Gamma_i\sum_{\{x,x^{(i)}\}}\bigl(p_x-p_{x^{(i)}}\bigr)\ln\frac{p_x}{p_{x^{(i)}}}\;\ge 0,
\]

with \(x^{(i)}\) the pattern \(x\) with carrier \(i\) flipped, each unordered edge counted once. With a symmetric leak, \(\sigma_\Gamma[p]=-\tfrac{d}{dt}D(p\,\|\,u)\) under the leak alone, with \(u\) uniform (**ELEMENTARY**; checked by finite difference).

**Step 1 (edgewise second law, KNOWN).** The total entropy production rate of the joint process is a sum over mechanisms and edges of nonnegative terms (Schnakenberg 1976). Its leak part, on the joint distribution \(p(m,y)\), is \(\sum_y\sum_{\text{leak edges}}(a_y-b_y)\ln(a_y/b_y)\) with \(a_y=\Gamma_i p(x,y)\), \(b_y=\Gamma_i p(x^{(i)},y)\), because the leak rate does not depend on \(y\). By the log-sum inequality, \(\sum_y(a_y-b_y)\ln(a_y/b_y)\ge(A-B)\ln(A/B)\) with \(A=\sum_ya_y\), \(B=\sum_yb_y\). So the total entropy production rate is at least \(\sigma_\Gamma[p_M(t)]\), evaluated on the record's own marginal (**ELEMENTARY**). The total entropy production equals \(\dot S_{\mathrm{joint}}+\dot S_{\mathrm{res}}\), with \(\dot S_{\mathrm{res}}\) the entropy delivered to the reservoirs. Write \(\dot Q\equiv kT\,\dot S_{\mathrm{res}}\) (entropy in nats) for the dissipated power: with only a work source it is the heat delivered to the bath; with chemical fuel it is the free energy consumed, part of which may leave as entropy carried by waste matter rather than as heat. Integrating over \([0,\tau]\), and using \(S_{\mathrm{joint}}(\tau)-S_{\mathrm{joint}}(0)\le S_{\max}\),

\[
\frac{1}{\tau}\int_0^\tau\dot Q\,dt\;\ge\;\frac{kT}{\tau}\int_0^\tau\sigma_\Gamma[p_M(t)]\,dt\;-\;\frac{kT\,S_{\max}}{\tau}.
\]

**Step 2 (single carrier, PROVED).** For \(n=1\) at error \(\varepsilon\), \(\sigma_\Gamma=\Gamma L(\varepsilon)\), and \(L(\varepsilon)\ge 4(\ln 2-h(\varepsilon))\). *Proof.* With \(x=1-2\varepsilon\), \(L=x\ln\frac{1+x}{1-x}=\sum_{k\ge1}\frac{2}{2k-1}x^{2k}\) and \(4(\ln 2-h)=2[(1+x)\ln(1+x)+(1-x)\ln(1-x)]=\sum_{k\ge1}\frac{2}{k(2k-1)}x^{2k}\). The coefficients compare termwise, with equality only at \(k=1\). So the ratio \(\sigma/D(p\|u)\) is at least \(4\Gamma\) for one carrier, and tends to \(4\Gamma\) as \(\varepsilon\to 1/2\).

**Step 3 (tensorization, ELEMENTARY given the standard entropy subadditivity).** For the uniform product measure, \(D(p\|u)\le\sum_i\mathbb E_{x_{-i}\sim p}\,D\bigl(p(\cdot\mid x_{-i})\,\|\,u_i\bigr)\), and \(\sigma_\Gamma[p]=\sum_i\mathbb E_{x_{-i}\sim p}\,\sigma_{\Gamma_i}\bigl[p(\cdot\mid x_{-i})\bigr]\) because each edge term is homogeneous of degree one. Step 2 on each slice gives \(\sigma_\Gamma[p]\ge 4\Gamma\,D(p\|u)\) for every \(p\) on \(\{0,1\}^n\) (checked on 1,500 random distributions, \(n\le 5\)). In the language of Bobkov and Tetali, \(4\Gamma\) bounds the modified log-Sobolev constant of independent carriers from below, uniformly in \(n\).

**Step 4 (the decoder, ELEMENTARY).** Let \(p_0,p_1\) be the record's distributions given \(b=0,1\) at some time, and \(\delta=|D|/2^n\). By data processing, \(D(p_0\|u)\ge\mathrm{kl}(p_0(D)\,\|\,\delta)\) and \(D(p_1\|u)\ge\mathrm{kl}(p_1(D^c)\,\|\,1-\delta)\), with \(p_0(D),p_1(D^c)\ge 1-\varepsilon\). Minimizing over the admissible masses and then over \(\delta\) gives \(D(p_0\|u)+D(p_1\|u)\ge 2\,\mathrm{kl}(1-\varepsilon\,\|\,\tfrac12)=2(\ln 2-h(\varepsilon))\): for \(\delta\in[\varepsilon,1-\varepsilon]\) the sum \(\mathrm{kl}(1-\varepsilon\|\delta)+\mathrm{kl}(1-\varepsilon\|1-\delta)\) is convex and symmetric about \(1/2\); outside that band one term alone is at least \(\mathrm{kl}(1-\varepsilon\|\varepsilon)=L(\varepsilon)\ge 2\,\mathrm{kl}(1-\varepsilon\|\tfrac12)\) by convexity of \(\mathrm{kl}\) in its second argument.

**Proposition RF (PROVED (in-model), class RF).** Averaged over the fair bit and over the horizon,

\[
\overline{\dot Q}\;\ge\;4\,kT\,\Gamma\,\bigl(\ln 2-h(\varepsilon)\bigr)\;-\;\frac{kT\,S_{\max}}{\tau},
\]

for every \(n\), code, decoder and corrector in class RF. For long horizons the second term vanishes and the repair floor is \(kT\Gamma g(\varepsilon)\) with \(g\ge 4(\ln 2-h(\varepsilon))\), which tends to \(4\ln 2=2.7726\) as \(\varepsilon\to0\). For one carrier it is \(kT\Gamma L(\varepsilon)\) exactly, the telegraph number at \(\Gamma\) (at \(\varepsilon=0.2\): \(0.831776616672\), against the general constant \(0.770979\)).

**Proposition RF-general (ELEMENTARY).** The same four steps, for any finite medium whose passive dynamics is reversible with respect to a stationary state \(\pi\) that is symmetric under the logical flip, give \(\overline{\dot Q}\ge kT\,\rho\,(\ln 2-h(\varepsilon))-kT S_{\max}/\tau\), with \(\rho=\inf_p\sigma[p]/D(p\|\pi)\) the medium's modified log-Sobolev constant. Independent carriers have \(\rho\ge 4\Gamma\). Expanding \(p=\pi(1+\eta f)\) along the slowest mode shows \(\rho\le 2\lambda\), with \(\lambda\) the spectral gap of the passive dynamics (**ELEMENTARY**; checked on 50 random reversible chains). So this lower bound can never exceed \(2kT\lambda(\ln 2-h(\varepsilon))\), and a medium whose gap closes leaves no repair floor from this argument. That the cost is actually near zero in such a medium needs a separate construction (passive metastable retention, §5). A small gap from a slow mode unrelated to the logical bit would weaken the bound without removing the cost. This inequality is where the self-correcting loophole of §5 enters.

**Proposition RF-forget (PROVED (in-model), standard Markov-chain fact).** If every degree of freedom that carries \(b\) leaks — the joint chain of record and corrector is finite and irreducible — then it has a single stationary state, which does not depend on \(b\), and the decoded error tends to \(1/2\). A finite, fully leaky scheme cannot keep a record forever, at any heat. The script shows this for three carriers with a majority corrector: one zero eigenvalue, and the error after \(2000/\Gamma\) is \(0.5\) to three decimals. "Forever" therefore needs either an infinite medium with a phase transition, or an unbounded horizon handled by blocks that grow with the horizon. That is why Proposition RF is stated over a horizon \(\tau\), and why the long-horizon cap below is a statement for \(\tau\gg S_{\max}/\Gamma\).

**Is the floor attained?** For a corrector that already knows \(b\) (a reference that is still present), fast near-reversible correction with fuel affinities \(\ln(p_x/p_y)\) on each leak edge attains \(kT\sigma_\Gamma[p]\) in the limit of fast correction (**ELEMENTARY**, the housekeeping construction). For a \(b\)-blind corrector, RF-forget says the state is only quasi-stationary; whether \(\min_p\sigma_\Gamma[p]\) is attained up to a lifetime correction that vanishes with block size is **OPEN**.

## 4. Redundancy: what the floor does with more carriers (NUMERICAL)

For a repetition code of odd length \(n\) with majority decoding, symmetrizing within Hamming-weight classes is exact (the weight chain has up-rate \((n-w)\Gamma\) and down-rate \(w\Gamma\), and the edge sum collapses), and \(\sigma_\Gamma\) is convex, so \(g_n^{\mathrm{rep}}(\varepsilon)=\min\sigma_\Gamma/\Gamma\) subject to \(P(w>n/2)\le\varepsilon\) is a finite convex program. It is a lower bound on the repair floor for any scheme that uses that code and decoder (Step 1). Values, in units of \(kT\Gamma\):

| \(\varepsilon\) | \(n=1\) | \(3\) | \(5\) | \(9\) | \(21\) | \(41\) | \(4(\ln2-h)\) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| \(0.2\) | 0.8318 | 0.9863 | 1.0448 | 1.0943 | 1.1367 | 1.1541 | 0.7710 |
| \(10^{-3}\) | 6.8929 | 6.8356 | 6.7998 | 6.7627 | 6.7275 | 6.7153 | 2.7410 |
| \(10^{-9}\) | 20.7233 | 12.2382 | 10.9797 | 10.0572 | 9.2378 | 8.8335 | 2.7726 |

At large error one carrier is best. At small error redundancy wins, and the floor stops growing with \(\ln(1/\varepsilon)\). An exploratory run outside the script (same program) reached \(7.96\) at \(n=321\), \(\varepsilon=10^{-6}\). In the diffusion limit the scaled magnetization is an Ornstein–Uhlenbeck process of rate \(2\Gamma\), holding \(p=\psi^2\varphi\) costs \(2\Gamma\cdot 4\int\psi'^2\varphi\), and the half-line ground state \(\psi=\sqrt2\,y\) gives \(8\Gamma\) with no mass on the wrong side (**CONJECTURE**, heuristic: that \(\lim_{\varepsilon\to0}\inf_n g^{\mathrm{rep}}_n(\varepsilon)=8\)). The exact infimum of \(g\) over all codes lies between \(4(\ln 2-h(\varepsilon))\) and the repetition values; which codes approach the lower end is **OPEN**.

The design reading: below a crossover error, small error is bought with carriers (room), not with heat per record. That moves pressure from the heat-shed constraint onto the room constraint \(N m\le\rho_cV\).

## 5. The self-correcting loophole: when the leak falls with block size

Proposition RF-general says the floor is \(kT\rho(\ln 2-h(\varepsilon))\) with \(\rho\le 2\lambda\). The loophole is a medium whose logical relaxation rate \(\lambda(m)\) falls as the block grows. Then the lower bound falls with it, and, more directly, for a horizon \(\tau\) a passive block with \(\lambda(m)\tau\lesssim\varepsilon\) holds the record with no correction at all: T59's metastable retention, made as long as you like by growing the block.

- **Independent or non-cooperative carriers, any dimension.** \(\rho\ge 4\Gamma\) for every block size (§3). No loophole. A per-carrier barrier \(\Delta E\) lowers \(\Gamma=\nu e^{-\Delta E/kT}\), but \(\Delta E\) is bounded by the medium's binding energies, an input.
- **Classical, \(d=1\).** No finite-temperature ordered phase (the Peierls argument needs \(d\ge2\)); no exponential protection with block size. The exact size dependence is not used here (**OPEN** in this note). Active 1D schemes exist (Gács 2001), but they correct continually and dissipate per cell, which is not a loophole for the heat cap.
- **Classical, \(d=2,3\), below the ordering temperature.** \(\lambda(m)\sim\exp(-c\,m^{(d-1)/d})\) in the low-temperature regime (**KNOWN**, Thomas 1989; Peierls 1936). For horizon \(\tau\) the block needs \(m(\tau)=\bigl(\ln(\nu\tau/\varepsilon)/c\bigr)^{d/(d-1)}\) carriers. Worked, with \(c=2\), \(\nu=10^{12}\,\mathrm{s^{-1}}\), \(\tau=10^9\,\mathrm s\), \(\varepsilon=10^{-9}\): \(m=203.0\) in \(d=3\), \(1193\) in \(d=2\). Ongoing heat zero, stock \(\rho_cV/m(\tau)\): volume scaling up to a polylogarithmic factor (**ELEMENTARY** given the known gap scaling). This is the magnetic-grain principle of ordinary hard drives. Toom's two-dimensional rule is active and dissipative, not passive; it does not supply this loophole.
- **Quantum, \(d=2\).** No self-correction for stabilizer codes (**KNOWN**, Bravyi–Terhal 2009), nor for commuting-projector local topological order (Landon-Cardinal–Poulin 2013); the 2D toric code's thermalization time does not grow with size (Alicki–Fannes–Horodecki 2009). For quantum records in 2D media the floor stands and the cap below applies.
- **Quantum, \(d=3\).** Partial self-correction only, in known models: Haah's cubic code has memory time \(\ge L^{c\beta}\) up to a size \(L^*\) set by temperature, beyond which the gain stops (**KNOWN**, Bravyi–Haah 2013); translation- and scale-symmetric stabilizer codes are excluded (Yoshida 2011); the welded code has a power-law barrier (Michnicki 2014), whose memory time this note does not claim. The 3D toric code keeps a classical sector up to a finite \(T_c\) (Castelnovo–Chamon 2008). Full quantum self-correction in a local 3D model is an open question in the literature (Brown et al. 2016), so whether quantum records evade the cap in 3D is **OPEN**.
- **Quantum, \(d=4\).** The 4D toric code is self-correcting (**KNOWN**, Dennis et al. 2002; Alicki et al. 2010). Not available in three-dimensional space without non-local wiring.

**When the fall is fast enough (ELEMENTARY, under one stated assumption).** Suppose that a block's long-horizon repair heat scales as \(kT\Gamma(m)g(\varepsilon)\) with its logical leak \(\Gamma(m)\) — an assumption, not proved here, since RF bounds the floor only from below. In a region of radius \(R\), the stock is \(N(R)=\max_m\min\{\rho_cV/m,\;qA/(kT\Gamma(m)g)\}\). A power-law leak \(\Gamma(m)\propto m^{-\kappa}\) gives \(N\propto R^{\,3-1/(1+\kappa)}\): area at \(\kappa=0\), volume as \(\kappa\to\infty\), and intermediate between (the script fits \(2\), \(2.5\) and \(2.75\) at \(\kappa=0,1,3\)). A stretched-exponential leak, the classical \(d\ge 2\) case, gives volume up to a polylogarithm. A leak that saturates at a block size \(m^*\), the partial case, returns to area scaling beyond \(m^*\) with an improved constant.

## 6. The transport-limited cap, in-model and worked

**Input (stated, not derived).** The region sits at temperature \(T\); in a long-run state the entropy it produces must leave through its boundary, as heat or carried by matter, at most \(q/T\) per unit area per unit time. So \(q\) is the dissipated power the boundary can carry away per unit area (\(q\) is an input, for example a cooling limit; it is not a Planck-unit quantity).

**Proposition RF-cap (PROVED (in-model), class RF plus the shedding input).** For \(N\) records in unprotected carriers, kept at error \(\varepsilon\) over a horizon \(\tau\gg S_{\max}/\Gamma\), Proposition RF applied to all \(N\) records at once (leak edges of different records add, and the log-sum step applies to each record's marginal; \(S_{\max}\) is then the total) and the input give \(N\,kT\Gamma g(\varepsilon)\le qA\), so

\[
N\;\le\;\frac{q\,A}{kT\,\Gamma\,g(\varepsilon)}\;\le\;\frac{q\,A}{4\,kT\,\Gamma\,(\ln 2-h(\varepsilon))}.
\]

Together with room, \(N\le\rho_cV/m\), the heat cap binds beyond a crossover size. It scales with area at fixed \(q,\Gamma,T,\varepsilon\). It does not apply to static records held against a present reference (T62, AL-stock-free), to horizons shorter than the lifetime (T59, metastable retention), or to classical cooperative media below their ordering temperature (§5).

**Worked numbers (SI).** \(T=300\,\mathrm K\), \(\nu=10^{12}\,\mathrm{s^{-1}}\), \(\varepsilon=10^{-9}\) (so \(g\ge 2.7726\)), \(q=10^{6}\,\mathrm{W\,m^{-2}}\) (\(100\,\mathrm{W\,cm^{-2}}\), a cooling-limited figure, input), carrier density \(\rho_c=10^{28}\,\mathrm{m^{-3}}\), one carrier per record for the most generous room bound, a sphere with crossover radius \(R^*=3qm/(\rho_c\,kT\Gamma g)\):

| barrier \(\Delta E\) | \(\Gamma\) (s\(^{-1}\)) | lifetime | heat cap per m\(^2\) of boundary | \(R^*\) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.50 eV | \(3.98\times10^{3}\) | 0.25 ms | \(2.19\times10^{22}\) | 6.6 µm |
| 0.75 eV | \(0.252\) | 4.0 s | \(3.46\times10^{26}\) | 0.10 m |
| 1.00 eV | \(1.59\times10^{-5}\) | 17.5 h | \(5.48\times10^{30}\) | \(1.65\times10^{3}\) m |
| 1.25 eV | \(1.00\times10^{-9}\) | 32 yr | \(8.69\times10^{34}\) | \(2.6\times10^{7}\) m |

Leaky carriers (sub-eV barriers, the DRAM-like regime) hit the heat cap at microscopic sizes; eV-scale barriers make it irrelevant at human scales. Dimensionless: with \(kT=\Gamma=q=1\), one carrier per lattice site, \(\varepsilon=0.2\), a cube of side \(n\) has room \(n^3\) and heat cap \(6n^2/0.770979\); the heat cap binds above side \(7.78\).

## 7. What is new, and how it differs from Bekenstein and Bousso

**Known.** That dense memory needing refresh, and dense logic, are limited by heat removal is engineering knowledge (for logic, Zhirnov et al. 2003; for memory, refresh power). That maintaining a nonequilibrium state costs at least its housekeeping entropy production is known (Hatano–Sasa, Esposito–Van den Broeck). That error correction and proofreading cost free energy is known (Hopfield, Ninio, Bennett 1979, Murugan–Huse–Leibler, Sartori–Pigolotti). Self-correcting media and their dimension dependence are known (§5 sources).

**What this note adds, in the named classes, and only that.** (i) Proposition RF: a pointwise-in-time floor for keeping a record of a *vanished* bit, uniform over codes, redundancy, decoders and correctors, with an explicit constant \(4(\ln 2-h(\varepsilon))\); (ii) the lower bound as \(kT\) times the medium's modified log-Sobolev constant, which through \(\rho\le 2\lambda\) disappears when the medium's relaxation slows (that self-correcting media then hold records at near-zero ongoing heat is shown separately, by passive retention, §5); (iii) the coincidence of the one-carrier floor with the telegraph tracking floor of T59/T60; (iv) the repetition-code numerics showing that redundancy caps the floor per record at small error; (v) RF-forget, stated for records; (vi) the conditional stock exponent \(3-1/(1+\kappa)\). The one-carrier value \(kT\Gamma L(\varepsilon)\) on its own is the measurement-free branch of Zhao 2026 (§2), so (iii) is new only as a link to T59/T60, and (i) is new as the uniform bound over codes and correctors with an explicit constant. We did not find these stated in this composition in the sources checked; the search was not exhaustive, and each step is elementary given its source.

**Difference from Bekenstein and Bousso.** Their bounds cap the entropy of a region, all of it, static or not, with a Planck coefficient (\(A/4\ell_p^2\), about \(9.57\times10^{68}\) nats per square metre), and they need gravity: a horizon, the generalized second law, light sheets. RF-cap caps only records that must be actively repaired in unprotected media, it depends on \(q\), \(\Gamma\) and \(T\), it sits about 38 orders of magnitude below the holographic density at the 1 eV example, and it is evaded by a passive cooperative medium. It is not a derivation of the holographic bound, and it does not derive part of it. The area in RF-cap comes from the heat-shed input; the cost of judgment contributes the price per record.

## 8. The GTAO method, run explicitly

**Cost, with named terms.** For \(N\) records over a horizon \(\tau\):

\[
J=N\Bigl[\tfrac{C_{\mathrm{write}}}{\tau}+\dot Q_{\mathrm{repair}}(\varepsilon,m)+w\,\varepsilon\Bigr]\quad\text{subject to}\quad N\dot Q_{\mathrm{repair}}\le qA,\;\;Nm\le\rho_cV,\;\;P(\mathrm{ruin})\le\varepsilon_{\mathrm{ruin}},
\]

with \(C_{\mathrm{write}}\ge kT(\ln2-h(\varepsilon))\) the one-time Landauer-type write (T59), \(\dot Q_{\mathrm{repair}}\ge kT\rho(m)(\ln 2-h(\varepsilon))\) (RF-general), \(w\) the stake per unit error, \(q\) the shedding input and \(\rho_c\) the room input.

**Minimum.** Minimizing the floor version \(kT\rho(\ln2-h(\varepsilon))+w\varepsilon\) over \(\varepsilon\) gives \(\varepsilon^*=1/(1+e^{w/(kT\rho)})\) (**ELEMENTARY**; checked at three ratios): a Boltzmann form, with the stake in the place of an energy and \(kT\rho\) in the place of a temperature. With the one-carrier floor \(kT\Gamma L(\varepsilon)\) the minimum is at \(kT\Gamma L'(\varepsilon^*)=-w\), so \(\varepsilon^*\approx kT\Gamma/w\) for a large stake. When the shed constraint binds, its multiplier raises the effective price of repair and moves \(\varepsilon^*\) up. This is the minimum of a lower bound, not of the true cost.

**Read-off design.** (1) Horizon shorter than the lifetime: hold, no repair, zero ongoing heat (T59). (2) Longer horizon and a cooperative medium below its ordering temperature: hold in blocks of size \(m(\tau)\); stock scales with volume up to a polylogarithm; no heat cap. (3) Only unprotected carriers: repair; at small error add carriers rather than heat (§4); the number kept is capped by \(qA/(kT\Gamma g(\varepsilon))\). (4) In every case, keep only the records the decision names, and freeze the rest against a present reference where one exists (T41, T44, T62).

**Scope.** Class RF: classical records, a finite joint Markov process with local detailed balance on every mechanism, one bath temperature, leak rates fixed by the medium, a medium symmetric under the logical flip, the shedding input. An asymmetric leak toward a ground state, as in DRAM charge loss, changes the reference measure and is not covered here. Quantum records enter only through the known results of §5. Steering worlds remain under T61.

**What remains a measurement or input.** \(\Gamma\) (that is, \(\Delta E\) and \(\nu\)), \(q\), \(\rho_c\), \(w\), and which medium is available at the given temperature. Whether any real system — refreshed memory, genome maintenance, synaptic maintenance — sits near this minimum is Part 2 of the theory (`CORE.md` §3); no system has been shown to qualify under THEORY §3, and this note makes no such claim.

## 9. Open, and the next angle

- **OPEN:** the exact infimum \(g(\varepsilon)\) over all codes (between \(4(\ln 2-h)\) and the repetition values), and whether a \(b\)-blind corrector attains it up to a vanishing lifetime term.
- **CONJECTURE:** \(\lim_{\varepsilon\to0}\inf_ng^{\mathrm{rep}}_n(\varepsilon)=8\) (diffusion limit).
- **OPEN:** an upper bound, with a proof, on the repair heat of a self-correcting block (the assumption used for the exponent \(3-1/(1+\kappa)\)).
- **OPEN:** quantum records in 3D (tracks the open literature question).
- **Next angle.** The cap this note derives is evaded exactly by cooperative media. The remaining live route to a stock cap that the cost could feed is the one T62 §6 names second: a gravitational or causal input stated explicitly (a dropping process and the generalized second law, or Casini's relative-entropy form, which has the shape of the cost's one-time write term). Whether a horizon forbids a passive cooperative medium from holding volume-many records is the question that would connect the two; it is **OPEN**, and this note does not start it.

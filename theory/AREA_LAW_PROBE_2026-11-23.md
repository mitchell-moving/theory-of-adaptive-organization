Labels follow the repo: **PROVED (in-model)** is a result inside a named class. **KNOWN** is published and credited, only for what that source states. **ELEMENTARY** is a direct consequence of a proved or known inequality, not a new named theorem. **CONJECTURE** is a named bet, not a result. **HYPOTHESIS** is a claim about the world, not a theorem. **OPEN** is not derived. The law of adaptive organization (hypothesis): strongly selected systems minimize the cost of judgment.

## 0. Bottom line

No, for equilibrium records. In class AL below, the cost of judgment does not force the maximum record a region can hold, at bounded ongoing cost, to scale with boundary area rather than volume, when the records are static biases held against a frozen reference. Records that must outlast their source, held in metastable wells that leak, are not covered by this answer; their indefinite-retention cost is OPEN (T59), and §6 names that as the next angle. It does not derive the holographic bound, and it does not derive part of that bound in the sense of producing a stock cap in Planck units from the heat floor.

The reason is the split this week's floor made exact, and that T41 had already seen for equilibrium bulk coordinates. Frozen storage held as a static bias has vanishing ongoing memory-bath heat (T57, T59, T60; Proposition H-iface's free equilibrium coordinates in `theory/HOLOGRAPHIC_2026-11-22.md`). A volume of static records can sit at a finite error and zero ongoing heat. A bound on ongoing cost therefore does not cap the frozen stock. The published holographic bound is a cap on that stock — the entropy of a region — not on a learning rate.

What the same math does give, once one extra physical input is stated: if the memory exchanges with the world only through a boundary of \(N\) channels with a finite capacity each, the sustainable *tracked* learning rate is at most the boundary capacity. If those channels are packed with a bounded rate per unit area, that flux scales with area. That is an area scaling of tracked flux, **PROVED (in-model)** in the class below. It is not the Bekenstein or Bousso bound. It carries no Planck-scale coefficient, and it does not bound frozen storage. T41 already showed that tracking every site of a changing volumetric field, at fixed per-site error, is a volume cost, and that a surface observer cannot hold a fixed per-site gap in a large cube. T44 already showed that decisions about the exterior of a region are blanket-sufficient, so their cost can follow the cut, and that decisions about the interior are not. Those results stand. The new split is that tracked flux can inherit an area cap from a boundary-capacity input, while frozen storage cannot inherit one from our floor.

The extra input is weaker than what Jacobson or Bousso assume: no Unruh temperature, no light sheets, no gravity, and no \(\hbar\) or \(G\). The conclusion is weaker in the same measure: a rate bound, not \(S\le A/(4\ell_p^2)\). Jacobson assumes an area law for horizon entropy and derives the Einstein equation, which is the opposite direction. To get the stock area law one still needs a geometric or gravitational ingredient the cost does not supply.

## 1. The question, and what is already in the repository

Chad's question, stated exactly, is this: does the cost of judgment — memory heat at least \(kT\) times the net learning rate, storage free in a frozen world, tracking change costly — together with standard physical inputs stated explicitly, force the maximum record a region can hold at bounded ongoing cost to scale with boundary area rather than volume? Or can it at least derive part of the holographic bound instead of assuming it?

A repository search was done first. The question is not empty, and it is not new under a different name.

- T41 (`theory/HOLOGRAPHIC_2026-11-22.md`) asked whether the physics-derived cost has a non-metaphorical holographic structure. For bulk per-site error, it does not. The floor is the interface learning rate (Proposition H-iface). At fixed per-site error the heat infimum scales with the volume (Proposition H-volume). A memory that sees only a surface of size \(A\) removes at most \(A/\alpha\) from the total variance (Proposition H-surface). Holding a designated cut scales with the cut, and that is the cost equation on the sites the decision names, not a bulk stored on a boundary. Equilibrium coordinates that leave the conditional unchanged are free at heat zero. A reading that "bulk dimension is bounded by the interface flow" is the counterexample already on that page.
- T42 (`theory/HOLOGRAPHIC_MEMORY_2026-11-22.md`) is a different sense of "holographic": a shared memory versus local copies, in the Gaussian class. It is not a Bekenstein–Hawking entropy.
- T44 (`theory/BOUNDARY_SUFFICIENCY_2026-11-22.md`) is the strongest existing lever for the area question. For decisions outside a region of an equilibrium Gaussian field, the Markov blanket is sufficient (BS-screen, BS-loss), and the interior is not (BS-interior). Tracked information about the exterior crosses the boundary. That page already says it is not an AdS/CFT dictionary and not a black-hole entropy.
- T59 (`theory/FREEZE_OR_HOLD_FLOOR_2026-11-23.md` §5) takes the area law and the Page curve as inputs, classifies a black hole as the extreme of the frozen class, and does not derive the area law. `theory/SCALE_ENDS.md` §4.2 already records the same gap: the theory does not derive the area law, and "horizons are what saturate the bound" comes from gravity. This note does not enlarge that file.
- T60 and T61 sharpen the floor: connected changing worlds have a world-only positive learning-rate floor; frozen or disconnected worlds do not; once the memory steers, a design-independent positive floor on memory heat alone is false in the actuation class.

This note asks a narrower question than T41, with the new floor in hand: not "does the cost look holographic in a Gaussian field," but "does the cost, plus stated physical inputs, force area scaling of the maximum record at bounded ongoing cost?" The interface and blanket results are used, not re-proved.

## 2. Prior art: what each derivation needs, and where information-only arguments get stuck

Every DOI in this section was resolved on `api.crossref.org` on 23 November 2026, except 't Hooft (1993) and Bremermann (1962, 1967), which have no Crossref works record and are cited by their printed sources and, for 't Hooft, by arXiv `gr-qc/9310026`. Nothing below is claimed as new. The ingredient list is for what that source uses, not a criticism of the paper. Full texts were not opened this session unless already in the repository; titles and bibliographic fields are from Crossref, and two abstracts that were fetched earlier in the repository are used as already credited there.

**Bekenstein 1973,** *Phys. Rev. D* 7:2333, doi 10.1103/PhysRevD.7.2333. Black-hole entropy is proportional to horizon area. Ingredients: general relativity (a horizon with an area), a thermodynamic reading of that area, and \(\hbar\). Gravity and a horizon are essential. This is the area law as an output of black-hole thermodynamics, not of a record-keeping cost in a box.

**Hawking 1974,** *Nature* 248:30, doi 10.1038/248030a0, and **Hawking 1975,** *Commun. Math. Phys.* 43:199, doi 10.1007/BF02345020. Quantum fields on a black-hole spacetime radiate at the Hawking temperature. Ingredients: quantum field theory in curved spacetime, a horizon, and \(\hbar\). The first law then confirms Bekenstein's entropy. Gravity, a horizon, and quantum fields are essential.

**Bekenstein 1981,** *Phys. Rev. D* 23:287, doi 10.1103/PhysRevD.23.287. A universal bound \(S\le 2\pi ER/(\hbar c)\) for a bounded system. Ingredients: a thought experiment in which the system is dropped into a black hole, the generalized second law, and general relativity (the area increase). Gravity, the generalized second law, and a causal dropping process are essential. In Planck units the bound is saturated by a black hole. The bound itself contains no \(G\), and it caps stock by energy and size, not by area; Casini 2008 later recovered a flat-space form inside quantum field theory without the dropping argument.

**Unruh 1976,** *Phys. Rev. D* 14:870, doi 10.1103/PhysRevD.14.870. An accelerated observer in flat space sees a thermal bath at \(T=\hbar a/(2\pi k)\). This is an ingredient of Jacobson's later argument, not an area law.

**'t Hooft 1993,** in *Salamfestschrift* (World Scientific Series in 20th Century Physics vol. 4), pp. 284–296, arXiv `gr-qc/9310026`. No Crossref works record was found for the chapter. The observable degrees of freedom, he argues, are Boolean variables on a two-dimensional lattice. Ingredients: the Bekenstein–Hawking area entropy (already in hand), unitarity, and gravitational collapse. The area law is an input from black-hole thermodynamics. The dimensional reduction is the output.

**Susskind 1995,** *J. Math. Phys.* 36:6377, doi 10.1063/1.531249. The world as a hologram. Ingredients: 't Hooft's counting, black-hole entropy, and a horizon-complementarity reading. The area law is again an input.

**Bousso 1999,** *JHEP* 1999:004, doi 10.1088/1126-6708/1999/07/004, and **Bousso 2002,** *Rev. Mod. Phys.* 74:825, doi 10.1103/RevModPhys.74.825. The covariant entropy bound: the entropy on a light sheet is at most \(A/4\) in Planck units. Ingredients: the causal structure of spacetime (light sheets of non-positive expansion), the Planck scale (\(\hbar\), \(G\), \(c\)), and motivation from black holes and cosmology. This is a stock bound on a null hypersurface, not a learning-rate bound, and not a result of information thermodynamics alone.

**Jacobson 1995,** *Phys. Rev. Lett.* 75:1260, doi 10.1103/PhysRevLett.75.1260. The Einstein equation as an equation of state. Ingredients: local Rindler horizons, the Unruh temperature, the Clausius relation \(\delta Q=T\,dS\), and the assumption that entropy is proportional to horizon area. The area law is an input. Gravity is the output. That is the opposite direction from the question asked here.

**Verlinde 2011,** *JHEP* 2011, doi 10.1007/JHEP04(2011)029. Gravity as an entropic force. Ingredients: holographic screens (holography assumed), equipartition, and the Unruh temperature. Holography is an input. Newtonian or Einstein gravity is the output.

**Padmanabhan 2010,** *Rep. Prog. Phys.* 73:046901, doi 10.1088/0034-4885/73/4/046901. A review of thermodynamical aspects of gravity, including holographic equipartition. The area-entropy link is part of the framework, not a result of the cost of judgment.

**Landauer 1961,** *IBM J. Res. Dev.* 5:183, doi 10.1147/rd.53.0183. Irreversible erasure costs at least \(kT\ln 2\). No geometry and no area law. This is the one-time write already used in T59, not a rate cap on a frozen stock.

**Bremermann 1962,** in *Self-Organizing Systems 1962*, and **Bremermann 1967,** *Proc. Fifth Berkeley Symp. Math. Statist. Prob.* 4:15–20 (Project Euclid `euclid.bsmsp/1200513783`). No Crossref works record was found. A processing-rate bound of order \(mc^2/h\) bits per second per mass. Ingredients: \(E=mc^2\), a quantum limit on distinguishable markers, and a rate, not a storage-area bound.

**Margolus and Levitin 1998,** *Physica D* 120:188, doi 10.1016/S0167-2789(98)00054-2. A system of energy \(E\) has a maximum speed of evolution \(2E/(\pi\hbar)\). Ingredients: energy and \(\hbar\). No area.

**Lloyd 2000,** *Nature* 406:1047, doi 10.1038/35023282. Ultimate physical limits to computation. The black-hole end of the bound uses the Bekenstein–Hawking bit count as an input. It does not derive area-versus-volume storage independently of that entropy.

**Bombelli, Koul, Lee and Sorkin 1986,** *Phys. Rev. D* 34:373, doi 10.1103/PhysRevD.34.373, and **Srednicki 1993,** *Phys. Rev. Lett.* 71:666, doi 10.1103/PhysRevLett.71.666. The entanglement entropy of a region, in a quantum field, scales with the boundary area over a power of a short-distance cutoff. Ingredients: quantum field theory, a spatial region, and a cutoff. This is an area law, and it does not need a black hole. It is also not the holographic bound: the coefficient depends on the cutoff and on the number of species, and the object is vacuum entanglement, not a cap on how much thermal or frozen record a region can hold. In a field theory without gravity, a thermal gas still has an extensive (volume) entropy.

**Casini 2004,** *Class. Quantum Grav.* 21, doi 10.1088/0264-9381/21/9/011, "Geometric entropy, area and strong subadditivity." Geometric entropy is tied to area from strong subadditivity together with quantum mechanics and relativity. Still a geometric-entropy statement, with a cutoff, not \(S=A/(4G)\).

**Casini 2008,** *Class. Quantum Grav.* 25:205021, doi 10.1088/0264-9381/25/20/205021. A flat-space form of the Bekenstein bound is the positivity of relative entropy between a local state and the vacuum. Ingredients: quantum field theory and the modular Hamiltonian. The species problem is avoided because relative entropy, not raw von Neumann entropy, is the quantity. This is the sharpest information-theoretic success in this list. It is still not the holographic bound: the inequality is \(S(\rho_V)-S(\rho_V^0)\le\langle K\rangle-\langle K\rangle_0\), which can look like \(S\le 2\pi ER\) in a limit, and it is not \(S\le A/(4\ell_p^2)\). Casini's own reading is that the bound holds automatically in quantum field theory and does not add a new semiclassical constraint.

**Casini 2007,** *Class. Quantum Grav.* 24, doi 10.1088/0264-9381/24/5/013, "Mutual information challenges entropy bounds." A place where a purely information-theoretic reading of a flat-space entropy bound does not go through: vacuum mutual information can sit outside some proposed caps. **Casini 2003,** *Class. Quantum Grav.* 20, doi 10.1088/0264-9381/20/13/304, is a geometric reading of the covariant bound; the PDF was not opened this session, and no formula is taken from it.

**Blanco, Casini, Hung and Myers 2013,** *JHEP* 2013, doi 10.1007/JHEP08(2013)060. Relative entropy in a holographic duality. The gravity dual is an input. **Bousso, Fisher, Leichenauer and Wall 2016,** *Phys. Rev. D* 93, doi 10.1103/PhysRevD.93.064044, the quantum focusing conjecture, is a quantum upgrade of the light-sheet bound and still lives in spacetime physics.

**Horowitz and Esposito 2014,** *Phys. Rev. X* 4:031015, doi 10.1103/PhysRevX.4.031015. The thermodynamic step the repo uses: heat is at least temperature times the net information flow, for bipartite jump processes. No geometry. **Parrondo, Horowitz and Sagawa 2015,** *Nature Phys.*, doi 10.1038/nphys3230, and **Goold, Huber, Riera, del Rio and Skrzypczyk 2016,** *J. Phys. A* 49:143001, doi 10.1088/1751-8113/49/14/143001, review the thermodynamic cost of information. They do not produce an area law.

**Where information-only arguments get stuck.** Three gaps recur. First, a cap on a *rate* (Landauer, Bremermann, Margolus–Levitin, Horowitz–Esposito) does not cap a *stock* that can be written once and then held at equilibrium. Second, a raw entropy of a region in quantum field theory is ultraviolet-divergent; cutting it off gives an area term whose coefficient is not \(1/(4G)\) and grows with the number of species. Third, turning a flux or energy bound into \(S\le A/(4\ell_p^2)\) requires a conversion that brings in \(G\), a horizon, or an assumed area-entropy density. Casini's relative-entropy form closes a version of the Bekenstein bound inside quantum field theory, and it still does not give the holographic cap. Jacobson and Verlinde travel the other way: they assume an area-entropy relation and derive gravity.

**Wheeler 1992,** *Ann. N. Y. Acad. Sci.* 655:349, doi 10.1111/j.1749-6632.1992.tb17083.x, and **Gibbons and Hawking 1977,** *Phys. Rev. D* 15:2738, doi 10.1103/PhysRevD.15.2738, and **Page 1993,** *Phys. Rev. Lett.* 71:3743, doi 10.1103/PhysRevLett.71.3743, are used in T59 and `theory/SCALE_ENDS.md` as already credited inputs (it-from-bit as a reading, de Sitter temperature, the Page curve). They are not re-derived here.

## 3. The cost, with named terms, and the split between tracked flux and frozen stock

The method is the same as everywhere: write the cost, find its minimum, and read off the design. The cost of judgment, as a long-run rate, is the memory-bath heat plus a stake on the decision loss, with the ruin cap outside the sum (`CORE.md` §3):

\[
J=\dot Q_M+w\,\mathbb{E}[\ell]\qquad\text{subject to}\qquad P(\mathrm{ruin})\le\varepsilon_{\mathrm{ruin}}.
\]

Under a memory/world split and local detailed balance, or the Einstein relation for additive-noise diffusions, \(\dot Q_M\ge kT\,\dot I_M\) in a steady state or a long-run average, with \(\dot I_M\) the *net* learning rate of the memory's own transitions (**KNOWN** for bipartite jumps, Horowitz and Esposito 2014; **PROVED (in-model)** for additive-noise diffusions; the bound is on net flow, not unsigned traffic, T59–T60).

Two different information quantities have to be kept apart. Mixing them is the likely place for a false yes.

**(a) Tracked information.** A memory that stays matched to a changing, connected world has a strictly positive net learning rate, in the classes of T60, once it beats chance. That rate is an interface quantity: T41's Proposition H-iface, and Horowitz–Esposito's identification of the steady learning rate with the information flow across the cut. T44's blanket is the same cut, for an exterior decision. Tracked information has to be paid for as long as the world keeps changing. It is the term our floor prices.

**(b) Frozen storage.** A static record in a frozen world, or an equilibrium coordinate that does not change the decision conditional, can be held at vanishing ongoing memory-bath heat after a one-time write (T57, T59, T60; T41's free equilibrium bulk). The stake on error remains. The stock of such a record can be large. Our floor, as a rate, does not price that stock.

The published holographic bound, and the Bekenstein 1981 bound, are caps on a stock of entropy in a region. They are statements about (b), or about (a) and (b) together as one undifferentiated "information in a region." They are not statements about a learning rate. If our cost is to derive them, it has to cap (b) at bounded ongoing cost. The next section checks that it does not, and that what it does cap, given an extra input, is (a).

## 4. A clean model, what is proved, and the design read-off

**Class AL.** An exogenous world \(X\) and a memory \(M\) that exchange only through a boundary \(B\) of \(N\) channels. Channel \(i\) is a communication channel of finite capacity \(c_i<\infty\), in nats per unit time: the mutual-information rate it can carry, over all input processes, is at most \(c_i\). The boundary capacity is \(C_B=\sum_{i=1}^N c_i\). The capacity input, stated in the form the proofs use: because \(M\) is coupled to \(X\) only through these channels, the steady transfer-entropy rate \(\mathcal T_{X\to M}\) is at most \(C_B\). The thermodynamic step \(\dot Q_M\ge kT\,\dot I_M\) is assumed in the same scope as T59–T61 (bipartite jumps with local detailed balance, or additive-noise diffusions). The world may be frozen or a product of independent symmetric telegraphs; those subclasses are named with the claims that use them. A packing input, used only when it is named: the \(N\) channels lie on a surface of area \(A\) with capacity density at most \(\varphi\), so \(C_B\le\varphi A\). Temperature units \(kT=1\) in the checks do not change the scaling.

**Proposition AL-iface (ELEMENTARY, given the Hartich–Barato–Seifert inequality and the capacity input).** The net learning rate cannot exceed the transfer-entropy rate across the cut, which the capacity input caps. Hence \(\dot I_M\le C_B\).

*Reason.* In a bipartite steady state, the learning rate of \(M\) is at most the transfer-entropy rate from \(X\) to \(M\) (**KNOWN**, Hartich, Barato and Seifert 2014, doi 10.1088/1742-5468/2014/02/P02016; the sensory capacity \(l/\mathcal T\le 1\) of Hartich, Barato and Seifert 2016, already in `theory/INFO_PHYSICS_GENERAL.md`). The capacity input caps that transfer-entropy rate by \(C_B\). (Horowitz and Esposito 2014 and T41's H-iface identify \(\dot I_M\) with the information flow across the same cut; they are the reason the cut is the right place to put the channels, not the step that gives the inequality.) Reviewer note, 23 November 2026: the earlier reason went from "information flow across the cut" to "each channel's mutual-information rate" without a named inequality; the transfer-entropy step is the missing link. The bound itself is unchanged.

**Proposition AL-flux (PROVED (in-model), in class AL).** \(\dot Q_M\ge kT\,\dot I_M\) and \(\dot I_M\le C_B\). No design in the class sustains a net learning rate above the boundary capacity. A memory that sustains \(\dot I_M\) pays at least \(kT\,\dot I_M\).

**Proposition AL-area (PROVED (in-model), in class AL, given the packing input).** If \(C_B\le\varphi A\), then \(\dot I_M\le\varphi A\). The sustainable tracked learning rate scales at most with the area of the boundary, at fixed capacity density. This is a bound on (a), the flux. It is not a bound on (b), and it contains no factor \(1/(4\ell_p^2)\). The packing input is an extra physical assumption, not a consequence of the cost.

**Proposition AL-stock-free (PROVED (in-model); T59's frozen record, packed in a volume).** Let the world be frozen. Let the region contain \(N_V\) independent two-state records, each held by a static bias at Boltzmann error \(\varepsilon\in(0,1/2)\), with memory rates in local detailed balance. Then each record has net learning rate zero and vanishing ongoing memory-bath heat (T59 §0–§1). The total stock is \(N_V\bigl(\ln 2-h(\varepsilon)\bigr)\), with \(h\) the binary entropy in nats, and it scales with \(N_V\). If the records are packed in the interior, \(N_V\) can scale with volume. Bounded ongoing cost, including zero, does not cap \(N_V\).

This is the same loophole T57 and T59 left open, now used as a counterexample to an area law for the maximum record. It is also T41's free equilibrium bulk, in the two-state language. A holographic reading that "bulk dimension is bounded by the interface flow" is already false on T41's page; AL-stock-free is that fact for the maximum frozen record at bounded ongoing cost.

**Proposition AL-write (PROVED (in-model), in class AL with a frozen world).** If the \(N_V\) records are written once through the boundary, the write takes a time at least \(I_{\mathrm{stock}}/C_B\), a finite duration. After the write, the ongoing heat is still zero. A rate bound on the boundary does not cap the eventual frozen stock. Landauer's one-time write is a finite cost, not a rate, as already in T59.

**Proposition AL-volume-track (PROVED (in-model), for a product of independent symmetric telegraphs).** Let the world be \(V\) independent symmetric telegraphs of flip rate \(r>0\), and let the task be Bayes error at most \(\varepsilon\in(0,1/2)\) on every factor. Then any finite joint memory has

\[
\dot I_M\ge V\,r\,L(\varepsilon),\qquad L(\varepsilon)=(1-2\varepsilon)\ln\frac{1-\varepsilon}{\varepsilon}.
\]

*Reason.* The T60 identity writes \(\dot I_M\) as a flux-weighted sum of Kullback–Leibler divergences of the memory conditionals, one term for each world jump. Jumps of different factors add. For the jumps of one factor, joint convexity of Kullback–Leibler divergence lowers the average of the conditional divergences to the divergence of the mixtures \(p(M\mid x_i)\) and \(p(M\mid x_i')\). T60's telegraph bound on that factor then gives at least \(r\,L(\varepsilon_i)\). Summing over factors, and using \(\varepsilon_i\le\varepsilon\), gives the claim. (The same volume scaling, for a different class and a per-site variance, is already Proposition H-volume in T41.)

If \(C_B<V r L(\varepsilon)\), the task is unattainable in class AL, by AL-flux. If \(V\) scales with volume and \(C_B\) scales with area at fixed \(\varphi\), the task fails past a finite size. A worked cube with \(\varphi=1\), \(r=1\), and \(\varepsilon=0.2\) first misses at side \(8\): the required rate is \(425.869627736030\), the capacity is \(384\). At side \(4\) the same task still fits (\(53.233703467004\) against \(96\)). This is not a holographic cap. It is T41's volume-versus-surface statement in the channel language: tracking a volumetric changing world at fixed per-site error is a volume cost.

**The minimum, and what is read off.** At a fixed attainable tracked error, the heat is at least \(kT\,\Phi\), with \(\Phi\) the learning-rate–distortion value of the world, and \(\Phi\) itself cannot exceed \(C_B\). The design that spends the least ongoing heat, in this class, freezes what can be frozen and spends the boundary flux only on the changing degrees of freedom the decision actually uses — the cut of T41, the blanket of T44, for an exterior decision. Interior records that the decision does not name can be left frozen, at vanishing ongoing heat. That is a design rule about where to spend tracking. It is not a derivation of the holographic bound, and it does not force the frozen stock itself onto an area.

**Scope.** Class AL, together with the named classes of T41, T44 and T59 already cited. The packing density \(\varphi\) is a stated input. The Planck-scale value of \(\varphi\), and the coefficient \(1/4\), are not derived. Worlds the memory steers remain under T61: a design-independent positive floor on memory heat alone is false once the memory may act, and a conservative trap is again a frozen loophole, now on the plant. Dual control is still open.

**What remains a measurement.** Whether a real system sits at the minimum is Part 2 of the theory, as in `CORE.md` §3. No system has been shown to qualify under THEORY §3. Whether actual spacetime has a capacity density set by the Planck scale, or a stock cap \(A/(4\ell_p^2)\), is a question for gravitational physics, not a measurement this note makes.

## 5. Is the extra input weaker, the same, or stronger than Jacobson or Bousso?

The packing input, "the learning flux passes through the boundary at a bounded rate per unit area," is weaker than Jacobson's assumptions and weaker than Bousso's. It does not use the Unruh effect, a local Rindler horizon, a light sheet, the Einstein equation, or Newton's constant. It is a finite-bandwidth interface, of the same kind as a Shannon capacity on a cut. The conclusion it buys, AL-area, is weaker in proportion: an upper bound on a learning rate, linear in area at fixed \(\varphi\), with \(\varphi\) unfixed by the cost, and with no claim about the frozen stock.

To turn AL-area into a stock bound of holographic form one would have to multiply the rate by a time and convert the result into Planck units. The natural times in the model are a correlation time of the world, or a light-crossing time of the region. The first produces a bound \(I_{\mathrm{tracked}}\lesssim\varphi A\,\tau\) on *tracked* stock, which still depends on \(\tau\) and still leaves frozen storage free. The second, together with a Planck-scale \(\varphi\), is no longer a weaker input: it is a Planck-scale bound on the bits that can cross a surface in one crossing time, which is already in the neighbourhood of the holographic counting, and it is not supplied by the cost of judgment. Jacobson assumes \(S\propto A\) and derives gravity. Bousso assumes light sheets and a Planck-scale stock cap. Our extra input, kept in its weak form, does not reach either, and strengthening it until it does would import the ingredient we were trying not to assume.

The area law in the published sense is about (b), or about (a) and (b) together. Our floor, taken alone, prices (a) and leaves (b) free when (b) is held as a static bias. That answer is scoped to equilibrium records; the leaky-record case is the open miss of §6. The genuine new angle is the split itself, together with the in-model area scaling of (a) under a boundary-capacity input, built on T41's interface and T44's blanket. It is a useful design rule. It is not a new understanding of gravitational physics.

## 6. The next angle

**First angle: records that must outlast their source (OPEN).** The counterexample AL-stock-free uses static biases, which stay matched because the frozen reference is still there. A record of something that is gone sits in a metastable well and leaks at a rate \(\Gamma>0\) (T59). Keeping it beyond \(1/\Gamma\) needs refresh or error correction, and that is where the cost could enter as a rate: if a non-LDB refresh floor of the form heat rate \(\ge kT\,\Gamma\,g(\varepsilon)\) per record were proved (T59 leaves this OPEN), the heat of \(N_V\) refreshed records would grow with the volume, while, under a stated input that heat leaves the region only through its boundary at a bounded flux per unit area, the heat that can be shed grows with the area. The refreshed stock would then be capped by area times shed flux over \(kT\,\Gamma\,g(\varepsilon)\). That is a stock cap to which the cost contributes the price per record. It has no Planck coefficient, and it must face passive error-correcting media, whose logical leak can fall with block size at no ongoing heat. It is a CONJECTURE until both pieces are proved.

**Second angle.** The next honest angle is not to relabel Bekenstein's collapse argument in the theory's terms and call the area law a derivation. It is to state a gravitational or causal input in the same explicit way this note stated the packing input — the generalized second law and a dropping process, or a light sheet, or a local Unruh–Clausius identification — and to ask what, if anything, the cost of judgment still adds once that input is on the page. The likely risk, already visible from §2, is that the stock cap will come from the gravitational input, and the cost will only name it. Casini's relative-entropy form is the other live route, and it already closes a Bekenstein-like bound inside quantum field theory without the cost of judgment. Its inequality has the shape of a one-time write cost, \(kT\) times a relative entropy to the thermal state, read in the modular frame; whether the cost of judgment adds anything beyond naming that shape is OPEN. Either path stays labeled until a proof exists. This note does not start them.

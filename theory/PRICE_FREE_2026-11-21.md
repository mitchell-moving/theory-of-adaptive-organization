## 1. Classification

Three bins. A prediction is **price-free** if it can be stated, and in principle scored, without a numerical value of *h* or *w* (direction only; an exponent or elasticity that cancels prices; a ratio across two conditions that share a price; a within-system shift when an independently measured non-price input changes; or the existence, but not the location, of a threshold). It is **price-ratio-only** if it needs a dimensionless ratio such as *w*/*h*, *w*/*c*, *d*/*c*, or *R* = *w*·*E*<sub>∞</sub>/*h*, but not the unit of the currency. It is **absolute-price** if it needs *h* or *w* in a stated unit, or any quantity that cannot be formed without that unit (including a numerical *K*\* that requires a numerical *R*).

Citations are to `THEORY.md` unless another file is named. “Proved” means proved in-model, which is not a claim about nature. A claim not already proved is marked **conjecture** or, where it is only a rearrangement of a proved result, **corollary**.

## 2. Rivals (blunt)

Ordinal monotone comparative statics are predicted by almost any optimizer. CT10, CT20 (ii) with sensing fixed, and CT33 with ruin slack (PF1, PF3, PF4) are Topkis / Milgrom–Shannon applied to a cost that is linear in *w*. Samuelson, any expected-cost minimizer of the form *S*(𝒟) + *w*·*E*(𝒟), and Sims 2003 (attention as a costly resource; already noted in the 21 November shortlist for TESTBEDS #2) all give “more at stake, no less spent” and “effort rises with stakes.” These do not distinguish the law. They can only fail if the system is not minimizing *any* cost of that form, or if ruin binds (PF2).

A cap on layers by the world's predictive degree (PF12) is Kalman–Bucy (1961), already labelled KNOWN in `theory/LAYERS.md` line 35. “Scale-free ⇒ many layers” was a claim of this project and has been withdrawn (L1; N46). Beating that withdrawn claim is not a win against an external rival. Interlacing (PF20) is Wiener–Kalman, and the store-noise timing rule is the ten Wolde line (THEORY line 19). Neither is a test of the *cost* hypothesis unless upkeep or a discrete layer price moves the design off the un-priced filter (the shortlist already said this of candidate 5).

The √ law at α = 2 (PF7, PF8) is Harris 1913, Barro 1972, Dixit 1991, Senders 1964, and Lee, Flack & Krakauer 2024, doi 10.1098/rspb.2024.1606 (Crossref issued 2024-10; title *Constructing stability: optimal learning in noisy ecological niches*; already credited in FRAMEWORK §6.7 and `litmap/`). Cost shares 1 : 1 at α = 2 (PF9) are the same EOQ identity. Currency invariance (PF19) is true of any degree-1 homogeneous objective. The *d*/(2β) lag (PF17) is Sella & Hirsh 2005, doi 10.1073/pnas.0501865102 (Crossref issued 2005-06-24), applied to *J*; any linear-in-cost fitness landscape has the same spread. Buffer versus τ^H (PF18) is self-similar first passage (Black & Cox 1976 is already credited). Existence of a sharing threshold (part of PF10) is already predicted by Beekman's own positive-feedback model (see §3.3). G3's rise-then-fall (PF5) is known for web crawlers (THEORY line 167). L3 (PF13) is rational approximation of the Wiener filter (Braess & Hackbusch; already credited). Discrete jumps in a discrete design variable (PF14, existence only) are what any integer-valued optimizer does.

**What, if anything, remains distinctive to *J*'s structure (after prior art):**

1. **The CT33 sign flip when ruin binds (PF2).** Survival-constrained models already predict reversals of monotone effort or risk comparative statics. Stephens 1981, doi 10.1016/S0003-3472(81)80128-5 (Crossref issued 1981-05; *Anim. Behav.* 29:628–629), and Caraco, Martindale & Whittam 1980, doi 10.1016/S0003-3472(80)80142-4 (Crossref issued 1980-08; *Anim. Behav.* 28:820–830), give the energy-budget rule: risk preferences flip when a starvation threshold binds (risk-averse above it, risk-prone below it). That is a flip in risk preference over reward variance, not a theorem about judgment spend in *S* + *w E* as *w* rises; in the CT33 counterexample the uncertified design is the *higher*-variance one, so the forced switch is toward lower variance, closer to a chance constraint than to risk-proneness. Closer still is Dall & Johnstone 2002, *Phil. Trans. R. Soc. Lond. B* 357:1519–1526, doi 10.1098/rstb.2002.1061 (Crossref issued 2002-11-29): under starvation risk, “gathering information is a luxury while insurance is a necessity,” and sampling declines as reserves fall and as the bout runs late (Crossref abstract and PMC `PMC1693060` abstract, opened this session). That is prior art for information *effort* falling when a survival constraint binds. Expected-cost optimizers without a feasibility set that depends on *w* never predict spend falling as stakes rise. What remains distinctive is the comparative static in the stake *w* of *S* + *w E*, with the reserve-pays-*J* / *w*²-variance mechanism (the reserve pays *J*, and realized-loss variance scales with *w*², so a costlier, lower-error design can become uncertified). CT24's Euler break under binding ruin is the accounting twin of the same mechanism.
2. **CT12 / S3 when α ≠ 2 (PF7, PF8), against R2's always-½.** At a locked α = 1.5 or 2.5, 1/α is 0.667 or 0.400. This is distinctive against R2, R1 (no α-dependent elasticity), and R3 (no price or drift-rate dependence). It is not distinctive at α = 2, and it is not proved for continuous filters (Lemma B2; the shortlist already said this). N51 is this test and does not qualify.
3. **CT15: the onset exponent is set by the level's sensing technology, not by α (PF10), and Lemma B3's opposite signs (PF6).** A rival that lets total volatility have a single sign (contingency theory; FRAMEWORK §6.4) loses if the signs oppose. A rival that lets the onset exponent track α loses if it tracks (*a_L*, *b_L*) instead. The −1 reset-read exponent is prior art in substance (Veldkamp & Wolfers 2007, Prop. 2; Van Zandt & Radner 2001; THEORY line 357). The α-independence across technologies, and the opposite-sign pair, are the distinctive remainder. The closed form *N\**(ρ) is distinctive as a *number* only after a preregistered map from the system to ρ and to *N*; existence of some threshold is not.
4. **Reads can replace layers, so *K\** can fall as stakes rise while spend still rises (CT33 / B.9 counterexample).** This is a *possibility* with a written counterexample, not a field prediction until a class is named in which the fall is predicted. It is distinctive against the naive “more stakes, more layers” reading of CT20 (ii).
5. **N55's curvature α − 1 (PF16), and N60's jump *order* from the measured spectrum (PF14).** “Spread falls with curvature” is standard stabilizing selection. That the curvature equals α − 1, and that layers appear in the order of the world's ε_K increments, are specific to this *J* in the CT12 / Model S settings. Scoring them still needs a measured α or spectrum, and, for N60's locations, a price ratio.

Everything else in §1.1 is either a general property of optimizers, or known filtering / inventory / first-passage mathematics, or a restatement of homogeneity.

## 3. At most three price-free predictions on the short list, with systems and fetched data

No system has been shown to qualify under THEORY §3 / FRAMEWORK §9 (`CORE.md` §7). “Plausible” below means a qualification *prospect*, not a verdict. Cities, nations and the biosphere remain excluded. Lab humans (N51) are not a strongly selected class. No prediction here is locked or scored.

### 3.1 PF2 — the ruin-binding sign flip (CT33)

**Why it is on the short list.** See §2, item 1. The qualitative reversal when a survival constraint binds is not unique to *J* (Stephens 1981; Caraco, Martindale & Whittam 1980; Dall & Johnstone 2002). The slack-side monotone (PF1) is not distinctive. What remains distinctive is the comparative static in the stake *w* of *S* + *w E*, with the reserve-pays-*J* / *w*²-variance mechanism.

**Plausible system.** A class with a sourced σ and *N_e*, an observable judgment spend *S* (not actuation, not a status proxy), and two stake conditions at the same prices, one with the ruin constraint slack and one with it binding (buffer, income and ε_r measured well enough to place the system relative to *B*<sub>slack</sub>). No such class was found. Power grids, already ranked first in TESTBEDS and preregistered as GRID-CROSS, are not a substitute: GB was scored at *N_e* = 1, public no-account pre-control imbalance data are not sufficient for five complete pairs (PR #128, official comparison not run, P25 UNTESTED), and frequency series after control cannot show the gap the flip needs.

**Public data this session.** No field dataset of judgment spend against a measured slack-versus-binding contrast was found or fetched. The in-model counterexample remains `theory/meq_checks/scope_checks.py` §1b (`chk('ruin-binding counterexample: S falls and E rises', …)`). ### 3.2 PF7 — look-rate elasticity 1/α versus R2's ½, in a world with α ≠ 2

**Why it is distinctive.** See §2, item 2. The test needs exact reset reads (not continuous filters), a measured α strictly inside (1, 3) and not 2, and a look or review rate that the system actually chooses. Drift *q* can be the exogenous lever; *w* and *c* then cancel in the elasticity (`THEORY.md` line 164). N51 already writes the kill; N51 does not qualify. The 21 November shortlist already reported that no qualifying public dataset with α, rates and prices was fetched that day.

**Plausible system.** A microbial or other Sung-class tracker with exact-reset sampling, or a software ecosystem that polls an upstream on a chosen schedule (FRAMEWORK §5, rank 2). Inventory firms are a natural CT12 setting, and TESTBEDS already ranked them low: “Prior art; no open holding- and stockout-cost data.” Qualification is not established for any of these.

### 3.3 PF6 and PF10 — opposite signs of shared versus private drift, and an onset exponent set by technology, not by α

**Why they are distinctive.** See §2, item 3. Existence of a sharing threshold is not distinctive. A numerical *N\**(ρ) is distinctive only with a preregistered map to ρ and to *N*. The opposite-sign pair, and CT15's technology-not-α exponent, do not need absolute *h*, *w* (the closed form is at *h* = 0; with *h* > 0 the small-ρ exponent stays price-free, and follows the case split at THEORY line 357).

**Plausible system.** Trail or dance recruitment as a shared judgment layer (shortlist candidate 4). FRAMEWORK already rates colony-level selection as moderate and lists ants as needing experiments. Pharaoh's ants and honey bees are not in the Sung et al. 2016 table. No colony-level *N_e*, σ, or *T*<sub>red</sub> was sourced this session. Qualification prospect is weak, as the shortlist said.

## 4. Honest bottom line

The law is testable in the field without absolute (*h*, *w*) **in principle**, and only on a short list.

What survives, as mathematics, is the classification in §1.1: signs, elasticities, shared-price ratios, within-system shifts when a non-price input changes, and the existence (not the location) of thresholds. A few of those can still distinguish the law from the named rivals, once prior art is credited: the comparative static in the stake *w* of *S* + *w E* when ruin binds, with the reserve-pays-*J* / *w*²-variance mechanism (the qualitative reversal itself is not unique; see §2, item 1); the 1/α elasticity when α ≠ 2; the onset exponent that tracks sensing technology rather than α, with opposite signs of shared and private drift; and, more weakly, the α − 1 curvature of cadence scatter and the order of layer jumps. The rest is what almost any optimizer, or Kalman–Wiener, or Harris / Lee–Flack–Krakauer, or Sella–Hirsh, already says.

What does not yet survive, as an empirical program, is a qualifying system with public, no-account data that actually scores one of those short-list claims. This session fetched real texts and a public inventory benchmark, and re-verified the Beekman and Dornhaus numbers the shortlist had left unchecked. None of that is a complete test bed: the ruin flip has no field dataset; InventoryBench has demand and assigned costs, not an observed selected *T\**; Beekman and Dornhaus have a size threshold and a null size effect, which do not identify *N\**(ρ) or Lemma B3. E. coli remains the best qualification prospect and still lacks a sourced σ and *T*<sub>red</sub>; its most obvious price-free spend-versus-benefit pattern is already in Ni et al. 2020 (the shortlist's candidate 2, at high risk of collapse). Stakes *w* have never been measured validly; this note does not change that.

So the obstacle recorded in [`OPEN_WORK.md`](https://github.com/Chad-Mitchell/theory-of-adaptive-organization/blob/1eed183f3888e8e3eead5956bbf1e8cb4233510a/OPEN_WORK.md) §8 is real, and it is not total. Dropping the demand for a fitness-unit *h* and *w* does not, by itself, produce a field test of the law. It changes the bottleneck from “absolute prices” to “a short-list price-free relation, in a class that can qualify, with a second independently measured quantity (α, ρ, a sensing class, or a slack-versus-binding contrast).” That is a narrower bottleneck. It has not been cleared. Until it is, the law is not shown to be field-testable, with or without absolute prices.

This file is not a lock, not a prereg, and not a kill or a pass.

## 0. Bottom line

**Plain answer.** The route works only with one extra assumption about matter, and the area it produces comes from its inputs, not from the cost.

1. **Retention needs a barrier (PROVED in-model, plus a KNOWN rate law).** A record kept passively, with no repair, for a horizon \(\tau\) at error at most \(\varepsilon\) needs a slow medium. For any reversible Markov medium, started thermalized in the basin that encodes the bit, the error at time \(\tau\) is at least \(\pi(A_1)\,(1-e^{-\lambda_2\tau})\), where \(\lambda_2\) is the spectral gap (Lemma R, PROVED). With the Eyring–Kramers rate law \(\lambda_2\simeq 2\nu e^{-B/kT}\) (KNOWN), this gives a free-energy barrier \(B\ge kT\ln(\nu\tau/\tilde\varepsilon)\) per logical record, with \(\tilde\varepsilon=-\tfrac12\ln(1-2\varepsilon)=\varepsilon+O(\varepsilon^2)\). The barrier is per record (per block), not per site.
2. **Gravity limits the energy (ASSUMED INPUT).** \(Mc^2\le Rc^4/(2G)\) (hoop / Schwarzschild); for static fluid spheres Buchdahl's \(M\le 4Rc^2/(9G)\) is tighter (KNOWN).
3. **The crux is not automatic (PROVED by counterexample in-model).** A barrier is an energy *difference* to a rarely visited saddle. Gravity sees the energy actually present, and at equilibrium only a fraction of order \(e^{-B/kT}\) of the records are at the saddle (§4.1). Three escapes exist: shifting the zero of energy (C1), purely entropic barriers (C2, NUMERICAL: zero energy barrier, gap \(\to 2\nu/M\)), and quantum tunneling at low temperature (C3). "Escape paths are energetically real" (A3) charges only the occupied saddles, not \(N\times B\).
4. **What makes the crux hold (ASSUMED INPUT A1).** A barrier-to-mass bound \(B\le\beta E_{\rm rec}\), with \(E_{\rm rec}\) the record's share of the region's gravitating mass-energy. It follows from a per-coupling bound, \(\sum_b\|h_b\|\le\kappa E_{\rm block}\), because a single-flip barrier never exceeds the span of the block Hamiltonian (Lemma S, PROVED), giving \(\beta=2\kappa\). In ordinary matter \(\beta\) is tiny (illustrative inputs: \(8.94\times10^{-11}\) for a 1 eV barrier on a carbon atom, \(3.37\times10^{-16}\) for a 60 kT magnetic grain). No theorem found forbids \(\beta\gg1\), so A1 stays an input.
5. **The cap, given A1 and the hoop input (PROVED in-model).** \(N\le \beta\,Rc^4/\bigl(2G\,kT\ln(\nu\tau/\tilde\varepsilon)\bigr)\). At fixed temperature this is **linear in \(R\)**. It closes T63's self-correction loophole for every medium that satisfies A1, including Ising-type blocks, which under a per-bond bound pay *more* mass per unit barrier as blocks grow (§4.3). No area appears at fixed \(T\). The closure is only as strong as A1, which is an input and is open: for a medium outside A1 (C1–C3 below) the loophole is not closed here. The linear-in-\(R\) form itself comes from the hoop input: any positive mass-energy floor per record gives it, and the barrier route ties that floor to \(kT\ln(\nu\tau/\tilde\varepsilon)/\beta\) (§5).
6. **The area, and the circularity check.** With a temperature floor \(kT\ge\hbar c/(2\pi R)\) (ASSUMED INPUT, the Unruh temperature at acceleration \(c^2/R\)), the cap becomes \(N\le\beta A/(4\ell_p^2\ln(\nu\tau/\tilde\varepsilon))\). Two identities show where this comes from. First, \(E/kT_U=2\pi ER/(\hbar c)\) is exactly Bekenstein's ratio, so the chain is Bekenstein's bound with retained-record nats in place of entropy, followed by the hoop step. That Bekenstein plus weak gravity gives the spherical bound \(S\le A/4\) is KNOWN (Susskind 1995; Bousso 2002). Second, with the Hawking temperature, \(E_{\max}/kT_H=2\,A/(4\ell_p^2)\) exactly, and \(T_H\) with the first law integrates to \(A/(4\ell_p^2)\). Assuming \(T_H\) is therefore assuming the black-hole entropy. Assuming \(T_U\) is not assuming \(S\le A/4\) outright, but it is the key ingredient in the Unruh–Wald and Casini arguments for Bekenstein's bound. For an ordinary region the floor is also false: at \(R=1\) m it is \(3.64\times10^{-4}\) K, and laboratories go far colder.
7. **Without \(G\) as an input (§5a, an extra angle).** G-free inputs give no energy ceiling, and any area cap they give has a free cutoff as its coefficient. A Jacobson-style read-off of \(G\) from it renames that cutoff and assumes the area scaling where Jacobson does (ELEMENTARY; the read-off is a CONJECTURE).

**What the cost adds.** A per-record retention price \(\ln(\nu\tau/\tilde\varepsilon)\) (65.62 at \(\nu=10^{12}\)/s, \(\tau=1\) yr, \(\varepsilon=10^{-9}\)) and the factor \(\beta\). It does not add the area, \(G\), \(\hbar\), or the coefficient \(1/4\). This is the model's implication, stated plainly. Nothing here derives general relativity, quantum mechanics, or holography.

## 3. Step (1): what passive retention requires

**Lemma R (PROVED).** Let \(L\) be the generator of a reversible continuous-time Markov chain on a finite state space with stationary distribution \(\pi\) and spectral gap \(\lambda_2\). Let \(A_0,A_1\) partition the states (the decoder's two answers). Started from \(\pi\) conditioned on \(A_0\),
\[
P_{\pi|A_0}(X_t\in A_1)\;\ge\;\pi(A_1)\bigl(1-e^{-\lambda_2 t}\bigr).
\]
*Proof.* Put \(h=1_{A_0}-\pi(A_0)\), so \(h\perp 1\) in \(L^2(\pi)\) and \(\|h\|^2=\pi(A_0)\pi(A_1)\). By stationarity, \(\langle 1_{A_0},e^{tL}1_{A_1}\rangle_\pi=\pi(A_0)\pi(A_1)-\langle h,e^{tL}h\rangle_\pi\). Reversibility makes \(e^{tL}\) self-adjoint with \(\langle h,e^{tL}h\rangle\le e^{-\lambda_2t}\|h\|^2\) on \(1^\perp\). Divide by \(\pi(A_0)\). ∎ (Spot-checked on 300 random reversible chains, §1 of the script.)

So for a symmetric code (\(\pi(A_1)=\tfrac12\)), error at most \(\varepsilon\) at \(\tau\) forces \(\lambda_2\tau\le -\ln(1-2\varepsilon)=2\tilde\varepsilon\) (PROVED). The start "thermalized within the basin" is what a written record relaxes to after its intra-basin mixing time. A record prepared off that state can do better transiently, and that is outside the lemma.

**From the gap to a barrier (KNOWN rate law, used as input).** For a two-state record with one-way rate \(\nu e^{-B/kT}\), \(\lambda_2=2\nu e^{-B/kT}\), and the condition is exactly \(B\ge kT\ln(\nu\tau/\tilde\varepsilon)\) (PROVED in that model; script §2 hits the error \(\varepsilon\) at equality). For a general medium the Eyring–Kramers law (Bovier et al. 2004; Holley–Stroock 1988 for the exponent on finite chains) gives \(\lambda_2\asymp\nu_{\rm eff}e^{-B/kT}\), with \(B\) the *free-energy* barrier of the logical flip and \(\nu_{\rm eff}\) a prefactor that carries the saddle's entropy. On a Metropolis double well the fitted slope of \(\ln(\text{passage time})\) against \(1/kT\) is within 5% of \(B\) (NUMERICAL, script §3).

**Block accounting.** For a record spread over a block of \(m\) sites (an Ising domain, a repetition code held cooperatively), \(B\) is the barrier of the *logical* flip of the whole block. The per-site energy \(B/m\) can be small. The number of saddle configurations enters \(\nu_{\rm eff}\), not \(B\). If all \(N\) records must be right at once with total error \(\varepsilon_{\rm tot}\), the union bound sets \(\varepsilon=\varepsilon_{\rm tot}/N\), and the log grows with \(N\): for \(10^{60}\) records at \(\varepsilon_{\rm tot}=10^{-9}\) it rises from 65.62 to 203.78 (ELEMENTARY).

## 4. Step (3), the crux: does a barrier \(B\) need about \(B\) of gravitating energy?

### 4.1 Why not automatically (PROVED, with counterexamples)

- **Gravity sees what is present, not the saddle.** With independent records in equilibrium, the mean number of records at the saddle is about \(N e^{-B/kT}\). At \(B=kT\ln(\nu\tau/\varepsilon)\) that fraction is \(\varepsilon/(\nu\tau)\), about \(3\times10^{-29}\) in the example (script §6). A requirement that escape paths be energetically real (**A3**: the saddle state must exist and the bath must lend \(B\)) therefore charges the region about \(N e^{-B/kT}B\), not \(NB\). A simultaneous excursion of all records has probability of order \(e^{-NB/kT}\). **A3 does not make the crux hold.**
- **Positive energy is too weak.** The positive energy theorems (Schoen–Yau 1979; Witten 1981) give \(M\ge 0\) for the total. That bounds \(E_{\min}\ge0\) and \(E_{\rm saddle}\ge 0\), not \(E_{\min}\ge B\).
- **C1, the shift (in-model counterexample).** Replace \(H\) by \(H-\min H\). The dynamics and the barrier are unchanged, and the record's energy is zero. Statistical mechanics has no zero of energy, and gravity does. So whether a barrier costs mass is a fact about the medium's actual mass-energy, which the cost of judgment does not contain.
- **C2, entropic barriers (NUMERICAL counterexample).** Two complete graphs \(K_M\) joined by one edge, all energies equal, rate \(\nu\) per edge: the gap falls as \(2\nu/M\) (\(M\lambda_2=1.95\) at \(M=80\), script §4) with *zero* energy barrier. Retention then needs about \(\nu\tau/\tilde\varepsilon\) microstates per side, that is \(\ln(\nu\tau/\tilde\varepsilon)\) nats of room per record, and no energy at all in the model. What limits room is a state-counting bound, which is Bekenstein's bound itself. With it as an input, \(N\ln(\nu\tau/\tilde\varepsilon)\le 2\pi ER/(\hbar c)\), and the entropic branch is circular by construction.
- **C3, tunneling.** As \(T\to0\) the classical requirement \(B\ge kT\ln(\cdot)\) vanishes, and retention is set by tunneling actions (barrier width and carrier mass), not by \(B\). Any temperature-independent cap must come from elsewhere (**OPEN**).

### 4.2 What would make it hold

- **A1, barrier-to-mass (ASSUMED INPUT).** \(B\le\beta E_{\rm rec}\), with \(E_{\rm rec}\) the record's share of the gravitating mass-energy (the mass that enters the hoop or Buchdahl condition). **Lemma S (PROVED).** For any block Hamiltonian, the single-flip minimax barrier between two states is at most \(\max H-\min H\), and for \(H=\sum_b h_b\) that span is at most \(2\sum_b\|h_b\|\) (triangle inequality; spot-checked on 40 random ferromagnetic graphs, script §5). So the per-coupling input **A1-local**, \(\sum_b\|h_b\|\le\kappa E_{\rm block}\) (couplings no larger than \(\kappa\) times the mass-energy of the constituents that carry them), implies A1 with \(\beta=2\kappa\). The entropic part of a free-energy barrier is not covered and falls under C2.
- **Does A1 hold in known models?** In ordinary matter, by a wide margin (illustrative inputs, not measurements): \(\beta=8.94\times10^{-11}\) for a 1 eV barrier on a carbon-12 atom, and \(3.37\times10^{-16}\) for a 60 kT barrier on an \((8\ \text{nm})^3\) grain at 16 g/cm³ (script §8b). Rest mass dominates. Strongly bound systems (hadrons, where binding is most of the mass) may reach \(\beta\sim1\) (a sketch, not computed here; no hadronic record with a barrier is exhibited). Degenerate-vacuum domains are a consistent case too: a random domain pattern carries wall energy of the same order as the barrier, so \(\beta\sim1\) (a sketch, not computed here; the repulsive active gravitational mass of domain walls is a known subtlety not pursued). **No theorem found** forbids a medium whose records sit at near-zero mass relative to the vacuum behind large barriers. Gauge-theory vacuum sectors separated by sphaleron barriers are the natural candidate, but they are not local records in any sense established here (**OPEN**, not a counterexample).
- **A2, localization (KNOWN, different route).** A carrier localized to size \(l\) has energy at least about \(\hbar c/l\). Packing \(N\) carriers into radius \(R\) under the hoop limit gives \(N\lesssim (R^2/\ell_p^2)^{3/4}\), the 't Hooft / Cohen–Kaplan–Nelson count. It needs no barrier, so it does not settle the crux, and it is sub-area.

### 4.3 Block accounting under a per-bond bound (PROVED for the 2D blocks computed; the trend is ELEMENTARY)

For an \(L\times L\) free-boundary Ising block with all couplings \(J\le\kappa\mu\) (\(\mu\) the mass-energy per site), the exact single-flip barriers are \(4J\), \(8J\), \(10J\) for \(L=2,3,4\), between \(2JL\) and \(2J(L+1)\). So \(B/(m\mu)\le 1,\ 0.8889,\ 0.625\) in units of \(\kappa\) (script §5b, §9). This per-bond, per-site bound \(J\le\kappa\mu\) is not the same normalization as A1-local (\(\sum_b\|h_b\|\le\kappa E_{\rm block}\)). Under A1-local itself, with \(2L(L-1)\) bonds, \(J\le\kappa L^2\mu/(2L(L-1))\) and the ratios are \(1,\ 0.667,\ 0.417\) (recomputed in the independent review of PR #242). The trend and the \(m^{1/d}\) scaling are the same under both. The barrier grows like the wall, \(m^{(d-1)/d}\), while the mass grows like \(m\). Cooperative self-correction buys zero repair heat (T63) but pays \(m^{1/d}\) more mass per unit of barrier. Under A1 the cheapest record is a single carrier whose barrier is as large as its own mass-energy allows.

## 5. Step (4): the cap, the temperature floor, and the circularity check

**Cap (PROVED in-model, given A1 and the hoop input).** \(N B\le\beta\sum E_{\rm rec}\le\beta Mc^2\le\beta Rc^4/(2G)\), with \(B\ge kT\ln(\nu\tau/\tilde\varepsilon)\), so
\[
N\;\le\;\frac{\beta\,Rc^4}{2G\,kT\,\ln(\nu\tau/\tilde\varepsilon)} .
\]
With Buchdahl's static-fluid limit, replace \(1/2\) by \(4/9\). Worked at \(\beta=1\), \(R=1\) m, \(T=300\) K, \(\nu=10^{12}\)/s, \(\tau=1\) yr, \(\varepsilon=10^{-9}\): \(E_{\max}=6.05\times10^{43}\) J and \(N\le2.23\times10^{62}\), against \(A/(4\ell_p^2)=1.20\times10^{70}\), 7.7 orders lower (NUMERICAL). At fixed \(T\) the cap is linear in \(R\). It falls below the holographic count for \(R>\beta\hbar c/(2\pi kT\ln)\), which is \(1.85\times10^{-8}\) m at 300 K. With real matter's \(\beta\) it is lower still. **Model implication:** given A1 and the hoop input, T63's self-correction loophole closes. Given those inputs, stock in any region at fixed temperature grows at most linearly in \(R\), and gravity, not heat, does the capping. *Scope (added in independent review).* The linear-in-\(R\) scaling is the hoop input applied to a positive mass-energy floor per record. Any such floor gives it, for example the rest mass of the carrier: \(N\le Rc^2/(2Gm_0)\), which for ordinary matter is the tighter of the two, since \(\beta\ll1\) means \(E_{\rm rec}\) far exceeds \(B\). That gravity forbids volume scaling of any massive stock is standard. What the barrier route adds is a floor on \(E_{\rm rec}\) set by retention, \(kT\ln(\nu\tau/\tilde\varepsilon)/\beta\), and it holds only where A1 holds. For a medium that violates A1 (C1–C3, or a hypothetical \(\beta\gg1\) medium), the loophole stays open.

**Temperature floor (ASSUMED INPUT).** \(kT\ge\hbar c/(2\pi R)\) gives \(N\le\beta A/(4\ell_p^2\ln(\nu\tau/\tilde\varepsilon))\), which is \(1.83\times10^{68}\) at \(R=1\) m with the same log. With the Hawking temperature of a hole of radius \(R\), \(kT=\hbar c/(4\pi R)\), the bound is \(\beta A/(2\ell_p^2\ln)\).

**Circularity check, explicitly.**
1. *Unruh form (ELEMENTARY identity, script §7).* \(E/kT_U=2\pi ER/(\hbar c)\) for every \(E,R\). The chain "\(NkT\ln\le\beta E\)" plus "\(T\ge T_U\)" is literally Bekenstein's bound with \(S\) replaced by \(N\ln(\nu\tau/\tilde\varepsilon)/\beta\). Adding the hoop limit is the KNOWN step from Bekenstein to the spherical bound. So the route reproduces a known derivation with records substituted. It does not assume \(S\le A/(4\ell_p^2)\) outright, because Unruh's temperature is flat-space field theory with no \(G\). But the temperature floor is the ingredient on which the Unruh–Wald and Casini arguments for Bekenstein's bound rest, so the result is not independent of that bound.
2. *Hawking form (ELEMENTARY identity, script §7).* \(E_{\max}/kT_H=2\cdot A/(4\ell_p^2)\) at every \(R\), and \(\int c^2dM/T_H=A/(4\ell_p^2)\) (checked numerically for one solar mass). Assuming the region sits at its own Hawking temperature is equivalent, through the first law, to assuming the black-hole entropy. That is circular with respect to the area law.
3. *Is the floor physical for an ordinary region?* No. A static region of radius \(R\) in flat space has no horizon and can be colder than \(\hbar c/(2\pi kR)\), which is \(3.64\times10^{-4}\) K at 1 m. The floor holds for a region bounded by a horizon, or as seen by an observer accelerating at \(c^2/R\).

**Answer.** In this route the area comes entirely from the two inputs, \(E_{\max}\propto R\) (gravity) and \(T\propto 1/R\) (horizon). Their ratio is \(A/\ell_p^2\). The cost of judgment supplies the per-record price \(\ln(\nu\tau/\tilde\varepsilon)\) and, through A1, the factor \(\beta\).

**A black-hole open question (identity plus a reading).** With the Hawking floor, cap\(/S_{\rm BH}=2\beta/\ln(\nu\tau/\tilde\varepsilon)\), which is below 1 for \(\beta\le1\) and any log above 2 (0.0305 at log 65.62). A black hole holds more than barrier-held records can, so its storage is not barrier storage in this class. That fits T59 and `theory/SCALE_ENDS.md`, where black holes are the frozen extreme with the area law as an input. Why the densest store is not a barrier store is an open miss, left **OPEN**.

**Lloyd's laptop.** Lloyd's memory, \(\tfrac43E/kT\) nats, and this cap, \(\beta E/(kT\ln)\), have the same \(E/kT\) shape. The cap pays \(\ln(\nu\tau/\tilde\varepsilon)\) per record for keeping the record, which a plasma at \(5.87\times10^8\) K does not do, and \(\beta\) for the barrier-to-mass link. Lloyd's count is a capacity at an instant, and this is a count of records that persist.

## 5a. An extra angle: could the area cap come without \(G\), with \(G\) then read off?

*Chad's idea, tested as an idea, not adopted as a spec. It does not change §4 or §5.*

**The idea.** Treat gravity as downstream, not as an input. Jacobson 1995 assumes horizon entropy proportional to area, \(S=\eta A\), on every local Rindler horizon, together with the Unruh temperature and the Clausius relation \(\delta Q=T\,dS\). From these he derives the Einstein equation, with \(G=c^3/(4\hbar\eta)\) read off from \(\eta\) (KNOWN). Verlinde 2011, *JHEP* 2011:29, doi 10.1007/JHEP04(2011)029, gets Newtonian gravity from holographic screens, equipartition and the Unruh temperature, with holography assumed (KNOWN; as T62 already records). Sakharov's induced gravity (1967; reprinted in *Gen. Rel. Grav.* 32:365, 2000, doi 10.1023/A:1001947813563) reads \(G\) off a microscopic cutoff (KNOWN). So the question is: can the record-cost floor plus a temperature or horizon input supply \(\eta\) without \(G\)?

**What the G-free inputs can and cannot do (ELEMENTARY, script §7b).** Without \(G\) the inputs are \(\hbar\), \(c\), \(k\), \(R\), the Unruh-type temperature \(\hbar c/(2\pi kR)\), the attempt rate \(\nu\), \(\tau\) and \(\varepsilon\). Up to combination with \(\nu\tau\) (for example \(c\tau/R\)), the only independent dimensionless group they form that involves \(R\) is \(R\nu/c\); \(kT_UR/(\hbar c)=1/2\pi\) is a pure number. So any cap built from them has the form \(N\le f(R\nu/c,\ \nu\tau,\ \varepsilon)\). Two consequences follow.
1. *The record cost gives no energy ceiling.* Without \(G\), nothing limits \(E\), so the barrier route of §5 has no cap at all. A cap needs a different G-free input, such as T63's shed flux \(q\) per area or T62's bounded channel density.
2. *An area cap needs a length.* \(N=\eta A\) requires \(\eta\propto(\nu/c)^2\) or some other inverse length squared, a cutoff \(\ell_c\). It matches \(1/(4\ell_p^2)\) only if \(\nu\sim c/\ell_p=1.85\times10^{43}\)/s, the inverse Planck time. That rate is not set by the cost. Choosing it is choosing \(G\).

**A Jacobson-style read-off (CONJECTURE, explicitly conditional).** Suppose one *assumes* that every local Rindler horizon carries retained records at a density \(\eta_{\rm rec}\) per area, each worth \(\ln(\nu\tau/\tilde\varepsilon)\) nats under the retention price of §3. Then Jacobson's argument would read off \(G_{\rm read}=c^3/(4\hbar\,\eta_{\rm rec}\ln(\nu\tau/\tilde\varepsilon))\) (identity checked in script §7b). This is a relabeling of \(\eta\), not a derivation. The area proportionality is still assumed, exactly where Jacobson assumes it, and \(\eta_{\rm rec}\) needs a cutoff that the cost does not provide. This is the same species/cutoff problem that T62 notes for entropy in field theory.

**Circularity, explicitly.** (i) Jacobson's input "entropy \(\propto\) area" is the holographic scaling itself, so a route that feeds it in has assumed it (ASSUMED INPUT, circular with respect to the area). (ii) The Unruh temperature contains no \(G\) and is legitimate as a G-free input (ASSUMED INPUT). It fixes \(T\propto1/R\) but not the area coefficient. (iii) The record-cost floors are inequalities, with dissipation at least a floor (T63). Clausius in Jacobson's form is an equality, and the nonequilibrium version (Eling, Guedens and Jacobson 2006, *Phys. Rev. Lett.* 96:121301, doi 10.1103/PhysRevLett.96.121301) adds an entropy-production term and changes the field equations. An inequality floor gives no field equation by itself (**OPEN**: whether a saturated floor could play the equality's role).

**Answer to the angle.** Not with what is in hand. G-free inputs give a cap whose area coefficient is a free cutoff, and reading \(G\) off it renames that cutoff. A non-circular version would need the cost itself to select a cutoff (for example, a minimum record size forced by the retention price), and nothing found here does that (**CONJECTURE** that such a selection exists; untested). The main crux in §4 is unaffected.

## 6. The GTAO method, run explicitly

1. **Cost, with named terms.** For \(N\) records in a region of radius \(R\) at temperature \(T\) over a horizon \(\tau\):
   \(\;J=\sum_i\bigl[c_{\rm err}\,\varepsilon_i(B_i)+\lambda_G\,E_{{\rm rec},i}\bigr]+J_{\rm write}+J_{\rm repair}\),
   with the **retention-loss term** \(\varepsilon_i(B)\approx\nu\tau e^{-B/kT}\) (Lemma R plus the Eyring–Kramers input); the **mass-energy term** \(E_{\rm rec}\ge B/\beta\) (A1); the **gravitational budget** \(\sum_iE_{{\rm rec},i}\le Rc^4/(2G)\) with multiplier \(\lambda_G\) (hoop input); the one-time **write term** (T57, T63); and the **repair term**, zero for passive records (T63). Equivalently: maximize \(N\) subject to \(\varepsilon_i\le\varepsilon\) and the budget.
2. **Its minimum.** Each record at \(B^*=kT\ln(\nu\tau/\tilde\varepsilon)\) and \(E^*_{\rm rec}=B^*/\beta\). Equal records at the smallest admissible energy fill the budget, so \(N^*=\beta Rc^4/(2G\,kT\ln(\nu\tau/\tilde\varepsilon))\) (PROVED, given the inputs).
3. **Read-off design.** Use the smallest carriers whose barrier is as large as their own mass-energy allows (\(\beta\) as large as matter permits). Avoid large cooperative blocks, which pay \(m^{1/d}\) more mass per unit of barrier under A1-local. Run cold, since the cap goes as \(1/T\) until tunneling takes over. Budget the error per record, since the log enters once per record.
4. **Scope.** Classical, thermally activated records in reversible Markov media; records independent; quasi-static, spherical, at most marginally self-gravitating regions; A1, the hoop limit and any temperature floor are inputs. Excluded: the tunneling regime (C3), purely entropic barriers (C2, which fall back on Bekenstein's bound), cosmological and collapsing settings, where only Bousso's covariant form is claimed to hold, and repaired (non-passive) records, which T63 covers.
5. **What remains a measurement.** \(\beta\) for real media and whether any medium has \(\beta\gg1\); the attempt prefactor \(\nu\); the actual temperature of a region; and whether long-lived records in real strongly selected systems sit near \(B^*\), with barriers just large enough for their horizon. That last item is the hypothesis's prediction in this setting, and it is untested.

## 7. What is new, and what is not

- **Known:** the retention barrier \(kT\ln(\cdot)\) (Kramers; Zhirnov et al.), the Eyring–Kramers law, the hoop and Buchdahl limits, Bekenstein's bound, its weak-gravity step to the spherical bound (Susskind; Bousso), the \(A^{3/4}\) localization count ('t Hooft; Cohen–Kaplan–Nelson), and Lloyd's \(E/kT\) memory. The cap in §5 at the Unruh temperature is the Bekenstein-plus-hoop argument with records substituted for entropy.
- **Not found stated elsewhere (search not exhaustive):** Lemma R as a one-line exact form of the retention requirement; the explicit statement that a barrier-to-mass input (A1) is what the route needs, together with its three counterexample families and the insufficiency of A3; the block accounting showing that cooperative self-correction raises the mass cost per unit of barrier; and the consequence that, given A1 and the hoop limit, T63's self-correction loophole closes at fixed temperature with linear-in-\(R\) stock. The linear-in-\(R\) scaling itself is the standard hoop limit on any massive stock (§5, scope). Only the retention-set mass floor is not found elsewhere. These are modest. None of them is an area law.

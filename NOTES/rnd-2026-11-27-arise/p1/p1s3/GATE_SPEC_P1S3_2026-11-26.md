It printed a final judge frequency of 0.20 (of 0.5 at start), births 3 and ruin share 0.34. It is recorded here because it is outcome-adjacent.*

**Design choice stated plainly:** the gate searches for a world and a price where a designed judge wins. Tuning the world until a judge can win is a design choice. It means a P1-S3 pass would show only that selection **can find** a judge **where one pays**. It would not show that judges pay in general or in nature, and it would not show that this world is typical.

- Grid: σ_f ∈ {1.0, 1.25, 1.5} × c ∈ {0.005, 0.02} per nat × ruin edge x_r ∈ {3, 4} × design reading strength k ∈ {0.1, 1.0}. Fixed elsewhere: shared income R = 50, upkeep m = 0.2, random death μ = 0.005, actuation price 0.02, τ_f = 30.
- Competitions (fixed designs, no mutation, 125 vs 125, T = 5000, pilot seeds 101–105, one force path per seed shared by both types): designed judge vs sieve, and designed judge vs reflex.
- **G1:** the judge's final frequency is ≥ 0.75 against the sieve in ≥ 4/5 seeds, with no extinction. **G2:** the judge's final frequency is ≥ 0.65 against the reflex in ≥ 4/5 seeds. **G3:** the median ruin share of deaths in the judge-vs-sieve runs is ≥ 0.25 (selection strength).
- **Choice rule if several worlds pass:** the smallest σ_f, then the larger c, then the larger x_r, then the smaller k (the least judge-friendly passing world).
- **If none passes:** report "the first nat never pays in this class (on this grid)" and run no test.
